Abstract

Background: Why do organizations do what they do, at what input value, on which underlying bases, and toward
what expected return—yet end up with 97.4% input-output misalignment, 99.6% operational inefficiency, 92.4% risk
non-detection, and $14.3 million in losses? This unresolved question persists predominantly in sub-Saharan
developing-economy boardrooms operating within complex international business (IB) environments. Its root
remains a primary source of self-organizational risk windows, disputes, threats, vulnerabilities, and loss gates. The
human-mediated RACI matrix (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) has served as a cornerstone
managerial-governance, operational, developmental, and input-output instrument. However, it faces human biases
and limits, and is challenged by the AI world to date, failing to provide real-time, error-free sensemaking of strategic
decision-making and beneficial results. Methods: This study employs a qualitative, in-depth case-study methodology
grounded in the ground-level reality of AI-Mediated RACI Matrix organizational boardroom functioning, witnessed
via the author's direct professional experience within an organization (anonymized for ethical compliance). The
research compares a legacy human-mediated RACI system with an Al-augmented version implemented over 24
months. Data includes 1,247 decision events, 89 strategic meetings, and pre- and post-implementation operational
metrics. Results: The AI-Mediated RACI Matrix demonstrated: (1) 97.4% accuracy in balancing inputs and
outcomes per activity or project to dead-end; (2) 99.6% enablement of absolute and complete real-time operational
efficiency, error-free sensemaking of strategic decision-making, management effectiveness, and organizational
defensibility; (3) 99.1% accuracy in allocating the cleaned and calculated Responsibility, Accountability,
Consultation, and decision-ownership to smart actors for achieving beneficial results; and (4) identification of 92.4%
of potential risk windows and vulnerability gates before they materialized into operational losses, with real-time
mitigation solutions provided. Conclusions: The framework shifts organizational governance and management from
self-risk windows, vulnerability, weakness, and loss gates to high-achieving success at domestic, regional, and
international levels. For developing economies, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa, this represents a mandatory; not
optional—technological leap to overcome systemic self-hindering and operational shame stages. The paper concludes
with a mandatory implementation protocol for Al-Mediated RACI in high-complex IB environments from region to
intercontinental arena.
