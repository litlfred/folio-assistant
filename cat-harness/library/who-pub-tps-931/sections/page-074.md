16. Politically and legally sensitive topics

Occasionally, manuscripts contain statements that may cause offence
tocertain Member States, or references to geographical entities whose
name or ownership is disputed. It would be unrealistic to try to provide
a comprehensive list of sensitive topics, and editors need to be alert to
potentially controversial issues. These may include, for example,
terminology that implies a Euro-centric view of the world (Middle
East, Far East), references to countries as underdeveloped, and sugges-
tions that a particular government is corrupt. As a general rule, any
text in a technical publication that describes the workings of, or
criticizes, a particular government or national health service should
have been cleared by the government concerned; this is the responsi-
bility of the technical unit. Brief statements of this nature presented as
examples from particular countries or as attributed views from other
publications are generally acceptable, but editors should always be
aware of the possibility of causing embarrassment to governments and
should clear any doubtful text through LEG.

Political and geographical designations

In general, WHO follows United Nations practice in the field of
geographical terminology. However, when situations are changing fast
it may be impossible for editors to keep up with the latest develop-
ments. If editors have any doubts on the acceptability of a particular
name or designation or the status of a country with respect to member-
ship of WHO, they should check with LEG. (For quick reference, the
Official List of Addresses contains a regularly updated list of Member
States.)

When several Member States are referred to in the same context in
tables, figures or text, and there is no obvious reason for grouping them
together in a particular way (e.g. by perinatal mortality rate or in order
of gross national product), they should be listed in alphabetical order.
