13. Mathematics and statistics

Mathematical matter and equations

Italics should be used for mathematical variables and certain physical
constants, roman type for operators (such as +, =, d and multipli-
cation and integral signs), abbreviations (such as log, sin and exp) and
representations of pure numbers (such as e and i).

Badly written or ambiguous symbols and signs should be identified for
the typesetter (e.g. italic letter “x”/Greek chi/multiplication sign;
capital letter “O”/Greek theta/zero), and the spatial relationship of
terms made clear. When several brackets have to be used in a math-
ematical expression, the sequence should be {[ (...) ] }.

Mathematical formulae should be written in such a way as to take up
as little space as possible, for example

x/(a—b) not __x ,

(a—b)

except when this could cause difficulty in understanding or lead to
confusion.

Mark thin spaces before and after mathematical operators, except
when + or—is used to indicate a positive or negative number (e.g. —6)
and around a medial multiplication point or solidus.

Equations for display should be clearly marked and guidance provided
on where to break those that are likely to be too long to fit on one line
(preferably before an operational sign and not in the middle of a term).
The second line may be set flush right, a standard indention from the
right margin, or aligned on operational signs. Equations (whether in
line or displayed) that form part of sentences may need punctuation
depending on their context.
