7. Transliteration

It is usual to transliterate Arabic and Russian from their original
alphabets to the Latin alphabet, and to transcribe Chinese into Latin
characters, ie. to romanize the language. Editors should consult the
Office of Language Services (TRA) if they are in any doubt about the
transliteration of particular words or phrases.

Arabic

The system of transliteration used by the United Nations is given in
document ES/TRANS/84/3/rev 2, Capitalization and spelling of some
terms related to the Middle East and North Africa. This document also
contains useful information on the official names of various people,
places and organizations often mentioned in connection with Arab
affairs. A copy is kept in CTT. References in general texts to well
known people or places should employ the spelling familiar to English-
speaking readers, e.g. Mecca not Makka.

Note the following in relation to Arabic proper names:

Russian

There are a number of systems for transliterating Russian. The ISO
“tinguistic system”, making free use of diacritical marks and with a one-
to-one correspondence between letters, is recommended for use in
WHO publications. The most recent version is ISO 9-1995: Informa-
tion and documentation — Transliteration of Cyrillic characters into
Latin characters — Slavic and non-Slavic languages. A slightly differ-
ent older version (ISO/R9-1954) is currently in general use in WHO.
