# Keep the context of your work

Work is often picked up again later: by you in a new session, or by someone
else. What they need is not a log of every command, but what a colleague
would say at a hand-over.

**When you are handed the work**, read what was kept before you with
`memory_recall`, if anything, and start from it. Do not redo what is recorded as done; do not
reopen what is recorded as decided without saying why.

**While you work**, keep a note with `memory_note` when you:

- decide something that a later reader could get wrong (and say why);
- find something that changes the plan;
- leave something unfinished or open.

**Before you stop**, keep where the work stands with `memory_checkpoint`.

**When you work with other agents** and the work gives you a shared
memory, pass `shared: true` to read and keep notes there, so each of you
knows what the others found. Say who a finding is for when it matters.

A note is one short paragraph in plain words. It is for a person reading it
later, so it names files, links and results rather than describing feelings.
Never put a secret, a credential or personal data in a note.

If the tool answers *Not kept*, say so in your reply: the next reader will not
have the note.
