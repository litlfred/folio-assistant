The publication is intended to assist countries in monitoring and analysing health workforce capacity, distribution, education, labour market dynamics, financing, governance and information systems in the context of universal health coverage and the health-related Sustainable Development Goals. It outlines the rationale for improved health workforce data and highlights the role of harmonized metrics in addressing challenges related to workforce availability, accessibility, performance and retention. The handbook presents a modular framework comprising 78 core indicators organized across 10 modules covering active workforce stock, education and training, regulation and accreditation, education financing, labour market flows, employment conditions, workforce spending and remuneration, skill mix, governance, and health workforce information systems. For each indicator, it provides definitions, methodological guidance, reporting requirements, data sources and disaggregation options. The publication also describes implementation principles, reporting standards and potential applications of the NHWA, supporting countries in strengthening health workforce monitoring, facilitating international comparability and informing policies aimed at improving health system performance and population access to quality health services.

WHO recommendations on antenatal care for a positive pregnancy experience

World Health Organization (تاريخ النشر: 2016-11-07)

This guideline presents the World Health Organization’s evidence-based recommendations on routine antenatal care to support a positive pregnancy experience for pregnant women and adolescent girls. It aims to provide a comprehensive, consolidated framework for antenatal care that complements existing WHO guidance on the management of specific pregnancy-related complications. The recommendations promote a person-centred, rights-based approach that extends beyond the prevention of maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality to enhance women’s physical, psychological and sociocultural well-being, support healthy pregnancies, and improve the quality and utilization of antenatal care. Intended for policy-makers, programme managers and health professionals, the guideline informs the development of national policies, clinical protocols and service delivery models. Developed according to the WHO guideline development process, the document draws on systematic reviews and applies the GRADE and GRADE-CERQual approaches together with the DECIDE evidence-to-decision framework. It contains 49 recommendations covering nutritional interventions, maternal and fetal assessment, preventive measures, management of common physiological symptoms, and health system interventions to improve antenatal care utilization and quality. The guideline also introduces the 2016 WHO antenatal care model, recommending a minimum of eight contacts during pregnancy, and emphasizes implementation, dissemination and periodic updating as new evidence becomes available.

Consolidated guidelines on the use of antiretroviral drugs for treating and preventing HIV infection: recommendations for a public health approach

World Health Organization (تاريخ النشر: 2016-06-06)

This evidence-based guideline consolidates World Health Organization recommendations on the use of antiretroviral (ARV) drugs for treating and preventing HIV infection, providing a comprehensive public health framework for HIV diagnosis, prevention, treatment, service delivery, and monitoring and evaluation across all age groups and populations. Developed in response to advances in scientific evidence and evolving programmatic needs, the second edition updates the 2013 consolidated guidelines and incorporates new recommendations, including universal eligibility for antiretroviral therapy (ART) regardless of CD4 cell count, expanded use of pre-exposure prophylaxis, and optimized approaches to HIV testing, treatment monitoring and management of coinfections and comorbidities. The guideline also outlines the methods used for guideline development and the principles underpinning a public health approach. The publication provides evidence-based recommendations on HIV testing services, antiretroviral therapy, prevention interventions, differentiated service delivery models, linkage to care, adherence, decentralization, task sharing, laboratory services and programme monitoring. It emphasizes integrated, people-centred and community-based approaches to improve access to high-quality HIV services, strengthen health systems and support implementation in diverse epidemiological settings. Intended primarily for national HIV programme managers, clinicians, policy-makers and implementing partners, the guideline aims to accelerate progress towards universal access to HIV services and the global goal of ending the HIV epidemic as a major public health threat by 2030.
