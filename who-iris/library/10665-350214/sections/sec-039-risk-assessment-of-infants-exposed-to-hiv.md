Risk assessment is based on prevailing maternal factors 
that affect the probability of HIV vertical transmission 
and is essential for selecting appropriate preventive 
interventions for infants exposed to HIV. Infants exposed 
to HIV are considered to have low risk for vertical 
transmission if their mothers are receiving ART and have 
suppressed viral loads during pregnancy and delivery and 
throughout breastfeeding.
WHO defines high-risk infants as those (Fig. 5):
• born to women with established HIV infection who have 
received less than four weeks of ART at the time of 
delivery; or
• born to women with established HIV infection and viral 
load >1000 copies/mL in the four weeks before delivery, 
if viral load is available; or
• born to women acquiring HIV during pregnancy or 
breastfeeding; or
• born to women identified for the first time during the 
postpartum period with or without a negative HIV test 
prenatally.
