(WHA63.18): summary of Member State commitments and requests 
	
to WHO (1) 
Member State commitments
	
f World Hepatitis Day. Designate 28 July as World 
Hepatitis Day, to raise awareness and strengthen 
prevention and control measures.
	
f Surveillance. Implement or improve 
epidemiological surveillance systems, to generate 
reliable data for guiding prevention and control.
	
f Integrated approach. Support integrated, 
cost-effective prevention and management of 
viral hepatitis, considering HIV coinfection and 
multisectoral collaboration.
	
f Adopt WHO guidance. Incorporate WHO-
recommended policies, strategies and tools for 
prevention, diagnosis and care.
	
f Strengthen health systems. Enhance national 
health systems for prevention, diagnosis, 
treatment, vaccination and injection safety.
	
f Protect health workers. Provide vaccination 
strategies and infection-control measures for 
health care workers.
	
f Mobilize resources. Use national and international 
resources to strengthen health systems and deliver 
affordable interventions suited to local needs.
	
f Access to medicines. Consider legislative 
mechanisms and flexibilities within the Trade-
Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights 
(TRIPS)a agreement to promote access to essential 
pharmaceuticals and technologies.
	
f Monitoring and evaluation. Develop and 
implement tools for monitoring preventive, 
diagnostic and treatment activities.
	
f Promote awareness. Celebrate World Hepatitis 
Day annually, to sustain public engagement.
Requests to WHO
	
f Guidelines and strategies. Establish time-bound 
goals and tools for the prevention and control of 
viral hepatitis.
	
f Research support. Promote scientific research on 
prevention, diagnosis and treatment.
	
f Burden assessment. Improve global estimates of 
economic impact and disease burden.
	
f Support resource-constrained countries. Help 
countries to organize World Hepatitis Day events 
and strengthen their capacity for diagnostics and 
treatment.
	
f Mobilize international support. Engage 
international organizations and financial 
institutions to provide resources and technical 
assistance equitably.

Box A3.2.  The second World Health Assembly resolution on viral hepatitis in 2014 
	
(WHA67.6): summary of Member State commitments and requests 
	
to WHO (2)
Member State commitments
	
f National strategies. Develop and implement 
coordinated multisectoral strategies for 
prevention, diagnosis and treatment, based on 
local epidemiology.
	
f Health promotion and immunization. Strengthen 
health promotion and prevention, and enhance 
immunization strategies, including for hepatitis A, 
and for hepatitis B through a timely birth dose.
	
f Civil society engagement. Involve civil society in 
all aspects of hepatitis prevention, diagnosis and 
treatment.
	
f Surveillance. Establish robust surveillance 
systems to guide evidence-based policy.
	
f Blood and organ safety. Ensure quality-assured 
screening of blood, tissues and organs, and 
promote voluntary, non-remunerated donation.
	
f Prevent perinatal hepatitis B virus transmission. 
Deliver the hepatitis B birth-dose vaccine and 
expand infant immunization coverage.
	
f Food and water safety. Strengthen measures to 
prevent hepatitis A and E through hygiene and safe 
water.
	
f Infection control. Prevent reuse of single-use 
equipment and ensure proper sterilization of 
multiuse devices.
	
f Equitable access. Guarantee prevention, diagnosis 
and treatment for vulnerable groups (e.g. 
indigenous people, migrants and people who inject 
drugs).
	
f Access to medicines. Use TRIPSa flexibilities 
and legal mechanisms to promote access to 
pharmaceuticals and technologies.
	
f Programmes for people who inject drugs. 
Implement comprehensive harm-reduction 
programmes (i.e. through nine core interventions) 
for people who inject drugs.
	
f Safe injection devices. Transition to WHO-
prequalified safety-engineered injection devices by 
2017.
	
f Anti-stigma measures. Review policies 
to eliminate discrimination and stigma in 
employment, education and travel.
Requests to WHO
	
f Technical support. Assist Member States in 
developing robust national strategies with time-
bound goals.
	
f Guidelines. Develop affordable diagnostic 
algorithms and cost-effective integration of 
hepatitis services into health systems.
	
f Monitoring and reporting. Establish systems 
for regular reporting of progress on prevention, 
diagnosis and treatment.
	
f Comprehensive access. Support equitable 
access to prevention, testing, care and treatment, 
including harm reduction for people who inject 
drugs.
	
f Blood safety guidance. Provide technical 
guidance on safe blood donation and screening 
practices.
	
f Elimination feasibility. Examine strategies and 
feasibility for eliminating hepatitis B and C, and 
consider global targets.
	
f Burden assessment. Estimate economic impact 
and disease burden at different levels: global, 
regional and national.
	
f TRIPS support. Provide technical assistance 
for using TRIPS flexibilities to improve access to 
medicines.
	
f Synergies. Maximize integration with 
noncommunicable disease prevention and other 
health programmes.
