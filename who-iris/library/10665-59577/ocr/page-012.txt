2.4 Management of parenteral and mucous membrane exposure

lt a HCW has a parenteral (e.g,, needlestick or cut) or mucous membrane
(e.g., splash to eye or mouth) exposure to blood or other body fluids, the
source patient should be assessed clinically and epidemiologically to
determine the likelihood of LAV/HTLV-1II infection. In some situations
serologic testing of the source patient may be considered. If the source
patient has evidence of LAV/HTLV-III infection, the HCW should be evaluated
clinically and serologically for evidence of LAV/HTLV-IIi infection as soon as
possible after the exposure. If the HCW is seronegative, he/she should be
retested and evaluated clinically 6 weeks later and on a periodic basis

thereafter (e.g., 3, 6 and 12 months following exposure) to determine if
transmission has occurred. During this follow-up period, especially the firet

6-12 weeks when most infected persons are expected to seroconvert, the HCW
should receive counseling about the riak of infection. The procedure outlined
above is addition to any other management protocols, éuch as for Hepatitie B.

If a patient has a parenteral or mucovs membrane exposure to blood or
other body fluids of an infected HCW, an analagous procedure to that outlined
above should be followed for both the source HCW and the potentially exposed

patient.

Rather than inatitute special precautions for knowa LAV/HTLV-III
contaminated specimens, routine precautions against transmission of blood

under the conditions deseribed. For work involving production and
purification of LAV/HTLV-IIZ, containment laboratory facilities and procedures

Manual).
