HIGHLIGHTS
• WHO continues to work with the WHO Information
Network for Epidemics (EPI-WIN) to strengthen
community engagement in pandemic influenza
preparedness. Thirteen webinars have been conducted
with the three EPI-WIN communities (Faith-based,
Youth, and World-of-Work networks) aimed at engaging
communities in pandemic influenza preparedness,
respiratory pathogen preparedness, and science
communication. These webinars focused on pressing
issues including the social determinants of health during
a pandemic, chatbots and COVID-19, and the combined
impact of influenza and COVID-19. These activities
strengthen the engagement and preparedness among
community groups.
• In 2022, five meetings and trainings were conducted to
strengthen key RCCE capacities relevant for COVID-19
and pandemic influenza – including evidence-based
science communication, developing integrated insights
for infodemic management, and promoting vaccine
demand.
• An online community knowledge platform called “Hive” 
was designed and developed during COVID-19. The
aim of this platform is to provide access to trustworthy 
information, facilitate collaboration and information 
sharing and foster communities of practice. A prototype 
of this platform was launched in July 2022, and the aim is 
to start onboarding communities on respiratory pathogen 
pandemic preparedness, including for influenza, in 2023. 
• OpenWHO continues to grow as a global learning 
platform for pandemic influenza preparedness and 
response. Users from 193 countries completed one 
of 29 influenza courses on OpenWHO. The newest 
course on seasonal influenza prevention and control 
for pandemic preparedness, has now enrolled over 
16,000 people. This platform has successfully 
demonstrated utility for pandemic response with over 
2.4 million enrolments on 46 COVID-19 courses. 
• Listening to questions and concerns from communities 
is critical for decision-makers to better predict, prepare 
for and respond to pandemics. During the COVID-19 
pandemic, WHO established the Early AI-supported 
Response and Social Listening system (EARS) to 
show real time information about how people are 
talking about COVID-19 online so as to better manage 
the infodemic and pandemic. EARS now analyzes 
publicly available data from 32 countries, with data 
collected daily from social media, online forums, news 
comments and blogs. Building on this progress, further 
work is being done on developing and implementing 
a taxonomy on acute respiratory infections including 
influenza. This information will be used to support 
community engagement in pandemic influenza 
preparedness and response.
