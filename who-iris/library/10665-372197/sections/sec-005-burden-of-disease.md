•	In 2022, two additional countries published their BOD 
estimates and 12 updated their previous findings, bringing the 
number of countries with published estimates to 50. Of the 50 
countries 68% (34) are low-middle income countries (LMICs). In 
addition, 51 have either already calculated or established a plan 
to calculate their national BOD estimates. 
•	To date, 69 countries including 23 LMICs have shared their data 
for use in regional or global BOD estimates. 
•	WHO has initiated a collaborative project with five modelling 
groups to undertake an in-depth review and comparison of 
global influenza-associated hospitalization estimates. Since 
June 2022, the Netherlands Institute for Health Services 
Research published their estimates, and once combined 
with the others, these estimates will be used to 1) develop a 
global consensus of the influenza-associated hospitalization 
burden; and 2) develop a report comparing estimates and 
methodologies. 
•	In April 2022, the Seasonal Influenza Disease Burden Estimator 
(pyramid tool) was launched online. Developed in collaboration 
with the Johns Hopkins Centre for Health Security, this tool 
supports countries with limited data to enumerate cases, 
hospitalizations, and deaths, helping country-level decision 
makers prepare more effectively for seasonal influenza epidemics 
and future influenza pandemics. This tool has been piloted in 
three countries in the Region for the Americas, with additional 
pilots ongoing in the Eastern Mediterranean Region. Feedback 
from these pilots are informing the refinement of the tool. 
•	WHO developed an excel-based tool to estimate the burden 
of influenza averted through ongoing or planned vaccination 
programmes. This tool was piloted in three countries and 
feedback from the tool has informed its further revision. 
The outputs of this tool will provide more direct evidence to 
support decisions on the introduction or expansion of influenza 
vaccination campaigns. 
•	In December 2022, WHO organized a meeting with key partners 
to discuss the development of tools and evidence for informed 
policy decisions on influenza prevention and control. In particular, 
discussions focused on the global burden of influenza-associated 
hospitalization, burden of influenza averted through vaccination, 
burden of influenza-associated non-respiratory illnesses, and the 
use of the pyramid tool for estimating the burden of influenza. 
Key recommendations from the meeting included 1) developing 
guidelines and plans on the communication of burden estimates 
and policy development; 2) integrate  the pyramid and burden 
averted through vaccination tools; 3) analyze summary estimates 
of influenza-associated hospitalization from the five global 
studies; and 4) conduct global studies to estimate the burden of 
influenza-associated non-respiratory illness across varying levels 
of severity (e.g. cardiovascular disease).
