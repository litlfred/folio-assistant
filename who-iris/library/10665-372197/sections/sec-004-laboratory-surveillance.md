•	Risk and severity assessments are critical to inform national 
and global pandemic preparedness. In 2022, 10 WHO 
influenza human-animal interface (HAI) risk assessments 
were published. 
•	In 2022, eight countries including one PC recipient country 
reported influenza epidemic severity assessments to WHO. 
58 trainings completed by seven countries in five regions 
on the pandemic influenza severity assessment (PISA) 
tool were conducted in the year. In addition, 42 countries 
from the WHO European Region reported on the intensity 
of influenza transmission. This is used to inform their 
national severity assessments, and can shape future global 
approaches for assessing pandemic influenza severity. 
•	Outbreak detection and response trainings are critical for 
pandemic influenza readiness. In 2022, 133 outbreak 
detection and response trainings were conducted in 27 
countries from five regions. Additionally, 28 country-level 
HAI risk assessments, coordination meetings, and joint 
investigations were conducted in seven countries from four 
regions. These activities strengthen influenza detection and 
response readiness.
•	In 2022, a National Influenza Centre (NIC) was newly 
recognized by WHO in one country – bringing the 
total number of NICs globally to 149 in 125 countries. 
Increasing access to NICs was the first objective for use 
of PC funds set by the PIP Advisory Group in 2013 as it 
improves data representativeness and facilitates a timely 
and effective response to an influenza pandemic. 
•	Yearly External Quality Assurance Programme (EQAP) 
panels were sent to countries to monitor, sustain, and 
drive improvements in virus detection capacity. In 
2022, the panel covered both influenza and a SARS-
CoV-2 omicron variant of concern, with 134 countries 
participating. Of the participating countries, 120 (90%) 
correctly identified all non-seasonal viruses, and 128 
(95%) correctly identified for all seasonal viruses. In 2022, 
one country participated for the first time.
•	In 2022, 68 countries from six regions benefited from 37 
laboratory training activities. These activities along with 
the yearly participation in the EQAP help improve and 
sustain quality national influenza virus detection capacity. 
•	Of 194 WHO MS, 146 reported to FluNet and 123 
reported to FluID. Most of these (141 to FluNet and 
114 to FluID) reported consistently during the influenza 
season.
•	Of the 43 PC L&S recipient countries, the proportion 
reporting to FluNet (91%) exceeded the indicator target, 
and to FluID (79%) almost met the indicator target. 
Data sharing is critical to monitor influenza activity and 
to inform risk assessments. 
•	In 2022, two countries started reporting for the first 
time to FluNet, and six countries started reported for the 
first time to FluID.  The participation of more countries 
increases the representativeness of the global systems. 
WHO supports all countries to improve and sustain 
their surveillance and global collaboration efforts. 
•	As of December 2022, 103 countries integrated 
SARS-CoV-2 into their influenza sentinel surveillance 
systems and used an established platform to share 
SARS-CoV-2 data. This increases the efficiency of 
preparedness and response.

•	1013 zoonotic influenza viruses and other IVPPs 
were characterized by GISRS in the 2022 biennium. 
The viruses were of 16 influenza A subtypes and 
originated from 18 countries Through the two 
VCM consultations in 2022, characterization led to 
the development of three new candidate vaccine 
viruses (CVV) for pandemic influenza preparedness. 
The selection and development of a zoonotic CVV 
is done to maintain a bank of viruses suitable for 
the immediate development of vaccines, including 
during a pandemic. 
•	In 2022, WHO updated and published guidance 
on the end-to-end integration of SARS-CoV-2 and 
influenza sentinel surveillance. Taking into account 
global consultations, this protocol updates the 
laboratory detection algorithms including the use of 
multiplex diagnostic methods.
•	57% (4/7) countries that reported zoonotic 
influenza cases to WHO timely shared influenza 
viruses with pandemic potential (IVPP) 
with GISRS according to WHO guidance. 
Two of these countries had exhausted 
the amount of viral material available and 
were not able to share. WHO continuously 
encourages countries to share IVPPs with 
GISRS according to global guidance. 
•	In 2022, 104 (54%) countries shared influenza 
viruses/clinical specimens at least once with 
WHO collaborating centres (CC), with 53 
(27%) sharing two timely shipments with 
WHO CCs. This was an increase compared to 
2021 (29% and 4% respectively). 
•	Using the Shipping Fund Project (SFP), 91 
countries from all six regions made 205 
shipments to WHO CCs in 2022. This is 
a marked increase in influenza sample 
sharing compared to previous years during 
the COVID-19 pandemic where there were 
significant disruptions. WHO continues to 
advocate for countries to share influenza 
samples through regional meetings, global 
webinars and pre-Vaccine Composition 
Meeting (VCM) reminders.
