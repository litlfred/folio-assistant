Acknowledgements
This training manual is the result of the collaboration of the WHO Global Health Ethics Unit (GHE) in the 
Department of Knowledge, Ethics and Research (KER), the Department of Emergency Risk Management 
and Humanitarian Response (ERM) and the Department of Pandemic and Epidemic Diseases (PED). The 
development of this manual was led by Andreas Reis (GHE) with assistance from Abha Saxena, Coordinator 
GHE, and Jonathan Abrahams, ERM department.
Special thanks go to Philippe Calain for overseeing the initial phase and design of the project and to Renaud 
Boulanger for coordinating the later phases of the project, as well as for providing research and writing 
support.
The training manual was conceived in the wake of a WHO technical consultation on research ethics in 
international epidemic response held in Geneva, Switzerland, on 10 and 11 June 2009. We are indebted to 
the members of the technical consultation for their pioneering work on this project.
Since that meeting, numerous people have shared their time, energy and expertise in the preparation of 
this manual. Richard Ashcroft, Carl Coleman, Jeremy Farrar, Ghaiath Hussein, Sergio Litewka, Lisa Schwartz, 
Ross Upshur and Mark White were involved in the initial consultation, which resulted in the learning 
objectives. Georgios Gavrilidis helped in providing a systematic bibliography, and Gabriella Lupu undertook 
proofreading in the early phase of the project. Wenzel Geissler provided useful material and directions for 
one of the modules.
We acknowledge the writers of the individual chapters for their commitment to rigorous scholarship and 
their dedication to the project as a whole: Michael Baxter, Renaud Boulanger, Philippe Calain, Caroline 
Clarinval, Carl Coleman, Leigh‑Anne Gillespie, Kenneth Goodman, Matthew Hunt, Ghaiath Hussein, Sergio 
Litewka, Dónal O’Mathúna, Elysée Nouvet, Marie‑Josée Potvin, Michael Selgelid, Lisa Schwartz, Maxwell 
Smith, Athula Sumathipala, A.M. Viens and Bryn Williams‑Jones.
We also wish to acknowledge the publishers who kindly provided permission for material to be adapted 
and/or reproduced here: the Council of State and Territorial Epidemiologists, the Public Health Agency 
Canada, The Nation and the Massachusetts Department of Public Health–Harvard Altered Standards of 
Care Working Group. Material from the WHO Casebook on ethical issues in international health research 
has also been used.
