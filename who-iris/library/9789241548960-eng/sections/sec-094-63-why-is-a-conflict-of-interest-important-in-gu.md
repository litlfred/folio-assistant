Certain data point to an association between the secondary interests of 
authors and funders and study outcomes (4), and between conflicts of interest and the conclusions drawn from systematic reviews (5). Data on the effects 
of industry relationships and the intellectual interests of guideline sponsors 
and authors are confined to case studies (6). Conflicts of interest can introduce bias into almost every step of the guideline development process, from 
scoping and the development of key questions, to the formulation and presentation of recommendations. In addition, conflicts of interest − with or 
without bias − can undermine the credibility of specific recommendations, 
the entire published guideline, and WHO as a global leader in normative 
and other work. It is thus critically important that WHO guideline developers attend to declarations and conflicts of interest in a proactive, reasoned, 
transparent and defensible manner.
