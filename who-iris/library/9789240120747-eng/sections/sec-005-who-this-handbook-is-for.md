## Who this handbook is for

This handbook has been developed for countries, organizations and teams seeking to plan and implement digital solutions for reporting substandard and falsified medical products by health care professionals and aims to provide guidance to the various target audiences across the different implementation phases and areas, as outlined in Table 1.

**Table 1. Intended audience for the handbook**

| Handbook section – phases | Illustrative target organizations | Illustrative target user teams and roles | Purpose of the section |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Assess the current state and enabling environment | Ministry of health<br>NMRA<br>Digital health departments | Senior management/leaders<br>Technical/systems team<br>Regulatory/monitoring/pharmacovigilance teams | To help assess the current situation. This includes identifying existing barriers to reporting, assessing the current digital landscape and readiness and reviewing the current procedures and policies that will inform the digital solution. |
| 2. Establish a shared understanding and governance framework | Ministry of health<br>NMRA<br>Digital health departments<br>Other stakeholders such as funders and implementation partners, medical product industry representatives, health care professional associations | Senior management/leaders<br>Regulatory/monitoring/pharmacovigilance teams<br>Health care professional association representatives | To help define goals and establish leadership and governance framework. This outlines the importance of fostering cooperation among stakeholders, setting clear objectives and developing a strategic plan that aligns with national digital health strategies. |
| 3. Define the future state | Ministry of health<br>NMRA<br>Digital health departments<br>Other stakeholders such as funders and implementation partners, medical product industry representatives, health care professional associations | Senior management/leaders<br>Regulatory staff<br>Technical/enterprise architects and team<br>Regulatory/monitoring/pharmacovigilance teams<br>Health care professional association representatives | To provide guidance on developing an investment roadmap and aligning the reporting application with a holistic enterprise architecture. |
| 4. Determine the requirements | Ministry of health<br>NMRA<br>Digital health departments<br>Other stakeholders such as funders and implementation partners | Technical/enterprise architects and team<br>Regulatory/monitoring/pharmacovigilance teams | To help define the reporting application’s functional and non-functional requirements, based on user scenarios. |
| 5. Plan the implementation | Ministry of health<br>NMRA<br>Digital health departments | Project managers<br>Technical/system/enterprise architects and team | To provide guidance on developing a comprehensive plan and budget to implement the substandard and falsified reporting application. |
| 6. Monitor, evaluate and scale | Ministry of health<br>National regulatory authority<br>Implementation partners | Technical team<br>Regulatory/monitoring/pharmacovigilance teams | To help develop a framework for monitoring and evaluation (M&E) of the performance and effectiveness of the substandard and falsified reporting application. |
| 7. Share implementation experiences | Ministry of health<br>National regulatory authority<br>Implementation partners | All:<br>Senior management/leaders<br>Technical/systems team<br>Regulatory/monitoring/pharmacovigilance teams<br>Health care professional association representatives<br>Regulatory staff<br>Technical/enterprise architects and team<br>Project managers | To share real-world implementation experiences and lessons learned. |
