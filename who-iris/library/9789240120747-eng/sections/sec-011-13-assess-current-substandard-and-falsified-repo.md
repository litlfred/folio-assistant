## 1.3 Assess current substandard and falsified reporting practices

**Current practices.** Conduct an analysis of current practices, challenges and barriers faced in reporting substandard and falsified medical products. The outputs of the analysis should be used to develop optimal business processes and workflows within the application. It is important to examine substandard and falsified reporting procedures and their challenges, alongside pharmacovigilance procedures to identify potential opportunities for synergy. The integration of these processes can enhance their mutual effectiveness in reporting. As part of this analysis key pharmacovigilance structures, systems, procedures and mechanisms within the country should be identified. This could include the existence of basic infrastructure to enable pharmacovigilance operations and reporting, policy and regulatory frameworks, and the collection, analysis and evaluation of adverse drug reactions.

- **Data collection tools.** Evaluate the current data collection tools and processes for pharmacovigilance and substandard and falsified medical products reporting. This typically involves a reporting form that gathers all essential information on a suspected adverse drug reaction or substandard and falsified medical product. Various tools are used to deploy these reporting forms across countries, including electronic or digital forms (such as applications or web-based forms), paper-based forms or a combination of both.
- **Digital tools.** Conduct a landscape analysis to identify existing or previously used software applications and other tools related to medical products or similar use (e.g. pharmacovigilance reporting tools) as well as related digital technologies. Identify current registry systems such as product registry and facility registry and if an interoperability layer exists. This inventory aids in identifying opportunities for reuse of existing applications and the required interoperability across systems. In some countries digital tools may already be in use for pharmacovigilance activities. If digital tools are already in use for pharmacovigilance reporting, it is advantageous to incorporate substandard and falsified reporting into existing pharmacovigilance applications. Assess how the tools are currently utilized, including the information flow from the receipt of the report to the integration of the data into global databases and the sequence of this process. This aspect is as crucial as confirming the existence of a data collection tool, as it ensures that any collected data are fully utilized and provided to the relevant organizations. Sample questions to support this evaluation are provided in Table 4.

Consider using the *Classification of digital health interventions v1.0* (34) to systematically categorize the digital solutions as part of the landscape analysis. See Annex 5 for a template for listing existing software applications.

**Barriers.** Understanding the barriers to reporting substandard and falsified medical products is crucial for the effective implementation of reporting systems. Identify these barriers and understand the reasons behind them. This ensures that the design and deployment of reporting tools are tailored to meet the needs of NMRAs, health care professionals and other stakeholders. This understanding is vital for creating a robust and efficient reporting system that can mitigate the challenges faced by users, thereby enhancing the overall effectiveness of the reporting process. Various potential barriers are outlined in Table 5 along with potential solutions that can be considered and evaluated within the country context when developing the reporting solution. Further analysis of barriers is provided in Annex 6.

**Table 4. Assessing current substandard and falsified reporting practices**

| Area | Actions and guiding questions |
| --- | --- |
| Assessing data collection for pharmacovigilance | • Confirm whether there is a standard adverse drug reaction reporting form in your country.<br>• Confirm whether there is a standard adverse drug reaction reporting form available to the general public.<br>• If there is a form, identify whether it contains relevant fields to report suspected medication errors.<br>• Identify whether there are relevant fields to report suspected substandard and falsified medical products.<br>• Identify whether there are relevant fields to report therapeutic failure or inefficacy. |
| Assessing data collection for substandard and falsified medicines | • Confirm whether there is a standard reporting form for substandard and falsified medical products used in your country, and whether it is independent of the standard adverse drug reaction reporting form.<br>• If there is a reporting form present, document the data fields. These could include:<br>  - product name<br>  - product type<br>  - registration or marketing authorization number<br>  - manufacturer<br>  - expiry date<br>  - adverse reaction reported or harm caused<br>  - outcome of adverse reaction<br>  - estimated number of patients affected<br>  - option to upload a photo if form is digital. |
| Guiding questions to explore data usage | • Is there a process in place for collection, recording and analysis of adverse drug reaction reports?<br>  - Does an information flow chain exist including causality assessments, feedback to reporters and an electronic database?<br>  - Are adverse drug reaction reports flowing into global databases (e.g. Vigilance hub, Vigiflow and Vigibase)?<br>  - What percentage of reports deemed satisfactory and submitted to the national pharmacovigilance centre were passed into global databases?<br>• Is there a process in place for collection, recording and analysis of substandard and falsified medical product reports?<br>  - Does an information flow chain exist including feedback for reporters and an electronic database?<br>  - Are reports of substandard and falsified medical products flowing into global databases (for example, the GSMS)? |

**Table 5. Common challenges and barriers to substandard and falsified reporting and potential solutions**

| Category | Challenges and barriers | Potential solutions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Health care professional capacity | • Health care professionals overburdened with work<br>• Time constraints<br>• Fear of repercussions | • Integrating app into existing workflow<br>• Automating data capture<br>• Minimizing manual data entry<br>• Reducing data requirements to the minimum needed for further analysis<br>• Protect the identity of reporter |
| Regulatory support | • Under-resourced NMRAs<br>• Lack of legal authority<br>• Lack of trained personnel | • Designing features for report processing, follow-up and feedback<br>• Providing training and resources<br>• Integrating NMRA feedback<br>• Legislating need for reporting by health care professionals |
| Health care system practices | • Polypharmacy, the use of multiple medicines by one patient, preventing health care professionals from pinpointing the effects of individual medicines<br>• Lack of continuity of care | While polypharmacy and lack of continuity of care are beyond the scope of reporting technology, the digital solution can be designed to account for these challenges by:<br>• providing detailed guidance on how to report suspect products in the context of polypharmacy<br>• designing the solution to be flexible and adaptable to different health care settings |
| Economic factors | • Economic and reputational damage for suppliers of having to recall medical products<br>• Cost of internet access and data | • Providing offline reporting options that can sync online when on Wi-Fi network<br>• Providing financial incentives for reporting |
| Social factors | • Fear of reporting due to reputational, professional or personal reasons<br>• Fear of legal consequences | • Providing options for anonymous reporting<sup>a</sup><br>• Incorporating regulatory and legal protection as part of reporting and investigative processes |
| Education | • Lack of awareness substandard and falsified medical products and how to recognize them | • Integrating guidance on substandard and falsified medical products detection within the reporting application |
| Technology | • Lack of digitalization of the reporting process, requiring health care professionals to use lengthy or paper-based reporting systems<br>• Lack of, or limited, connectivity | • A user-friendly reporting solution that is intuitive and appropriately automated<br>• Providing offline reporting options that can sync online when on Wi-Fi network |

*Notes:* <sup>a</sup> Anonymizing the reporting of suspected substandard and falsified medical products is highly valuable because it encourages more reports by reducing fear of legal, professional or personal repercussions. It protects vulnerable individuals, such as health care professionals, patients or whistleblowers, particularly in environments where retaliation is a risk. Anonymity also helps focus investigations on the evidence rather than the identity of the reporter, building public trust in the reporting system. However, challenges include difficulties in following up for more details, assessing credibility and managing potential false reports.
