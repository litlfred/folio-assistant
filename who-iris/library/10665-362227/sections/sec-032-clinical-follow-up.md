Follow-up is generally limited to those with persistent symptoms after a stipulated period after the recommended treatment. 
Clients may return for further assessment if the condition has not resolved or it has recurred. The health care provider will 
need to determine the reason for the user’s return to guide next steps (see Box 10).
Box 10. Reasons for STI treatment failure that need to be assessed to guide further treatment
•	 Adherence: such as a client failing to complete a 7-day or 21-day course of doxycycline for the treatment of chlamydia, 
including for lymphogranuloma venereum (LVG).
•	 Reinfection: perhaps because sex took place without a condom with an untreated sex partner or a new partner.
•	 Antimicrobial resistance: of particular importance in gonococcal and M. genitalium infections.
•	 Other untreated infection: such as T. vaginalis and/or M. genitalium when urethral discharge was treated only for 
N. gonorrhoeae and C. trachomatis at the first visit.
1	 WHO does not have specific guidelines for the treatment of trichomoniasis or other STIs, but treatments can be found in the WHO guideline for the management of 
symptomatic STIs (84). 
1	 WHO is currently revising its treatment recommendations and dosages for gonorrhoea due to increasing antimicrobial resistance to azithromycin and reduced susceptibility to 
cephalosporins.
