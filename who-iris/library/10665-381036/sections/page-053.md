Selection criteria   
We included cross-sectional and cohort studies that evaluated the accuracy of Xpert Ultra for 
diagnosing pulmonary or extrapulmonary tuberculosis and rifampicin resistance in children 0 to 9 
years of age with signs and symptoms of tuberculosis. For the detection of pulmonary and 
extrapulmonary tuberculosis, we used a microbiological reference standard (MRS, solid or liquid 
culture) and a CRS. For the evaluation of Xpert Ultra on sputum and gastric aspirates we allowed 
culture to be performed on sputum and gastric aspirate specimens, respectively. For evaluation of 
Xpert Ultra in stool, we included Xpert Ultra results in a traditional specimen (gastric aspirate or 
sputum), in addition to culture (gastric aspirate or sputum), for the MRS. We did not compare to 
culture on stool due to the known poor performance of Mycobacterium tuberculosis culture on stool. 
For the evaluation of Xpert Ultra on nasopharyngeal aspirates we allowed culture to be performed on 
a sputum, gastric, or nasopharyngeal aspirate specimens due to difficulty in collecting an adequate 
nasopharyngeal aspirate sample for culture, but did not include Xpert on those specimens to avoid the 
potential for incorporation bias. The CRS was defined as a positive test by culture or a clinical 
decision to initiate treatment for tuberculosis. In the absence of data on tuberculosis treatment 
initiation, we accepted a research definition if available. For the detection of rifampicin resistance, the 
reference standards were culture-based phenotypic drug susceptibility testing or whole 
genome/targeted sequencing for molecular resistance. Studies were eligible for inclusion if they 
described the use of Xpert Ultra in sputum to test for rifampicin resistance. 
  
Data collection and analysis   
Owing to the need for data stratified by age and comorbidity, we contacted and requested data directly 
from the authors of all identified reports and studies that met the inclusion criteria following full-text 
review. All disaggregated data received were included. We assessed study quality using the 
QUADAS-2 tool tailored to this review. We performed meta-analyses using a bivariate random-
effects model to estimate the summary sensitivity and specificity of Xpert Ultra separately for each 
specimen type. We investigated potential sources of heterogeneity by age group, nutrition and HIV 
status. We considered an Xpert Ultra trace result as positive (Mycobacterium tuberculosis detected) 
consistent with the recommendation that an Ultra trace result is sufficient to initiate therapy in those 
with known or suspected HIV infection and in children (WHO Xpert Ultra 2017; WHO Consolidated 
Guidelines (Module 3) 2020). 
  
Assessment of certainty of the evidence 
Certainty of evidence assessments were determined using the GRADE approach for reviews of 
diagnostic accuracy studies.  We made  judgments separately for sensitivity and specificity. We 
applied GRADE as recommended (Schünemann 2020a; Schünemann 2020b).
