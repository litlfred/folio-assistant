In the patient selection domain, we judged all six studies to be at low risk of bias. Regarding applicability, 
four studies (66.7%) had low concern because the studies included appropriate participants and settings 
[Chipinduro 2017; Hanrahan 2018; Jaganath 2024]. We judged one study (16.7%, [Orikiriza 2021]) to 
have high concern, as these studies exclusively recruited participants in inpatient settings at tertiary 
level  and two studies (33.3%) to have unclear concern because index tests were performed in 
centralized reference laboratories, or we could not tell [LaCourse 2018; Olbrich 2024].  
In the index test domain, we thought that five studies (83.3%) had low risk of bias [Hanrahan 2018; 
Jaganath 2024; LaCourse 2018; Olbrich 2024; Orikiriza 2021]. We judged one study (16.7%, [Chipinduro 
2017]) to have unclear risk of bias as blinding procedures were not reported.

unclear risk of bias as the CRS only included tuberculosis treatment decision [Chipinduro 2017]. All other 
studies (83.3%) were judged as having a high risk of bias as these included both clinical features as well
