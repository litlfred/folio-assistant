For children without HIV or HIV unknown, in the patient selection domain, we judged 11 studies (91.6%) 
to have low risk of bias. One study (8.3%; [Donkeng 2022]) had unclear risk of bias. Regarding 
applicability, eight studies (66.7%) had low concern because the studies included the appropriate 
participants and settings  [Ainan 2021; Babo 2023; Hanrahan 2018; Hasan 2017; Jaganath 2024; Olbrich 
2024; M. P. Nicol 2013; Wobudeya 2024]. We judged three studies (25.0%) to have high concern, as 
these studies exclusively recruited participants in inpatient settings [Chabala 2024; Kabir 2021; Orikiriza 
2021]. We judged one study (8.3%, [Donkeng 2022]) to have unclear concern because index tests were 
performed in centralized reference laboratories or we could not tell [Donkeng 2022]. For CHIV, out of 
the seven studies, one was judged at unclear risk of bias in the patient selection domain (14.3% 
[Donkeng 2022]), while all other had a low risk of bias. For applicability, one study had a high concern as 
they only recruited children in inpatient settings [Orikiriza 2021]. 
In the index test domain, we judged all studies to have low risk of bias and no applicability concerns, as 
the results of LC-aNAAT (index tests) were automatically generated, except for one study for CHIV as we 
could not tell [Chipinduro 2017].  
In the reference standard domain, we judged all eight studies (100%) based on a MRS to have low risk of 
bias as all followed our harmonized definition. For the CRS, eleven studies (91.7%) for children without 
HIV and six (85.7%) for CHIV were judged as high risk of bias as these included the index test results in 
their reference standard, nine (75.0%) of which included both clinical features as well as the index test 
[Babo 2023; Chabala 2024; Hanrahan 2018; Jaganath 2024; M. P. Nicol 2013; Olbrich 2024; Orikiriza 
2021; Wobudeya 2024] [Chipinduro 2017; LaCourse 2018]. One study (8.3%) for children without HIV 
and one study (14.3%) for CHIV had unclear risk of bias as the CRS only involved clinical features to 
confirm tuberculosis [Hasan 2017] [Donkeng 2022].  
Regarding applicability, we judged 10 studies (83.3%) for children without HIV to have low concern as 
these studies performed a test to identify M tuberculosis species (speciation) for the MRS [Chabala 
2024; Hasan 2017; Jaganath 2024; M. P. Nicol 2013; Olbrich 2024; Orikiriza 2021] and used appropriate 
criteria for CRS [Ainan 2021; Babo 2023; Kabir 2021; Wobudeya 2024]. For two studies (16.7%), we could 
not tell if speciation was performed and therefore judged to have unclear concern [Donkeng 2022; 
Hanrahan 2018].
