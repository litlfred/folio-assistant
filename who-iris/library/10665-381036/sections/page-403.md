Pooran et al., 2022 determined the cost-effectiveness of five different testing strategies [Smear 
microscopy, MGIT, Adenosine deaminase, Interferon-gamma Release Immuno-Suspension Assay or 
(IRISA-TB)] for pleural TB diagnosis in South Africa (51). The Ultra baseline test was $13.28. IRISA-TB was 
the most cost-effective strategy, as the cost per pleural TB patient diagnosed and initiated on treatment 
was $53,181. Ultra was one of the tests found to be less cost-effective than any of the biomarker-based 
assays, mainly due to poor sensitivity. The cost per pleural TB patient diagnosed and initiated on 
treatment using Ultra was $ 114,869. 
 
Thompson et al., 2023 evaluated the cost and cost-effectiveness of centralised testing compared to 
decentralised testing with Ultra (52). The costs of decentralised and centralised testing were found to be 
similar. However, decentralised testing resulted in more patients receiving appropriate Xpert testing. The 
cost of centralised and decentralised testing was $21.96 and $24.68, respectively, and the cost-
effectiveness results were $834 and $1616 per additional diagnosis, respectively.  
 
Ultra cost-effectiveness studies with health utility outcomes (n=3) 
Fekadu et al., 2023 conducted a model-based cost-effectiveness analysis of sputum-based Xpert as a 
single test and concurrently with urine-based FujiLAM or AlereLAM among HIV-infected individuals with 
signs and symptoms of TB disease (48). The analysis included costs from the literature and publicly 
available data, shown in Table 2. The results of this study showed that Ultra with FujiLAM was the 
preferred, cost-effective option, ICER ($770.44/DALY averted) less than the WTP threshold 
($8,029.97/DALY averted).  
 
Mafirakureva et al., 2022 assessed the cost-effectiveness of implementing the simple one-step stool 
method at the primary healthcare level to diagnose paediatric TB in Ethiopia and Indonesia (39). The cost 
per test was $31.41 in Ethiopia and $28.59 in Indonesia (Table 4). The ICERs were $159.24 and $113.40 
per DALY averted in the base-case analyses for Ethiopia and Indonesia, respectively.
