Diagnostic accuracy of the component tests: LC-aNAATs combined or urine LF-
LAM based on the microbiological reference standard  
The sensitivity of LC-aNAATs combined alone ranged from 25.0% [Orikiriza 2021] to 85.7% [Jaganath 
2024] and the specificity from 90.0% ([Olbrich 2024]) to 100% [Hanrahan 2018; Orikiriza 2021] between 
studies. The pooled sensitivity and specificity for LC-aNAATs combined alone were 69.3% (95% CrI 49.9 
to 84.0) and 95.4% (95% CrI 91.1 to 98.0), (six studies, 653 participants, 43 [6.6%] of which had 
tuberculosis). 
The sensitivity of LF-LAM alone ranged from 7.1% [Olbrich 2024] to 50.0% [Orikiriza 2021] and the 
specificity from 69.7% [Jaganath 2024] to 97.0% [Olbrich 2024] between studies. The pooled sensitivity 
and specificity for LF-LAM alone were 27.4% (95% CrI 13.5 to 47.5) and 88.2% (95% CrI 78.2 to 93.8), (six 
studies, 653 participants, 43 [6.6%] of which had tuberculosis). See Appendix 18: Forest plots of test 
accuracy for LC-aNAATs combined and LF-LAM alone, MRS (PICO 2). 
Incremental diagnostic accuracy of LC-aNAATs combined and LF-LAM versus LC-
aNAAT combined alone based on the microbiological reference standard 
Incremental difference in sensitivity for parallel use of LC-aNAATs combined and LF-LAM versus LC-
aNAATs combined alone ranged from 0% [Jaganath 2024; LaCourse 2018; Olbrich 2024] to 11.1% 
[Chipinduro 2017] between studies. The difference in specificity for parallel use versus LC-aNAATs 
combined alone ranged from 2.0% [Olbrich 2024] to -28.0% [Jaganath 2024]. See Figure 16. 
The pooled difference in accuracy was 6.9% (95% CrI 1.5 to 20.1; 95% PI 0.8 to 33.6) for sensitivity and 
−10.2% (95% CrI -19.6 to -4.9; 95% PI -43.0 to -1.6) for specificity. See Figure 17.

and specificity estimates together with the credible and prediction regions, MRS (PICO 2).
