Diagnostic accuracy of the component tests, respiratory LC-aNAAT and urine 
LF-LAM, based on the microbiological reference standard  
The sensitivity of respiratory LC-aNAAT alone ranged from 24% to 100%, and specificity from 91% to 
100%. The pooled sensitivity LC-aNAAT alone was 68.0% (95% CrI 60.8 to 74.9) and specificity 96.7% 
(95% CrI 95.7 to 97.6), (27 studies; 12,651 participants, 2,368 (18.7%) with tuberculosis). 
The sensitivity of urine LF-LAM alone ranged from 0% to 75%, and specificity ranged from 59% to 
100%. The pooled sensitivity of LF-LAM alone was 39.1% (95% CrI 32.6 to 45.9) and specificity 91.9% 
(95% CrI 88.7 to 94.4), (27 studies; 12,651 participants, 2,368 (18.7%) with tuberculosis) 
See Appendix 13: Forest plots of individual test accuracy for respiratory LC-aNAAT and urine LF-LAM.  
Incremental accuracy of parallel testing versus LC-aNAAT alone based on the 
microbiological reference standard  
Within studies, difference in sensitivity for parallel use of respiratory LC-aNAAT and urine LF-LAM 
versus respiratory LC-aNAAT alone ranged from 0% [Bjerrum 2015; Kebede 2021; Liu 2022; Olbrich 
2023; Spooner 2022] to 38% [Gupta-Wright 2018]. The difference in specificity for parallel testing 
versus LC-aNAAT alone ranged from 0% [Broger-Cohort4 2020; Kebede 2021] to -34% [Huerga 2019]. 
See (Figure 12).  
The pooled difference in sensitivity and pooled difference in specificity between parallel testing 
versus LC-aNAAT alone were 6.7% (95% CrI 3.8 to 10.7; 95% prediction interval (PI) 0.6% to 45.9) and 
-6.8% (95% CrI -9.5 to -4.7; 95% PI -32.8 to -6.8). See (Figure 13).  
See Appendix 14: SROC plot for parallel testing and LC-aNAAT pooled sensitivity and specificity 
estimates together with the credible and prediction regions.
