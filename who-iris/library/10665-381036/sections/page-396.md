3.1.1. Methodological characteristics of included full economic evaluation studies. 
 
Xpert cost-effectiveness studies (n=10) 
Seven of the ten studies were done from the provider perspective (20–24,27,28), one from a clinical trial 
perspective (26) and two from a societal perspective (19,25). All full economic evaluation studies 
performed a variation of cost-effectiveness analyses (CEA), four presenting utility outcomes, three of 
which presented Quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) (19,25,26)and one disability adjusted life years 
(DALYs) (27).   
 
Ultra cost-effectiveness studies (n=6) 
All included studies adopted a provider perspective, either that of the healthcare system (32–35,37) or 
the public sector (36). The type of analysis varied, as shown in Table 3. Only half of the studies (n=3) 
conducted primary costing (33–35). The cost items included in the three studies with primary costing are 
shown in Table 3.  
Two studies that presented utility outcomes employed disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) (32,37). 
Modelling was conducted in 4 studies, three were decision tree models (32,34,37), and one was an 
integrated transmission-dynamic model (36). In all studies, at the minimum, one-way sensitivity analyses 
were performed.   
 
TrueNat (n=1) 
Using decision tree analysis, Bhatia et al. conducted a model-based cost-utility analysis from the health 
system perspective. Costs were derived from the literature, and the primary health effectiveness 
outcome was DALYs.
