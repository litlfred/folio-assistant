complement each other since the sensitivity gain is larger than the specificity loss among children 
who are respiratory culture positive for M. tuberculosis. The greater loss of specificity may reflect 
false positive results, but also detection of tuberculosis in respiratory culture-negative tuberculosis 
disease, reflecting that the MRS might underestimate true pulmonary tuberculosis in children. We 
found similar conclusions when we performed the analysis among CHIV, although the incremental 
change sensitivity was smaller. This may reflect the lower sensitivity of molecular TB testing on 
respiratory specimens in those living with HIV, as they are more likely to have paucibacillary disease.  
Based on CRS, the sensitivity for parallel testing and LC-aNAAT of each sample type decreased for 
children with and without HIV. This reflects that the CRS definition of tuberculosis includes children 
who were diagnosed clinically and could be culture- and LC-aNAAT-negative. Among children 
without HIV or unclear HIV status, the sensitivity of parallel testing and LC-aNAAT respiratory testing 
had a similar decrease, and so the added sensitivity from parallel testing remained the same. LC-
aNAAT stool had a smaller decrease in sensitivity, and so consequently, the added sensitivity of 
parallel testing was smaller.   
The main analysis included all children enrolled in the studies regardless of available or valid LC-
aNAAT results. In sensitivity analysis, participants with at least one invalid or missing index test were 
excluded, and as expected the sensitivity of individual tests increased as the denominator 
decreased, and the sensitivity of parallel testing was similar (both numerator and denominator 
decreased). Consequently, the estimated difference between parallel and individual testing had a 
reduction in sensitivity; specificity remained similar. In addition, when we limited analysis to studies 
with low risk of bias and low applicability concerns, overall estimates remained similar as overall 
quality was high per QUADAS-2 criteria for the primary analysis.
