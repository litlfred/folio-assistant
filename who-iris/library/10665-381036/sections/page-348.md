The 31 studies were conducted in 19 different countries, four (21%) in low-income countries, 10

4.4 PICO 1 on incremental diagnostic accuracy  
PICO 1: What is the incremental diagnostic accuracy of parallel use of respiratory LC-aNAATs and 
urine LF-LAM for diagnosis of tuberculosis disease in adults and adolescents with HIV who present 
with presumptive tuberculosis, compared to any of the tests alone?  
Of 31 studies, 27 studies evaluated diagnostic accuracy against a microbiological reference standard 
and 23 studies against a composite reference standard, with 20 studies evaluating accuracy against 
both reference standards.  
Main results are presented below with summary of findings tables in Appendix 19.  
4.4.1 Comparative diagnostic accuracy based on the microbiological reference 
standard  
Description of included studies  
We identified 27 studies that compared the accuracy of the parallel strategy of LC-aNAAT and LF-
LAM versus each of the tests alone using the microbiological reference standard, involving 12,651 
participants, 2,368 (18.7%) with tuberculosis.  
Tuberculosis prevalence, according to the microbiological reference standard, ranged from 5% in 
[Åhsberg 2023] to 71% in [Boloko 2022]. For 11 of 27 studies, the reference standard was exclusively 
based on sputum culture (prompting high risk of bias judgement) while 16 studies had a higher 
quality reference standard in which both respiratory and extrapulmonary samples were included for 
reference standard testing [Åhsberg 2023; Benjamin 2019; Boloko 2022; Garcia 2020; Gupta-Wright 
2018; Huerga 2023a; Huerga 2017a; Lawn 2017b; Liu 2022; Olbrich 2023; J.G. Peter 2016; Shah 
2014; Spooner 2022; Stead 2023; Szekely 2022; Van Hoving 2019]. The number and type of samples 
obtained for the reference standard varied. In [Åhsberg 2023] non-respiratory Xpert or culture was 
not standardized (relied on routine care) and in [Gupta-Wright 2018], sputum culture was not 
standardized (relied on routine care), such that the reference standard could have been exclusively 
based on either respiratory or non-respiratory samples alone.
