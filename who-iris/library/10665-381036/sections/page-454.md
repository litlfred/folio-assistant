Background 
Tuberculosis (TB) continues to be among the world’s top causes of mortality while drug resistant TB (DR-
TB) is the leading cause of death due to anti-microbial resistant disease and poses a global security risk.1 
2 According to the WHO Global Tuberculosis Report published in 2023,1 about 10.3 million people 
developed TB and 410,000 people developed multidrug rifampicin resistant TB (MDR-TB/RR-TB) globally. 
There were an estimated 1.3 million deaths attributed to TB. Deaths due to TB were higher in HIV-negative 
people (1.16 million deaths) compared to HIV-positive people (167, 000 deaths). Ending the TB epidemic 
by 2030 is also among the health-related targets of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals.3 
4  
Prompt and accurate detection of TB is essential in guiding appropriate treatment and reducing 
transmission, morbidity and mortality due to TB.5 Specifically, microbiological detection of TB is important 
in facilitating correct diagnosis, testing for DR-TB and guiding appropriate treatment depending on pattern 
of drug resistance. In 2021, 5.3 million people were diagnosed with pulmonary TB, 63% of whom were 
microbiologically confirmed. The highest microbiological detection rates were in the Americas and lowest 
in Western Pacific regions with a lot of variation between countries.1  There are several new diagnostic 
tests for TB but sputum smear microscopy continues to be used despite its limited sensitivity in detecting 
TB and DR-TB. Mycobacterial culture has a high sensitivity but takes long to yield results (weeks to months 
depending on setting).6  
 
Molecular TB tests, Nucleic Acid Amplification Tests (NAATs) have been recommended by WHO as initial 
diagnostic tests for TB due to their high accuracy, rapid results and impact on patient important 
outcomes.5 The WHO has introduced different classes of NAATs depending on their infrastructure needs 
and mode of conduct. They include low complexity manual NAATs for the detection of TB (e.g. TB-Loop 
Mediated Isothermal Amplification(TB-LAMP)), low complexity automated NAATs for detection of TB and 
resistance to rifampicin, isoniazid and second line anti TB drugs (e.g. standard Xpert MTB/RIF, Xpert 
MTB/RIF Ultra, Truenat plus), moderate complexity automated NAATs for detection of TB and resistance 
to rifampicin and isoniazid (e.g. Fluorotype MTB DR, Bioneer) and high complexity hybridization-based 
NAATs for detection of resistance to pyrazinamide (e.g. Genoscholar).5 7 The eligible tests for this review 
are detailed in the methods section.
