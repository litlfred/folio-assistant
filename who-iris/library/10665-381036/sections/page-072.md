Detection of extrapulmonary tuberculosis 
12. Should LC-aNAATs on cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) be used to diagnose 
tuberculous meningitis in children with signs and symptoms of 
tuberculous meningitis, against a microbiological reference standard? 
 
 
Figure 24. Risk of bias and applicability summary for extrapulmonary tuberculosis using CSF and a 
microbiological reference standard  
Three studies (215 participants) were included with sensitivities of 67% to 100% and specificities of 
91% to 100% (Figure 25). Meta-analysis was not performed due to the small number of cases. 
For a population of 1,000 children where 200 have tuberculosis on culture, 134 to 272 would be 
Xpert Ultra positive; of these, 134 to 200 would have tuberculosis meningitis (true positives) and 0 to 
72 would not have tuberculosis meningitis (false positives); 728 to 866 would be Xpert Ultra 
negative; of these 728 to 800 would not have tuberculosis meningitis (true negatives) and 0 to 66 
would have tuberculosis meningitis (false-negatives).
