8. Should LC-aNAATs on nasopharyngeal aspirate specimens be used to 
diagnose pulmonary tuberculosis in children with signs and symptoms or 
screened positive for pulmonary tuberculosis, against a composite 
reference standard? 
 
Figure 16. Risk of bias and applicability summary for pulmonary tuberculosis: nasopharyngeal 
aspirate and composite reference standard 
 
Four studies (676 participants) evaluating nasopharyngeal aspirate against a CRS were included with 
sensitivities between 9% and 36% and specificities of 100% (Figure 17). All four studies were 
included in the meta-analysis. The summary sensitivity (95% CI) was 12.7% (9.2 to 17.3) and the 
summary specificity (95% CI) was 100.0% (98.3 to 100.0) (moderate-certainty (sensitivity and 
specificity) evidence). 
 
For a population of 1,000 children where 100 have tuberculosis on CRS, 13 would be Xpert Ultra 
positive; of these, 13 would have pulmonary tuberculosis (true positives) and 0 would not have 
pulmonary tuberculosis (false positives); 987 would be Xpert Ultra negative; of these 900 would not 
have pulmonary tuberculosis (true negatives) and 87 would have pulmonary tuberculosis (false-
negatives). 
 
 
Figure 17. Xpert Ultra for pulmonary tuberculosis using nasopharyngeal aspirate and a composite 
reference standard. FN: false negative; FP: false positive; TN: true negative; TP: true positive.
