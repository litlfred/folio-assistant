4.7.2 Impact of respiratory LC-aNAATs and urine LF-LAM on cure and loss-to-
follow up in adult inpatients with HIV 
No trials evaluating diagnostic strategies including respiratory LC-aNAATs and urine LF-LAM were 
identified that reported data on cure or loss-to-follow up. 
 
4.7.3 Impact of respiratory LC-aNAATs and urine LF-LAM on time to diagnosis 
and proportion diagnosed with tuberculosis in adult inpatients with HIV 
Two trials evaluated time to diagnosis, reporting median and interquartile ranges, which precluded 
meta-analysis of mean differences. With study-directed, lab-based urine LF-LAM and urine LC-aNAAT, 
Gupta-Wright 2018 reported that the time from randomization to diagnosis was shorter in the 
intervention group: median 0 days (IQR 0 to 1), versus 1 day (IQR 0 to 6) in the control group. The 
adjusted hazard ratio was 1.55 (95% CI 1.29 to 1.87) i.e. participants in the intervention group were 1.55 
times more likely to be diagnosed with tuberculosis per unit time (days) than those in the control group. 
With clinician directed urine LF-LAM testing, interpretation, and treatment, Åhsberg 2023 reported that 
patients were diagnosed with tuberculosis after a shorter time in the intervention group than in the 
standard-of-care group (median, 0 days [IQR 0 to 2] versus 2 days [IQR 0 to 7]; P = 0.037). 
Gupta-Wright 2018 reported increases in tuberculosis diagnoses (either microbiologically confirmed 
[any test positive] or clinically diagnosed) in the intervention group, in which 22% of the participants 
were diagnosed with tuberculosis, compared to the standard-of-care group, in which 15% of participants 
were diagnosed with tuberculosis, with an overall adjusted absolute risk difference of 7.3% (95% CI 4.4 
to 10.2). Åhsberg 2023 reported more patients in the intervention group (59/173 (34.1% [95% CI 27.1% 
to 41.7%]) were diagnosed with tuberculosis (either microbiologically confirmed or clinically diagnosed) 
compared to the standard-of-care group that included LC-aNAAT but did not include LF-LAM (46/246 
(18.7% [95% CI 14.0% to 24.1%]). The aRR for being diagnosed with tuberculosis in the intervention 
group was 2.0 (95% CI 1.9 to 2.1) compared with the standard-of-care group.  
The pooled RR of diagnosis was 1.56 (95% CI 1.29 to 1.88), indicating that the intervention group had 
1.56 times the risk of being diagnosed with tuberculosis (either microbiologically confirmed or clinically 
diagnosed) compared to the standard-of-care (Figure 16).
