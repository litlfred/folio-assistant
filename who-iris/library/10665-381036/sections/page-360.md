4.7.1 Impact of respiratory LC-aNAATs and urine LF-LAM on mortality in adult 
inpatients with HIV 
Three trials provided data on the impact of parallel testing with respiratory LC-aNAAT and urine LF-LAM 
on mortality, compared to respiratory LC-aNAAT alone, with differences in trial specific intervention 
characteristics (Table 11). The pooled risk ratio for the impact of parallel testing of LF-LAM and 
respiratory LC-aNAAT on mortality was 0.93 (95% CI 0.74 to 1.17) using a random-effects meta-analysis 
(I2 = 72%) (Figure 15).  
Using beside urine LF-LAM and study directed treatment recommendations, Peter 2016, reported an 
absolute risk reduction in mortality of 4% (1% to 7%) (Table 11). Using a combination intervention that 
included study directed lab-based testing of study participants to receive both urine LC-aNAAT and LF-
LAM, with blinding of specific test results to clinical teams, Gupta-Wright 2018, found an absolute risk 
difference with this intervention of -2.8% (-5.8 to 0.3%, p=0.074). In a cluster-randomized stepped-
wedge trial where urine LF-LAM was made available to intervention clusters (clinician directed testing 
and treatment), Åhsberg 2023 found mortality was 32.4% when urine LF-LAM was available with sputum 
LC-aNAAT, compared to 25.2% with sputum LC-aNAAT alone (p=0.088) (Table 18).
