Culture (microbiological reference standard) 
Culture is considered the best reference standard for TB using either automated liquid culture or solid 
culture methods. Since both culture methods are used interchangeably in clinical practice (Moreira 
2015), a positive result either alone or in combination was accepted as a diagnosis of TB while all 
negative cultures indicated no TB. 
Composite reference standard (CRS) 
We defined CRS as a positive culture or a clinical decision to initiate treatment for PTB (i.e., clinically 
diagnosed PTB). The CRS may be based on the results of microbiological tests including culture or 
NAATs other than the index test being evaluated, imaging studies, and clinical characteristics. At least 
one component test must be positive, according to the definition of the primary study authors, to be 
diagnosed with TB using CRS. Clinical characteristics may include cough longer than two weeks, fever, 
or weight loss; pneumonia that did not improve with antibiotics; or a history of close contact with a 
person with PTB. The CRS definitions that were used in the included studies are found in Table 3.

Drug resistance 
We considered two reference standards for detection of drug resistance: phenotypic drug 
susceptibility testing (pDST) using solid or liquid media and a composite reference standard combining 
pDST and genotypic DST (gDST, preferably whole genome sequencing). pDST is the conventional 
method for detecting resistance to first- and second-line TB drugs. The composite reference standard 
indicates drug resistance if pDST or gDST detects drug resistance and absence of drug resistance if no 
drug resistance is detected by pDST and gDST.
