Based on six studies included in this meta-analysis for a microbiological reference standard (653 
participant, tuberculosis prevalence 6.6% [43/653]), the estimated diagnostic accuracy of the parallel

higher sensitivity and -10.2 (95% CrI -19.6 to -4.9) percentage points lower specificity.  
Based on six studies included in this meta-analysis for a composite reference standard (674 participant,

aNAAT on respiratory samples plus LC-aNAAT on stool and LF-LAM a pooled sensitivity of 30.1% (95% CrI 
13.2 to 54.9) and a pooled specificity of 83.3% (95% CrI 69.6 to 90.2). Compared to LC-aNAAT on

sensitivity and -10.1 (95% CrI -21.6 to -4.9) percentage points lower specificity.  
Authors’ conclusion  
LC-aNAAT on respiratory samples and stool, and urine LF-LAM may be complementary when performed 
as part of a parallel testing strategy to diagnose tuberculosis disease in children. Parallel LC-aNAAT with 
respiratory and stool samples increased sensitivity in children with and without HIV, and so may be of 
value to increase case detection. Stool collection is non-invasive and simple stool processing methods 
have been introduced to support LC-aNAAT in community-based settings. However, in these settings, 
collection of respiratory specimens such as induced sputum or gastric aspirates may not be available for 
children. Given the greater increase in sensitivity in parallel testing compared to LC-aNAAT stool, further 
work is needed to explore the benefits and barriers of increasing local capacity or referral for collection 
and LC-aNAAT on respiratory samples. The benefits of parallel testing may be greater in high 
tuberculosis prevalence settings, while there may be a larger proportion of false positive results and
