Both MDR-TB and rifampicin-resistant TB (RR-TB) require treatment with second-line drugs 
(3). WHO uses five categories to classify cases of DR-TB: isoniazid-resistant TB, RR-TB, MDR-
TB, extensively drug-resistant TB (XDR-TB) and pre-XDR-TB. 
The definitions for DR-TB have been revised in response to advances in TB treatment in recent 
years (8). The updated pre-XDR-TB and XDR-TB definitions are: 
• Pre-XDR-TB: TB caused by M. tuberculosis strains that fulfil the definition of MDR/RR-
TB and are also resistant to any fluoroquinolone (FQ). 
• XDR-TB: TB caused by M. tuberculosis strains that fulfil the definition of MDR/RR-TB 
and that are also resistant to any FQ and at least one additional Group A drug. 
The fluoroquinolones currently recommended by WHO include levofloxacin and moxifloxacin 
and the Group A drugs are currently levofloxacin or moxifloxacin, bedaquiline and linezolid. 
 
Description of the intervention 
WHO now recommends several rapid molecular tests as the initial diagnostic test for TB, some 
of which can detect drug resistance simultaneously (9). These tests can be used at the lower 
levels of the health system. Due to the increasing number of novel tools that serve similar 
purposes, WHO introduced a class-based recommendation approach in December 2020 with 
a Guideline Development Group (GDG) recommending three new classes of tests. WHO 
groups individual tests with similar characteristics and performances into a class. The classes 
are defined by the type of technology (e.g., automated or reverse hybridization NAATs), the 
complexity of the implementation test (e.g. low, moderate or high—considering the 
requirements of infrastructure, equipment and technical skills of laboratory staff) and the 
target conditions (e.g. diagnosis of TB, and detection of resistance to first-line or second-line 
drugs) (10).
