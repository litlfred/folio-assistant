EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
Background 
Low complexity automated nucleic acid amplification tests (LC-aNAATs) are part of the molecular World 
Health Organization (WHO)-recommended rapid diagnostics widely used to diagnose tuberculosis 
disease. They are recommended as the first test for symptomatic children presenting with presumptive  
tuberculosis disease. The lateral flow urine lipoarabinomannan (LF-LAM) antigen test is recommended 
by WHO to assist in diagnosing tuberculosis disease among children with HIV. Previous systematic 
reviews have traditionally assessed diagnostic accuracy of LC-aNAATs and LF-LAM tests each in isolation 
for detection of tuberculosis in children, but in clinical practice the tests may be used in parallel (i.e., LC-
aNAAT in combination with LF-LAM). 
Objectives 
PICO 1: Parallel use of LC-aNAATs on respiratory samples and stool 
Primary: To compare the diagnostic accuracy of the parallel use of LC-aNAATs on respiratory samples 
and stool versus each sample type alone for diagnosis of pulmonary tuberculosis disease in children with 
and without HIV with signs and symptoms or screened positive for pulmonary tuberculosis, compared to 
use of the LC-aNAAT on one sample type (either respiratory or stool). 
PICO 2: Parallel use of LC-aNAATs on respiratory samples and stool plus LF-LAM 
Primary: To compare the diagnostic accuracy of the parallel use of LC-aNAATs on respiratory samples 
and stool and LF-LAM on urine versus each assay alone for diagnosis of pulmonary tuberculosis disease 
in children with HIV with signs and symptoms or screened positive for pulmonary tuberculosis, 
compared to any of the tests (either LC-aNAATs combined or LF-LAM) alone. 
Secondary: To investigate the following sources of heterogeneity: CD4 cell-count or percent category, 
advanced HIV disease, and serious illness. 
Search methods  
We searched the Cochrane Infectious Disease Group Specialized Register, MEDLINE, Embase, Science 
Citation Index, WHO Global Index Medicus, ClinicalTrial.gov, and the WHO International Clinical Trials 
Registry up to November 3rd, 2023.   
Selection criteria 
We included studies that allowed assessment of the accuracy of both parallel testing and one of the 
component tests in the same study group against a microbiological or composite reference standard.  
Data collection and analysis 
Two reviewers independently extracted data using a standardized form and assessed study quality using 
QUADAS-2 and QUADAS-C tools. We performed bivariate random-effects meta-analysis using a Bayesian 
approach to estimate sensitivity and specificity and absolute differences between index tests. We 
performed subgroup analyses by CD4 cell count and advanced HIV.  
Main results for comparative diagnostic accuracy  
PICO 1 
For children without HIV or HIV status unknown, based on eight studies included in this meta-analysis 
for a microbiological reference standard (2,145 participant, tuberculosis prevalence 8.1% [173/2,145]), 
the estimated diagnostic accuracy of the parallel use of LC-aNAAT on respiratory samples and stool had 
a pooled sensitivity of 79.9% (95% Credible Interval [95% CrI] 67.9 to 89.8) and a pooled specificity of 
93.4% (95% CrI 87.2 to 97.0). Compared to LC-aNAAT on respiratory samples alone, parallel testing had 
7.1 (95% CrI 3.2 to 13.4) percentage points higher sensitivity and -1.7 (95% CrI -3.8 to -0.6) percentage 
points lower specificity. Compared to LC-aNAAT on stool alone, parallel testing had 22.1 (95% CrI 13.7 to 
32.7) percentage points higher sensitivity and -4.1 (95% CrI -8.0 to -1.7) percentage points lower 
specificity.
