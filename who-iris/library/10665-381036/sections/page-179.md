Characteristics of included studies 
The characteristics of the two included RCTs are summarised in Table 2. Of note, no study 
reported all-cause mortality and cure as outcomes. 
 
Cattamanchi 2021 (32) conducted a cRCT amongst adults aged ≥18 years in Ugandan 
community health centres for TB diagnosis. They compared a multicomponent diagnostic 
strategy (on-site Xpert Ultra testing, guided restructuring of clinic workflows, and monthly 
feedback of quality metrics) to routine care (on-site sputum smear microscopy with referral-
based molecular testing). The trial included 10,644 patients across 20 clusters; 5,546 were 
randomised to receive Xpert Ultra and 5,098 received routine care. Outcomes included were 
time to treatment initiation and time to diagnosis of confirmed TB.  
 
Esmail 2023 (33) compared Xpert Ultra to same-day smear microscopy for the diagnosis of TB 
in a RCT in community-based mobile clinics in South Africa. All patients also received TB 
culture and 584 patients were included: 288 were randomised to receive Xpert Ultra and 296 
received same-day smear microscopy. All participants were aged >18 years and had WHO-
defined symptoms of TB and/or HIV infection. Outcomes included were time taken to initiate 
treatment and diagnosis (Table 3).
