Overall, the search strategy contained three overarching concepts: concept 1 (tuberculosis), concept 2 
(nucleic acid amplification test specified for each NAAT), and concept 3 (costs and cost analysis). 
 
 For LC-aNAAT, we used the following electronic databases to search: PubMed, EMBASE, and SCOPUS for 
published studies, with no restrictions on language. Since we expect fewer studies reporting on LC-mNAAT 
and MC-aNAAT, we searched Web of Science and CINAHL in addition to the three databases mentioned 
above.  
 
For LC-aNAATs, only studies published after September 2019 until July 2023, the end date for the previous 
systematic review, were included. We additionally screened the reference list of review articles and key 
identified articles for additional studies. 
 
For MC-aNAAT and LC-mNAAT, we searched for all studies published till August 2023. We additionally 
screened the reference list of review articles and key identified articles for additional studies. We repeated 
the search in January 2024 to confirm that no newly published studies were missed.  
                       
Data Abstraction:  
Data from the included studies was extracted to assess study quality and synthesize evidence using a 
standardized, pre-piloted form. Critical study data, including the primary research question, year of study, 
country, patient population, setting, year of evaluation, currency, study population, diagnostic 
intervention, diagnostic comparator, analysis perspective, type of economic analysis, primary outcome 
and secondary outcomes, source of costing, discount rate, type of model used, sensitivity and scenario 
analyses performed and willingness to pay threshold were extracted from each study by two independent 
reviewers. Any discrepancies were identified and resolved through discussion and, where necessary, by a 
third reviewer. Key economic outcomes for each study that were extracted include any test or 
implementation cost component, cost per patient tested for each diagnostic strategy, and ICER per
