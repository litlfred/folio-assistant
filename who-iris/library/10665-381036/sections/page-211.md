Search methods 
Electronic searches 
The Cochrane Infectious Diseases Group Information Specialist (Vittoria Lutje (VL)) performed 
literature searches between 2nd and 3rd October 2023, without language restrictions, in the following 
databases, using the search terms in Appendix 1: Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials 
(CENTRAL), included in the Cochrane Library, issue 10, October 2023; MEDLINE (Ovid); Embase (Ovid); 
Science Citation Index and Biosis previews (ISI Web of Knowledge); WHO Global Index Medicus; and 
SCOPUS (Elsevier). VL searched ClinicalTrials.gov (clinicaltrials.gov/) and the WHO Clinical Trials 
Registry Platform (www.who.int/trialsearch) to identify ongoing trials.  
Searching other resources 
The review authors examined the reference lists of included articles and relevant review articles 
identified through electronic searches. VL also searched for relevant dissertations in ProQuest 
Dissertations & Theses A&I. The review authors searched for information on ongoing and unpublished 
studies from experts working on new diagnostics for TB such as STOP TB Partnership’s New Diagnostic 
Working Group and FIND (the global alliance for diagnostics). A WHO public call was made between 
December 2023 and 15th February 2024 for ongoing and unpublished studies from manufacturers and 
researchers. 
Selection criteria 
Types of studies  
We included cross-sectional and cohort studies of the diagnostic accuracy of TB-LAMP against culture, 
composite or both reference standards. We included studies that reported the number of true 
positives (TP), true negatives (TN), false positives (FP), and false negatives (FN) or provided statistics 
that enabled their derivation. We excluded studies with a multiple-group (i.e. diagnostic case control) 
design because these studies may lead to biased estimates of diagnostic accuracy. We used abstracts 
to identify published or unpublished studies. 
Participants  
We included studies that evaluated the index tests for pulmonary or extrapulmonary TB in people 
presumed to have TB. We considered studies from all types of health facilities and all laboratory levels 
(peripheral, intermediate, and central) from all countries. We included studies that recruited people 
living with HIV, had diabetes mellitus, or a previous history of TB. We included studies that enrolled 
only adolescents and adults (>10 years old). We only included participants who had the index test 
performed on respiratory samples (i.e., expectorated sputum, induced sputum, bronchial alveolar 
lavage and tracheal aspirates) for the evaluation of pulmonary TB. For extrapulmonary TB, we included 
non-respiratory specimens such as cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), pleural fluid, and lymph node aspirate or 
tissue. We considered blood for disseminated TB. We only included studies that provided at least five 
specimens for a given form of extrapulmonary TB. We excluded studies designed to find people with 
active TB in community settings as well as drug resistance surveys. If a study included children and 
adolescents or adults and if disaggregated data were not available in the published paper, we 
contacted the study authors for the data. If the authors declined, did not respond or data were not 
available, we excluded the study. 
Index test 
The index test is TB-LAMP. We only considered design-locked marketed test technologies.  
Reference standard  
We included two reference standards: culture and composite reference standards.  
Culture (microbiological reference standard)  
Mycobacterial culture using either automated liquid culture or solid culture methods is considered 
the best reference standard for TB. Since both culture methods are used interchangeably in clinical 
practice (Moreira 2015), a positive result either alone or in combination was accepted as a diagnosis
