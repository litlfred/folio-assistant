Among CHIV, the incremental increase in sensitivity of parallel stool and respiratory LC-aNAAT was 
lower than among children without HIV. This likely reflects the lower sensitivity in general of molecular 
TB testing in people living with HIV given their paucibacillary disease. The urine LF-LAM test is a non-
invasive, point-of-care test to detect tuberculosis and is currently recommended as an add-on test. We 
demonstrated increased sensitivity when LF-LAM on urine was used in parallel with combined LC-aNAAT 
on respiratory samples and stool. CHIV further face challenges in obtaining microbiological confirmation, 
and when children with culture-negative TB disease were included in the CRS, the incremental increase 
in sensitivity was larger when LF-LAM was included in the parallel test strategy. However, there was a 
larger reduction in specificity.  CHIV are at higher risk of mortality from tuberculosis, especially those 
with advanced HIV disease and low CD4 count. Therefore, while greater detection of cases is crucial, it 
must be balanced with the risk of overtreatment. Similar to parallel LC-aNAAT, the net benefit of a 
parallel test strategy including LF-LAM, may be greater in higher tuberculosis prevalence settings. The 
increase in sensitivity was greater when LC-aNAAT was added to LF-LAM. Although as a complementary 
test, LF-LAM may be the only test available in settings without access to LC-aNAAT, and consideration is 
needed to assess the feasibility and benefits of building local capacity for LC-aNAAT or referring patients 
for this testing.
