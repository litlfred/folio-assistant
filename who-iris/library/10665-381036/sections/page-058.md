In the index test domain, we judged the risk of bias as low for all (100%) because Xpert Ultra test 
results are automatically generated, and the user is provided with printable test results (thus there is no 
room for subjective interpretation of test results) and the threshold for positivity is pre-specified. 
Regarding applicability, we judged low concern for most studies (8/10, 80%) with unclear concern for 
two studies (20%) because the stool processing method was different from the simple one-step (SOS) 
or sucrose filtration (SF) method, or not described. 
  
In the reference standard domain, for MRS, we judged the risk of bias as low for most studies (6/10, 
70%) because these studies used multiple cultures to verify tuberculosis. We judged the risk of bias as 
unclear for three studies (2/10, 20%) that only used Xpert Ultra on respiratory specimens as a 
reference standard, and culture was not performed, and two studies where only a single culture was 
performed. Regarding applicability, we judged low concern for six studies (6/10, 60%) and high 
concern for two studies (2/10, 20%) because in these studies culture was not used as part of the 
reference standard, we identified two studies (2/10, 20%) as unclear because the culture method was 
not specified or only solid culture was performed.  For CRS, we judged the risk of bias as low for 2/5 
studies (40%); and unclear for 2/5 studies (40%) because it was unclear if Xpert Ultra on stool was 
used in the CRS; and high for 1/5 studies (20%), because Xpert Ultra on stool was included in the 
CRS.  
  
In the flow and timing domain, we judged a low risk of bias for most studies (9/10, 90%) and an 
unclear risk of bias for one study (10%) because index and reference tests were not collected within 
the pre-specified time period.
