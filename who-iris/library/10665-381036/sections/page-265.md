This systematic review will estimate the change in diagnostic accuracy of parallel use of LC-aNAATs 
(from respiratory specimens and stool) and LF-LAM compared to using each of the tests alone to 
diagnose pulmonary tuberculosis among children with and without HIV. The findings will inform an 
upcoming WHO guideline development.  
2. REVIEW QUESTION  
We addressed the following PICO (population, intervention, comparison, outcome) questions also listed 
in (see Appendix 1: PICO question). 
1. What is the incremental diagnostic accuracy of parallel use of LC-aNAATs on a respiratory sample 
and stool for diagnosis of pulmonary tuberculosis disease in children with and without HIV with 
signs and symptoms or screened positive for pulmonary tuberculosis, compared to use of the LC-
aNAAT on one sample type (either respiratory or stool)?   
 
2. What is the incremental diagnostic accuracy of parallel use of LC-aNAATs on a respiratory sample 
plus stool and LF-LAM on urine for diagnosis of pulmonary tuberculosis disease in children with HIV 
with signs and symptoms or screened positive for pulmonary tuberculosis, compared to any of the 
tests (either LC-aNAATs combined or LF-LAM) alone?  
In addition, we estimated incremental accuracy in the following subgroups: 
a. In those seriously ill 
b. In those with advanced HIV disease  
c. In those with CD4 < 200 (in children ≥ 5 years old)  
d. In those with CD4% < 25% (in children < 5 years old) 
Presumptive tuberculosis’, i.e., children with signs and symptoms or screened positive for pulmonary 
tuberculosis, refers to a child with signs or symptoms suggestive of pulmonary tuberculosis or who has a 
positive or abnormal screening test for pulmonary tuberculosis. In children, WHO recommends using the 
following tools for screening: 
- 
Children younger than 15 years who are close contacts of someone with TB: symptom screen 
including any one of cough, fever or poor weight gain, or chest X-ray; or both 
- 
CHIV younger than 10 years: symptom screen including any one of current cough, fever, poor 
weight gain or close contact with a TB patient [World Health Organization 2022b] 
We defined the following main comparisons of interest:  
• 
For PICO 1, we compared the diagnostic accuracy of parallel use of LC-aNAAT on respiratory 
samples versus LC-aNAAT stool for detecting pulmonary tuberculosis disease in children with and 
without HIV that present with presumptive tuberculosis. 
• 
For PICO 2, we compared the diagnostic accuracy of parallel use of LC-aNAAT on respiratory 
samples and LC-aNAAT stool versus LF-LAM on urine for detecting pulmonary tuberculosis disease 
in children with HIV that present with presumptive tuberculosis. 
We also aimed to assess the impact of implementing a parallel testing strategy of LC-aNAAT on 
respiratory samples and stool (versus a strategy of LC-aNAAT on each alone), and parallel testing 
strategy of LF-LAM on urine on patient-important outcomes. Of patient-important outcomes, we
