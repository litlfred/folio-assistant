Background 
The World Health Organization (WHO) estimated 1.3 million children (aged 0–14 years) were affected 
by tuberculosis (TB) in 2022. Globally, paediatric TB contributed to 16% of deaths among HIV-negative 
children in the same year. Multiple factors contribute to diagnostic delays and the consequent rise in 
incidence and mortality among children. The unique pathophysiology and clinical manifestations of 
TB in children make diagnosis particularly difficult. While there are practical difficulties in obtaining 
specimens for diagnosis, about 15% are positive on acid-fast bacilli (AFB) smear, and 30% to 40% are 
culture-positive in children with presumptive TB. (Lighter 2009; Starke 2003; Zar 2005). The clinical 
criteria still have a risk of missing a significant number of paediatric TB cases.  
Timely diagnosis of paediatric TB is crucial not only to prevent complications and mortality but also to 
measure the ongoing transmission of Mycobacterium tuberculosis as childhood TB is a good indicator 
of recent and ongoing transmission in the community. The WHO has recommended the replacement 
of smear microscopy with mWRD (WHO recommended molecular tests) wherever possible. One such 
class of molecular tests, low complexity manual nucleic acid amplification test (LC-mNAAT), requires 
the same biosafety measures and infrastructure as smear microscopy. Because of the merits, LC-
mNAAT can be used as a replacement for smear microscopy even in resource-limited settings. This 
systematic review aims to assess the diagnostic accuracy of LC-mNAATs for detecting pulmonary and 
extrapulmonary TB in children. 
Target condition being diagnosed 
M. tuberculosis is the bacteria that causes TB, an infectious disease that spreads through the air via 
respiratory droplets from an infected individual. M. tuberculosis can cause pulmonary and 
extrapulmonary TB. Pulmonary TB refers to TB disease that exclusively affects the lungs. When TB 
affects organs of the body except the lungs, it is referred to as extrapulmonary TB. Pulmonary TB is 
the most prevalent form of TB. Loss of appetite, loss of weight, lethargy, fever, chills, night sweats, 
cough, and haemoptysis are common symptoms of pulmonary TB. 
The clinical spectrum of TB in children may range from asymptomatic primary infection, symptomatic 
TB disease, and extrapulmonary manifestations to disseminated TB or adult-type disease (Marais 
2011). Adult cavitary-type diseases (abnormal hollow spaces within the pulmonary parenchyma) are 
often found in older children. The symptoms include a low-grade fever, non-productive cough, loss of 
weight, and non-specific symptoms such as wheezing due to compression of the bronchi by lymph 
nodes or reduced size of the bronchial lumen by granulomas and croup in case of laryngeal 
involvement (Piccini 2014). 
Index test  
The index test is LC-mNAATs and the most common LC-mNAAT is TB-LAMP (loop-mediated isothermal 
amplification). TB-LAMP was developed and marketed by Eiken Chemicals (Tokyo, Japan). TB-LAMP is 
based on an amplification process that uses four primers that match six locations of the target gene 
and a strand displacement reaction at 65°C for 15 to 60 minutes. An improved amplification process 
of 109 to 1010 times can be achieved by using loop primers in TB-LAMP assay (WHO 2016). Since this 
assay is insensitive to the buildup of DNA and pyrophosphate salt by-products, it can continue until a 
substantial amount of amplicons has been produced. This also makes the amplification process visible 
to the naked eye with the use of double-stranded DNA dyes, such as SYBR Green. The assay can be 
completed within one hour and consists of three steps: sample preparation (10 to 20 minutes), 
amplification (40 minutes), and visual detection of fluorescence using ultraviolet light (0.5 to 1
