Searching other resources 
The authors examined the reference lists of included articles and relevant review articles identified 
through the electronic searches. We did not search conference websites but limited the inclusion to 
published conference proceedings identified through the aforementioned databases. We asked experts 
within our personal networks for potentially eligible unpublished reports of implementing partners or 
technical agencies. 
 
Data collection and Sampling of studies 
We added articles identified in the screening search to a Covidence database.17 Three review authors (EO, 
MM and PK) independently screened the titles and abstracts of the search results in duplicate to identify 
eligible articles. They then independently assessed the full texts of journal articles or conference 
proceedings in duplicate for eligibility based on our a priori inclusion criteria. We resolved any 
disagreements by consensus. 
 
We assessed the number or data richness or thickness of potentially eligible studies, to check if they were 
likely to present challenges with analysis and excluded two studies that were very thin due to vague point 
of care tests being discussed or very weak reporting of study demographics and methods18 19. We defined 
richness or a thick study as one in which the author analyzes their findings besides a descriptive list of user 
perspectives, shows insights from participants perspectives, explains variations and meanings, or 
develops and contributes to theory.20 
In addition, we sampled studies from the eligible full texts to include studies about Xpert MTB/RIF or rapid 
NAATs. These would provide supporting or contextual evidence when faced with insufficient studies about 
the newer NAATs.
