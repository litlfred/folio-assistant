3. METHODS 
The methodology of this review is similar to those used in previous reviews and protocols in the field 
[Shah 2016a; Bjerrum 2019c; Shapiro 2021; Kohli 2021a; Zifodya 2021; Inbaraj 2023a; Inbaraj 
2023b]. 
3.1 Criteria for considering studies for this review 
3.1.1 Types of studies 
We included comparative diagnostic accuracy studies that evaluated the accuracy of a parallel 
combination of respiratory LC-aNAATs and urine LF-LAM (henceforth referred to as ‘parallel testing’) 
and the diagnostic accuracy of one of the tests used alone, in the same study group (Table 8), 
against a microbiological reference standard (that did not include the index tests) or a composite 
reference standard. Fully paired studies (i.e. both index tests evaluated on the same patients) and 
studies designed with randomization of participants to two or more index test groups were eligible 
for inclusion.  
We included studies not originally designed as comparative diagnostic accuracy studies (e.g. 
randomized controlled trials), but from which we could extract data to determine the diagnostic 
accuracy of the index tests (i.e. two-by-two table data). Additionally, we included studies that were 
originally designed to assess the accuracy of LF-LAM, with LC-aNAAT and mycobacterial culture as 
reference standards. If data from such studies was made available for estimation of comparative 
diagnostic accuracy, such studies could be reanalysed for the purpose of this review (Table 8).

We also included studies with an attempt to obtain fully paired data for the index tests, but where 
data on some participants were unavailable (e.g., either a urine sample for LF-LAM or a respiratory 
sample for LC-aNAAT was not available or there was an available sample but no final result). We 
included these studies because respiratory samples may be difficult to obtain in people with HIV 
especially in those with severe disease [Broger 2023b; Lawn 2017a], and excluding those unable to 
produce samples (or in whom one test did not lead to a valid result) may underestimate net 
sensitivity of parallel testing [Bjerrum 2019c]. We included studies where there was an attempt to 
collect urine for LF-LAM and respiratory samples for LC-aNAAT for all participants. Respiratory 
samples included sputum, induced sputum, tracheal aspiration, and fluid from bronchial alveolar 
and gastric lavage. 
We included published studies and preprints. We included unpublished studies if the data provided 
by study authors on request were the final data; otherwise, the studies were reported as ongoing 
studies. We included data provided by manufacturers and investigators in response to the public call 
for data by the WHO Global TB Programme. We excluded studies enrolling tuberculosis cases and 
non-cases from separate populations (also called two-gate or case-control studies). We also 
excluded reviews, commentaries, and editorials. We did not have language restrictions.
