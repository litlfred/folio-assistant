Should LC-mNAATs on respiratory samples be used to diagnose pulmonary TB in 
HIV negative adults and adolescents with signs and symptoms of pulmonary TB, 
against a microbiological reference standard? 
Figure 8 summarises the results of the risk of bias and applicability assessment for each of the three 
included studies. The risk of bias and applicability concern was low across all the studies.  
Figure 8. Risk of bias and applicability for each study: pulmonary TB detection in HIV 
negative adults and adolescents 
 
The sensitivities in the three studies (1541 participants) included in the meta-analysis ranged 
between 53% and 89% and the specificities between 98% and 100% (Figure 9). The summary 
sensitivity (95% CI) was 76.7% (57.6 to 88.8) and the summary specificity was 98.9% (98.2 to 99.3). 
 
For a population of 1000 HIV negative adults and adolescents where 100 have pulmonary TB on 
culture, 87 would be LC-mNAAT-positive and 10 (11%) would not have TB (false-positives); 913 would 
be LC-mNAAT-negative and 23 (3%) would have TB (false-negatives).
