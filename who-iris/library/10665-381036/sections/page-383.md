Low-complexity automated nucleic acid amplification tests (LC-aNAATs) are a class of TB diagnostics 
included in the mWRDs that can be used for TB diagnosis. They include Xpert® MTB/RIF (Xpert), Xpert® 
MTB/RIF Ultra (Ultra) (Cepheid, USA), and TrueNat™ (11). These tests provide initial detection of TB and 
resistance to rifampicin detection. 
 
Moderate complexity automated NAATs (MC-aNAATs) offer a high sample throughput for the initial 
detection of TB and resistance to rifampicin and isoniazid, providing more options for early diagnosis of 
TB and rifampicin-resistant TB but also addressing an essential gap in the rapid diagnosis of isoniazid-
resistant and rifampicin-susceptible TB (11,12).  
 
Low-complexity manual NAATs (LC-mNAATs) include the TB-LAMP, a point-of-care diagnostic test based 
on the loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) technique. It is a manual diagnostic assay that 
offers a rapid and user-friendly testing solution. This technology is temperature-independent, requiring 
limited skills and resources to operate, and is practical in low-resource settings where maintaining 
temperature and equipment is challenging (13). The WHO recommendations indicate TB-LAMP may be 
used as the initial diagnosis test for detecting pulmonary tuberculosis in symptomatic adults and as an 
add-on test following negative smear microscopy, mainly when additional testing of smear-negative 
sputum specimens is necessary (13). TB-LAMP does not provide any resistance testing.     
  
As TB programs consider different diagnostic approaches and algorithms, economic evidence is critical to 
guide implementation decisions and ensure that programs use limited resources wisely. Previous 
systematic reviews have looked at the costs and cost-effectiveness of LC-NAATs such as Xpert, Xpert Ultra, 
and TrueNat, demonstrating that model-based cost-effectiveness analyses predominantly support the use 
of Xpert in detecting TB in areas with varying TB burdens (14). These reviews also highlighted that Xpert 
is generally cost-effective across several countries, varying socio-economic factors, HIV burden, and 
geography. Important heterogeneity exists across studies regarding analytical perspectives, cost 
components, the standard of care or status quo comparator used, and effectiveness/outcome measures. 
While economic evidence exists to support the use of Xpert, the economic evidence around more recent 
developments like TrueNat, Xpert Ultra, MC-aNAATs, and LC-mNAATs for TB diagnosis is more limited and 
has not been recently reviewed and summarized.
