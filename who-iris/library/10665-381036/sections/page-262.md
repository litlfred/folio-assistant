Several clinical scores have been proposed to aid in establishing a diagnosis, especially at lower levels of 
health care where most children present [World Health Organization 2018]. In 2022, the WHO 
recommended the use of treatment decision algorithms for the interim, with evidence of its 
performance to be reviewed in 2024 [World Health Organization 2022b]. To be broadly applicable across 
a range of health care levels, one treatment decision algorithms including CXR scoring and one without 
were proposed. The algorithms are designed to achieve a high sensitivity at cost of specificity, thus 
accepting potential overdiagnosis and thereby overtreatment. Novel diagnostic assays need to be 
identified that can support a more definitive diagnosis of tuberculosis disease.  
Current tuberculosis diagnostic tests, often performed on respiratory specimens, have low sensitivity for 
childhood tuberculosis [World Health Organization 2022b]. Collection of multiple specimens for testing 
are recommended to improve diagnostic yield [Lewinsohn 2016]. In children, studies have also found 
that collection of different respiratory sample types (i.e. induced sputum and nasopharyngeal aspirate) 
may be beneficial [Song 2021; Zar 2019]. Furthermore, given the low sensitivity of sputum-based testing 
for children, there is an urgent need for a non-sputum point-of-care test, that could be performed in 
parallel or in replacement of current testing.  
In this systematic review, we evaluated the incremental diagnostic accuracy of the parallel use of 1) Low 
complexity automated NAAT (LC-aNAAT) on respiratory and stool samples, and 2) LC-aNAAT on 
respiratory and stool samples, and LF-LAM on urine in CHIV, both for the diagnosis of pulmonary 
tuberculosis. We define positivity of a parallel test strategy as either test positive.  The review does not 
include data on drug resistance.
