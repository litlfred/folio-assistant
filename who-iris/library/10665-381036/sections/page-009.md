The STANDARD M10 is an in vitro molecular diagnostics system that automatically extracts and 
amplifies nucleic acids (DNA, RNA) from the collected samples, and analyses the data from 
amplification in real-time, by combining the processes of isothermal amplification and real-time PCR 
in a single platform using all-in-one cartridge systems. There are two variations currently available for 
TB detection: STANDARD™ M10 MTB/NTM and STANDARD™ M10 MDR-TB. These assays are intended 
for use with the STANDARD M10 system and provide qualitative results within 80 minutes from human 
sputum or sputum sediment specimens. 
1. STANDARD™ M10 MTB/NTM assay aids in the simultaneous detection of Mycobacterium 
tuberculosis complex (MTBC) and non-tuberculous mycobacteria (NTM) DNA (SD Biosensor 
2021a). 
2. STANDARD™ M10 MDR-TB assay aids in the simultaneous detection of M. tuberculosis and 
drug-resistance against rifampicin (RIF) and isoniazid (INH) (SD Biosensor 2021b) 
 
IRON qPCR, Bioneer, Republic of Korea 
IRON-qPCR ™ is a molecular diagnostics system which combines nucleic acid extraction and real-time 
PCR. It utilises syndromic diagnostics, i.e., the process of using a single cartridge test to detect >10 
gene targets. IRON-qPCR ™ has a high multiplexing capacity of up to 40 targets in a single test and 
provides results in 40 minutes. The IRON qPCR RFIA kit is a cartridge-based assay that detects M. 
tuberculosis and resistance to 11 drugs: rifampicin, isoniazid, fluoroquinolones (levofloxacin, 
moxifloxacin, gatifloxacin and ofloxacin), aminoglycosides (kanamycin, amikacin, capreomycin), 
prothionamide and ethionamide. It uses IRON qPCR, which is a single instrument for extraction, 
amplification, and detection. Two modules are available per instrument (Bioneer 2023). IRON qPCR 
RFIA is not commercially available. 
 
Objectives 
Primary objective 
To assess the diagnostic accuracy of LC-aNAATs for the detection of PTB and rifampicin resistance in 
adults (15 years of age and older) and adolescents (10 to 14 years) with presumptive PTB and among 
people living with HIV (PLHIV). 
Secondary objectives 
1. To investigate potential sources of heterogeneity. For detection of PTB, we investigated the effect 
of smear status (smear positive/smear negative), and history of TB on test accuracy. For detection 
of drug resistance, we planned to investigate the effect of smear status on test accuracy. 
 
2. To summarise the frequency of non-determinate index test results and Xpert Ultra trace positive 
results. We evaluated the effect on summary sensitivity and specificity of interpreting Xpert Ultra 
trace positive results as negative (i.e., results are TN or FN compared to MRS). 
 
Methods 
Methods follow the generic protocol for six diagnostic accuracy reviews that will provide evidence to 
inform the 2024 update to WHO policy guidelines on tests for TB detection. The protocol is available 
on the Open Source Repository.
