4.7 PICO 2 on impact 
PICO 2: What is the effect of parallel use of respiratory LC-aNAATs and urine LF-
LAM on patient important outcomes in adults and adolescents with HIV who 
present with presumptive tuberculosis, compared to respiratory LC-aNAAT alone? 
We identified three trials (n=4602 participants) with data on the impact of parallel use of respiratory LC-
aNAATs and urine LF-LAM for clinical decision-making on mortality, time to tuberculosis diagnosis and 
time to tuberculosis treatment [Åhsberg 2023; Gupta-Wright 2018; J.G. Peter 2016](Table 18). Two were 
multi-national randomized trials, one [Peter 2016] conducted in South Africa, Tanzania, Zambia, and 
Zimbabwe (n=1609), and the other [Gupta-Wright 2018] conducted in Malawi and South Africa 
(n=2574). The third was a stepped wedge cluster randomized trial [Åhsberg 2023] conducted in Ghana 
(three clusters) (n=419). Each trial was primarily designed to evaluate the impact of including LF-LAM as 
a tuberculosis diagnostic test to guide treatment initiation in adult inpatients with HIV in addition to 
standard of care, comparing all-cause mortality at 8 weeks between the LF-LAM intervention group and 
standard-of-care control group. Each trial included sputum Xpert MTB/RIF as part of standard of care to 
guide management of patients in both the intervention and control groups at all sites for all three trials, 
with exception of the Zimbabwe study site in Peter 2016, that did not have Xpert MTB/RIF available and 
was excluded from analyses. Gupta-Wright 2018 also included urine Xpert MTB/RIF along with urine LF-
LAM in the intervention group. Consequently, these trials were considered to meet review inclusion 
criteria as clinical decisions for intervention group participants in all three trials were informed by the 
use of sputum Xpert MTB/RIF and urine LF-LAM (in addition to urine Xpert MTB/RIF in Gupta-Wright 
2018), in comparison to the control group participants who received LC-aNAAT but did not receive LF-
LAM. We did not identify any trials with data on the impact of parallel use of respiratory LC-aNAATs and 
urine LF-LAM for clinical decision-making in outpatient settings. 
Main results are presented below with summary of findings tables in Appendix 20. 
Figure 14 shows the risk-of-bias assessment of the three included impact trials. Overall, trials were 
judged to be at low risk of bias (Figure 14), aside from unclear risk of bias related to blinding in Peter 
2016 since participants and research staff were not masked to either allocation, and in Åhsberg 2023 
since this was an open-label trial without blinding. It is unclear how the lack of blinding may have 
affected clinical decision-making for participants in these trials, which may have impacted the diagnostic 
and treatment outcomes but would not have affected the mortality outcome. Other potential sources of 
bias within all three included trials relate to the informed consent requirement, which may have 
resulted in the exclusion of people who were critically ill, who would have been part of the target 
population for the intervention in real-world practice settings. Similarly, limiting enrolment to standard 
working hours and for less than 48 hours after admission may have also introduced other bias.
