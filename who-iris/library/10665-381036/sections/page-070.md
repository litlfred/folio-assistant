10. Should LC-aNAATs on stool be used to diagnose pulmonary 
tuberculosis in children with signs and symptoms or screened positive for

Figure 20. Risk of bias and applicability summary for pulmonary tuberculosis: stool and composite 
reference standard  
Six studies (1778 participants) evaluating stool against a CRS were included with sensitivities of 9% 
to 58% and specificities of 97% and 100% (Figure 21). All six studies were included in the meta-
analysis. The summary sensitivity (95% CI) was 31.4% (17.2 to 50.2) and the summary specificity 
(95% CI) was 99.0% (97.4 to 99.6) (moderate-certainty (sensitivity) and high-certainty (specificity) 
evidence). 
 
For a population of 1,000 children where 100 have tuberculosis on CRS, 40 would be Xpert Ultra 
positive; of these, 31 would have pulmonary tuberculosis (true positives) and 9 would not have 
pulmonary tuberculosis (false positives); 960 would be Xpert Ultra negative; of these 891 would not 
have pulmonary tuberculosis (true negatives) and 69 would have pulmonary tuberculosis (false-
negatives).
