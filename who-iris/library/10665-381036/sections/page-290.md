4.4.2 Comparative diagnostic accuracy against a composite reference standard 
Summary of Findings Tables for the incremental diagnostic accuracy of the parallel use of LC-aNAATs 
combined versus each LC-aNAAT alone using the CRS can be found in Appendix 12. 
Description of included studies 
We identified 12 studies that compared the accuracy of a parallel strategy of LC-aNAATs combined 
versus each LC-aNAAT alone in children without HIV or HIV status unknown against a CRS, involving 
3,579 participants, 1,464 (40.9%) of which had tuberculosis disease. In CHIV, seven studies assessed the 
same, involving 718 participants, 287 (40.0%) of which had tuberculosis disease. 
The tuberculosis prevalence as confirmed by a CRS ranged from 8.9% ([Babo 2023]) to 89.2% ([M. P. 
Nicol 2013]). The definition of the CRS differed between studies and was based on different 
combinations of microbiological tests, radiographic findings, clinical assessment, and/or decision to 
initiate tuberculosis treatment. LC-aNAAT results were part of the reference standard in eleven studies 
(91.7%; [Ainan 2021; Babo 2023; Chabala 2024; Donkeng 2022; Hanrahan 2018]  [Jaganath 2024; Kabir 
2021; M. P. Nicol 2013; Olbrich 2024; Orikiriza 2021; Wobudeya 2024]). 
Three studies (25.0%) were conducted exclusively in an inpatient [Kabir 2021; Chabala 2024], [Orikiriza 
2021], three studies (25.0%) exclusively in an outpatient [Babo 2023; Hasan 2017], [Hanrahan 2018], and 
six of studies (50.0%) in both settings [Ainan 2021; Donkeng 2022; M. P. Nicol 2013; Wobudeya 2024; 
Olbrich 2024; Jaganath 2024]. See Appendix 8. Characteristics of included studies. 
Diagnostic accuracy of parallel use of LC-aNAATs combined based on the 
composite reference standard  
For children without HIV or HIV status unknown, the sensitivity of the parallel use of LC-aNAATs ranged 
from 4.6% [Hanrahan 2018] to 87.5% [Donkeng 2022], and the specificity from 77.5% [Jaganath 2024] to 
100% [Ainan 2021; Chabala 2024; Donkeng 2022; Hanrahan 2018; Kabir 2021; Orikiriza 2021; Wobudeya 
2024] between studies, see Figure 12a. The pooled sensitivity was 29.0% (95% CrI 17.3 to 44.7) and the 
specificity 99.1% (95% CrI 96.1 to 99.8), (12 studies; 3,579 participants, 1,464 [40.9%] with tuberculosis). 
For CHIV, the sensitivity of the parallel use of LC-aNAATs ranged from 0.0% [Hanrahan 2018] to 100.0% 
[Donkeng 2022], and the specificity from 79.0% [Jaganath 2024] to 100.0%  Hanrahan 2018; Orikiriza 
2021] between studies, see Figure 12a. The pooled sensitivity was 16.1% (95% CrI 7.6 to 31.2) and the 
specificity 95.5% (95% CrI 89.9 to 98.2), (seven studies; 718 participants, 287 [40.0%] with tuberculosis).
