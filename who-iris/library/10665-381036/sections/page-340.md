3.3.7 Sensitivity analysis 
We performed the following sensitivity analyses by limiting inclusion in the meta-analysis based on 
the following criteria: 
• Only screening positive for tuberculosis (e.g. chest X-ray abnormal, CRP elevated or 
mWRD screening test positive) 
• Only seriously ill  
• Only advanced HIV  
• Only studies using Xpert Ultra as LC-aNAAT 
• Exclusion of studies at high risk of bias (QUADAS-C) and high concerns regarding 
applicability (QUADAS-2) in at least one domain 
• Exclusion of participants with at least one unavailable or non-determinate index test 
result  
• Alterative prior distributions which are less informative: increasing the variance of 
the normal distributions over the summary log odds of the sensitivity or specificity to 
100 and using a uniform distribution ranging from zero to three over the between-
study standard deviation on the log odds scale 
3.3.8 Assessment of reporting bias 
We did not carry out formal assessment of publication bias using methods such as funnel plots or 
regression tests because such techniques have not been helpful for diagnostic test accuracy studies 
[Takwoingi Y 2022]. However, with our extensive outreach to researchers to obtain data from 
unpublished studies as well as WHO public call for data, we believe the risk of publication bias to be 
low. 
3.3.9 Assessment of the certainty of the evidence  
We assessed the certainty of the evidence using the GRADE approach for comparative test accuracy 
questions [Yang 2021b], which involves rating risk of bias, indirectness, inconsistency, imprecision, 
and risk of publication bias across the body of evidence. We assessed the certainty that the true 
difference in sensitivity and true difference in specificity lie within the bounds of the 95% CrI (a 
minimally contextualized rating). We used the QUADAS-2 and QUADAS-C judgments for rating risk of 
bias and indirectness. We examined variability in study-specific estimates (of difference in sensitivity 
or specificity) and overlap in confidence intervals, and rated down for inconsistency when point 
estimates of individual studies were outside our target of certainty rating. We did not rate down for 
imprecision, as our target of certainty rating were defined by the 95% CrIs of summary estimates.
