We identified 19 review findings from all included NAATs, nine of which were also about Xpert Ultra.  We 
grouped the findings into three overarching categories (Critical aspects users’ value, challenges related to 
those values and concerns for equity and access).  Findings from the additional studies confirmed the 
findings of the previous review. Therefore, our confidence in most of the review findings remained high 
for all NAATs.  
Critical aspects users’ value 
Findings from Xpert MTB/RIF studies showed that providers valued Xpert’s utility in PLHIV, making a 
diagnosis of drug resistance, accuracy and resulting confidence in the test, rapid turnaround times, low 
costs of diagnostic testing to patients and better patient-provider relationship. Providers also valued the 
diversity of sample types the test utilized. Laboratory personnel valued its ease of use, and increased staff 
satisfaction compared to sputum microscopy. People with TB valued an accurate diagnosis, avoiding 
diagnostic delays and low costs associated with diagnostic testing. 
Compared to Xpert MTB/RIF, providers valued Xpert Ultra‘s capacity in improving TB case detection 
among these hard-to-diagnose patients (EPTB, pediatric TB and TB patients who are co-infected with 
COVID-19) and detecting more cases. By building on existing Xpert platforms, its ease of implementation 
and integration with testing for other diseases was valued. 
Challenges reported to realizing those values 
Findings on Xpert MTB/RIF demonstrated health system challenges including underutilization and delays 
in the diagnostic pathway due to poor sample quality, insufficient resources and maintenance, lack of 
functional data connectivity systems or record systems, inefficient patient flows, unavailability of updated 
clinical guidelines and poor ownership and accountability of the tests by health facilities. Overreliance on 
test results rather than clinical judgement and lack of data driven implementation processes was also 
reported. 
Providers’ acceptability of Xpert Ultra seemed high but there was uncertainty about its accuracy, 
potentially leading to reduced trust and litigation should there be a false diagnosis. Like Xpert MTB/RIF, 
its implementation could also be hindered by infrastructural issues such as power outages, staff shortages, 
limited sputum transportation and limited availability of Xpert testing platforms in health facilities.
