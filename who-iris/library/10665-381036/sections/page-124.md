Discussion 
The sensitivity and specificity of LC-aNAATs for detection of extrapulmonary tuberculosis 
varied with site of disease and specimen type. Based on a microbiological reference standard, 
sensitivity was lowest for detection of peritoneal tuberculosis using peritoneal fluid with a 
summary sensitivity (95% CI) of 50.0% (20.0 to 80.0) and summary specificity (95% CI) of 
98.4% (89.3 to 99.8). Conversely, sensitivity was highest for detection of lymph node 
tuberculosis using lymph node tissue with a summary sensitivity (95% CI) of 96.3% (89.1 to 
98.8) and summary specificity (95% CI) of 79.1% (63.0 to 89.4). However, for lymph node 
tuberculosis in lymph node aspirate and a microbiological reference standard, the summary 
sensitivity (85%) and specificity (74%) were lower than those for lymph node tissue. Using a 
composite reference standard, the specificity rose to 97%, suggesting that most specimens were 
decontaminated for lymph node aspirate, leading to loss of viable bacteria and possibly lower 
proportion of culture positives.  
 
When we evaluated the trace results in these included studies. All trace results were considered 
positive, and it was seen as an additive value of using Xpert Ultra, especially as extrapulmonary 
tuberculosis is paucibacillary in nature. Primary study authors also noted in a few studies that 
trace results need to be correlated with other clinical signs. One included study (Minnies 2023) 
evaluated the impact of trace reclassification. The data suggested that for lymph node aspirate, 
pleural and pericardial fluid, exclusion of trace results did not change the sensitivity but 
increased the specificity. Reclassifying trace results as negative decreased sensitivity, while 
increasing the specificity.  
 
For CSF, we could analyze the impact of concentration for CSF where both sensitivity and 
specificity estimates increased for concentrated specimen relative to unconcentrated specimen 
(Table 3). For studies that provided data on starting volumes and specimen processing, 
especially for CSF and pleural fluid, even though there were no obvious relationship between 
volume and accuracy, future research could consider the impact of starting volumes and 
concentration simultaneously to assess if this increases the accuracy of LC-aNAATs for 
various forms of extrapulmonary tuberculosis.  
 
All summary estimates were derived from data for Xpert Ultra as Truenat MTB Plus did not 
qualify for the class level analyses based on the pre-defined criteria. The estimates suggest that 
future studies are needed to evaluate the accuracy of other LC-aNAATs for different forms of 
extrapulmonary tuberculosis.  
 
Combining all specimen types due to limited data for each form of extrapulmonary 
tuberculosis, LC-aNAAT had a high sensitivity (100%) and specificity (99.6%); high certainty 
(sensitivity and specificity) evidence.
