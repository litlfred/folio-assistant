Background 
There were an estimated 10.6 million people who newly developed tuberculosis (TB) in 2022 and of 
the 7.5 million reported cases, 83% involved the lungs (WHO Global Tuberculosis Report 2023). In 
2022, there were about 410,000 new cases of rifampicin-resistant or multidrug resistant TB.  Several 
rapid molecular tests are recommended by the WHO as the initial diagnostic test for TB, some of which 
can detect drug resistance simultaneously (WHO 2022). These tests can be used at the lower levels of 
the health care system. 
 
WHO groups individual tests with similar characteristics and performances into a class. The classes are 
defined by the type of technology (e.g., automated or reverse hybridization nucleic acid amplification 
tests [NAATs]), the complexity of the test for implementation (e.g., low, moderate or high—
considering the requirements of infrastructure, equipment and technical skills of laboratory staff), and 
the target conditions (e.g., diagnosis of TB, detection of resistance to first-line or second-line drugs) 
(WHO 2021). Tests that are considered low complexity automated NAATs (LC-aNAATs) include 
GeneXpert MTB/RIF Ultra, Truenat (Truenat MTB, Truenat MTB Plus, and Truenat MTB‐RIF Dx), 
STANDARD M10, SD Biosensor, and IRON-qPCR, BIONEER.  
 
Authors on this report performed a systematic review of GeneXpert MTB/RIF and GeneXpert MTB/RIF 
Ultra to inform a WHO Guideline Development Group Meeting for Molecular Diagnostics in 2019 
(Horne 2019). In the current report we update this systematic review to inform a Guideline 
Development Group that will meet in May 2024. The current review differs from prior systematic 
reviews in two important ways: 1) we assess the performance of all tests included in a class,  LC-aNAAT; 
2) we include adolescents (i.e., persons aged 10-14 years) in addition to  adults (persons >15 years). 
In the following sections we discuss LC-aNAATs for detecting TB and rifampicin resistance from 
pulmonary specimens in adolescents and adults. Of note, Molbio Diagnostics in 2024 will no longer be 
producing Truenat MTB assay for the international market and Truenat MTB is excluded from this 
systematic review. 
 
Target condition being diagnosed 
Pulmonary TB 
Pulmonary TB (PTB) is the most prevalent form of TB. Cough, fever, chills, night sweats, lethargy, loss 
of appetite, weight loss, and hemoptysis are common symptoms of PTB. PTB is the primary source of 
TB disease transmission via airborne infectious aerosols. PTB is diagnosed using various sample types, 
including sputum (spontaneous or induced), tracheal aspirates, and bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL). 
Laboratory confirmation using standard smear and culture on these samples is time consuming, with 
sputum cultures taking up to 6-8 weeks to provide positive results. In 2022, only 63% of new TB cases 
had a microbiologic diagnosis (WHO 2023).  
Rifampicin resistance  
Rifampicin-resistant TB (RR-TB) is a global health security threat. RR-TB affects hundreds of thousands 
of people annually and is the principal cause of death due to global antimicrobial-resistant disease 
(WHO 2023). Detection of drug resistance requires bacteriological confirmation of TB and testing for 
drug resistance using rapid molecular tests, culture methods, or sequencing technologies. Despite new 
bedaquiline-based shortened treatment regimens for RR-TB, it remains an extremely challenging 
condition to treat due to the need for increased monitoring, higher costs, and toxicities of the second-
line drug regimens. Resistance to rifampicin and isoniazid is defined as multidrug-resistant TB (MDR-
TB). Both MDR-TB and RR-TB require treatment with second-line drugs (WHO 2022).
