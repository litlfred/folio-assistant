Discussion 
Our review included 27 studies that assessed pulmonary TB and three studies assessed 
extrapulmonary TB. The estimated summary sensitivity (95% CI) was 84.1% (78.3 to 88.6) and the 
summary specificity was 96.1% (94.2 to 97.4) for the detection of pulmonary TB (18297 participants; 
26 studies). The summary sensitivity of TB-LAMP for detection of pulmonary TB was lower [84.1% 
(78.3 to 88.6)] than that of Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra [90.9% (86.2 to 94.7)] reported in a systematic 
review of the accuracy of Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra (Zifodya 2021). In contrast the summary specificity of 
TB-LAMP [96.1% (94.2 to 97.4)] was similar to that of Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra [95.6% (93.0 to 97.4)]. 
 
For detection of lymph node TB, the summary sensitivity (95% CI) was 94.3% (79.8 to 98.6) and the 
summary specificity was 90.0% (79.5 to 95.4) from three studies (95 participants). Due to the paucity 
of data, we did not perform meta-analyses of extrapulmonary TB involving other sites. 
 
TB-LAMP's inability to detect rifampicin resistance is one of its limitations. Considering the ease of its 
operation in resource-constrained settings, TB-LAMP could be a potential alternative for smear 
microscopy and other mWRDs that necessitate complex infrastructure.
