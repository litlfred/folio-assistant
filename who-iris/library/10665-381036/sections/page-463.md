We rated our confidence of the evidence as either high (not downgraded), moderate, low, or very low for 
each of the four domains and made a judgment about the overall confidence in the evidence supporting 
the review finding.26 Our final assessment was based on consensus among the review authors.  We 
summarized this information in the ‘Summary of findings’ tables. This summary of findings tables included 
summaries of the review findings, our overall CERQual assessments with an explanation of each 
assessment and references to studies contributing to each review finding.  
The description of the four CERQual components is detailed below25 27-29: 
 
• 
Methodological limitations of included studies: the extent to which there are concerns about the 
design or conduct of the primary studies that contributed evidence to an individual review finding. 
• 
Coherence of the review finding: an assessment of how clear and cogent the fit is between the 
data from the primary studies and a review finding that synthesizes those data. By cogent, we 
mean well supported or compelling. 
• 
Adequacy of the data contributing to a review finding: an overall determination of the degree of 
richness and quantity of data supporting a review finding. 
• 
Relevance of the included studies to the review question: the extent to which the body of evidence 
from the primary studies supporting a review finding is applicable to the context (perspective or 
population, phenomenon of interest, setting) specified in the review question. 
 
Review author reflexivity 
The diverse experiences of the author team was beneficial to the conduct and interpretation of the 
review: Epidemiology (EO); Public health (EO, MM, NE); Qualitative Evidence synthesis (EO, PK, MM, NE); 
Pharmacoepidemiology and Pharmacovigilance (PK, MM); experience with diverse geographical settings 
(EO, PK, MM, NE); and experience with researching diagnostic tests including test accuracy studies (EO), 
Qualitative research (EO, NE), implementation (EO, NE) and evaluation of diagnostic processes (EO and 
NE). 
We minimized the risk of the authors’ views affecting the analysis and interpretation of review findings 
by using a standardized data extraction form to collect the data, identifying our underlying assumptions 
at regular review progress meetings and discussion of extracted and summarized data.
