Gupta-Wright 2018 reported that the proportion of participants with a microbiological test positive 
tuberculosis diagnosis (including LF-LAM result) was higher in the intervention group that included use 
of respiratory and urine LC-aNAAT (Xpert MTB/RIF) and LF-LAM compared to the standard-of-care group 
that included LC-aNAAT but not LF-LAM (16% [210/1287] versus 7% [85/1287]; adjusted risk difference 
9.9% [95% CI 7.5 to12.4]; p<0.0001). Åhsberg 2023 reported that more patients in the intervention 
group that included use of LC-aNAAT (Xpert MTB/RIF) and LF-LAM (26% [45/173]) had a microbiological 
test-based tuberculosis diagnosis compared to the standard-of-care group (6.1% [15/246]; p<0.001).  
The pooled RR for a microbiological test positive tuberculosis diagnosis was 3.06 (95% CI 1.82 to 5.16) 
indicating that the intervention group had three times the risk of a microbiological test positive 
tuberculosis diagnosis compared to the standard-of-care (Figure 17).

Proportion of participants who underwent tuberculosis diagnostic testing  
Gupta-Wright 2018 reported that 2548/2574 (99%) of participants produced urine, whereas only 
1464/2574 (57%) were able to produce sputum. Of note, the proportion of participants able to produce 
sputum in Malawi (39%, 518/1316) was lower than in South Africa (75%, 946/1258). Åhsberg 2023 
reported that LF-LAM was requested for 169/174 (97.1%) participants, and 162/169 (95.9%) with a 
request produced a urine sample and received a test result, after a median of 0 (IQR 0 to 1) days from 
enrolment. For LC-aNAAT (Xpert), the overall request rate was 66.6%, which increased to 73.0% 
(P<0.020) in the intervention group.
