Some composite reference standards may include one or more index tests (LF-LAM, respiratory LC-
aNAATs, or both) as part of the reference standard; for example, if the decision to start tuberculosis 
treatment directly depended on the results of respiratory LC-aNAAT. This will likely lead to 
overestimation of diagnostic specificity. If the reference standard incorporated any of the index 
tests, we considered the risk of bias to be high in the reference standard domain.    
Because of the uncertainties that surround a clinical diagnosis of tuberculosis, a composite reference 
standard based on additional tests and clinical characteristics (and no tuberculosis by the 
microbiological reference standard), may incorrectly classify people without tuberculosis as having 
tuberculosis disease. If patients are correctly classified as negative by the index test but incorrectly 
classified as positive by the composite reference standard, this will lead to an underestimation of the 
index tests’ sensitivity. In absence of any other risk of bias, we answered ’unclear’ if the composite 
reference standard involved clinical features to confirm tuberculosis. This is consistent with the 2016 
Cochrane review [Shah 2016a] which also included composite reference standards. Variability in 
definition is an inherent characteristic of the composite reference standard.  
3.2 Search methods for identification of studies 
We attempted to identify all relevant studies regardless of language or publication status (published, 
unpublished, in press, and ongoing).  
3.2.1 Electronic searches  
The Cochrane Infectious Diseases Group Information Specialist developed and performed the 
literature search on November 3rd, 2023, using the search strategy described in Appendix 2. We 
searched the following electronic databases: 
• Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL)  
• MEDLINE (OVID, from 1946)  
• Embase (OVID, from 1947)  
• Science Citation Index Expanded (SCI-EXPANDED, from 1900), Conference 
Proceedings Citation Index- Science (CPCI-S, from 1900), and BIOSIS Previews (from 
1926), all three using the Web of Science platform  
• Scopus (Elseviers, from 1970) 
• WHO Global Index Medicus  
• www.clinicaltrials.gov and the search portal of the WHO International Clinical Trials 
Registry Platform (WHO ICTRP, www.who.int/trialsearch) for ongoing trials  
• ProQuest Dissertations & Theses A&l (from 1861) for relevant dissertations  
We did not use search filters for diagnostic accuracy studies [Beynon 2013]. We limited the search to 
studies published after 2010; Xpert MTB/RIF was first endorsed in 2010 for clinical use and other LC-
aNAATs and LF-LAM first became available several years later. 
3.2.2 Searching other resources.    
We examined reference lists of included studies and any relevant reviews and studies. We contacted 
experts in the field and leading research groups working on new diagnostics such as SMART4TB, 
STOP TB and FIND (Foundation for Innovative New Diagnostics). In addition, we reviewed studies 
submitted after a WHO call for literature.
