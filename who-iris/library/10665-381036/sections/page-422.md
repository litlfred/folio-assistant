3.1.3. Economic Evidence Around LC-mNAAT (TB-LAMP): 
 
Diagnostic Cost per Sample:  
According to the costing studies, the cost per test ranged from $1 to $19 (all values in 2024 USD currency). 
The lowest cost of $1 did not provide any details on the cost calculation (81), while the second lowest cost 
of $2 included the cost of equipment, reagents, and consumables in a laboratory setting (82). The study 
integrated LAMP and Screen-printed graphene electrodes (SPGE). The TB-LAMP-EC components used in 
the study include LAMP, Redox probe, and SPGE, with various other components such as primers, betaine, 
MgSO4, dNTP, nuclease-free water, and Bst enzyme 2.0 WS. The comprehensive cost per test for the 
entire testing setup, including LAMP components, the redox probe, and screen-printed graphene 
electrode strips, is approximately $2.  However, it did not factor in the production cost for commercial 
use. 
 
 Nliwasa et al. (2016) and Sohn et al. (2019) calculated costs using a bottom-up approach that considered 
direct medical and indirect non-medical costs, including travel, food, accommodation, and formal care 
(80,83). Batch size was also a factor in the cost, with Nliwasa et al. (2016) and Sohn et al. (2019) reporting 
costs ranging from $8 to $19 for 14 tests, with slight variations due to location (Vietnam<Malawi) and 
inflation (Vietnam > Malawi) (80,83). The highest cost of $19 was comparatively higher as it included the 
cost of unused tests in a batch of 14. 
 
Cost-utility analysis: 
Chitpim et al. (2022) performed a cost-utility analysis, with ICER as the primary outcome (41). The study 
compared the cost-effectiveness TB diagnostic algorithms: sputum smear microscopy (SSM) followed by 
TB-LAMP and if positive then DST, TB-LAMP followed by DST, SSM followed by Xpert MTB/RIF, only Xpert 
MTB/RIF, with SSM followed by culture and DST during a lifetime period with monthly cycle. It employed 
a hybrid decision tree Markov model incorporating diagnostic algorithms followed by treatment regimens 
for drug-sensitive TB, multidrug-resistant TB, and extensively drug-resistant TB. The analysis considered 
societal perspectives, encompassing direct medical and non-medical care, drug treatment, and formal 
care costs, all adjusted to 2021 values and discounted at 3% per annum. Chitpim et al. (2022) derived 
direct medical and non-medical costs for TB diagnostic algorithms in Thailand from literature reviews and 
previous research (41). Direct medical costs encompassed disease and drug treatment costs, including 
those associated with anti-tuberculosis drugs and laboratory tests. Direct non-medical costs covered 
transportation, food, hotel, and formal care.
