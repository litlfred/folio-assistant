Detection of pulmonary tuberculosis  
Of the 27 studies included, 62.9% (17/27) were conducted in high TB burden settings. One of the 
studies (Getahun 2017) assessed only smear negative patients and so was not included in the main 
analysis for detection of pulmonary TB. One study (Pham 2018) collected two sputum samples per 
participant and each sample was tested with TB-LAMP. The primary analysis reported by the study 
authors was based on 964 participants and 1928 sputum samples. Raw data were not available from 
the authors, so we extracted data for 976 participants from the previously published WHO policy 
guidance on TB-LAMP for the diagnosis of pulmonary TB (WHO 2016). 
Methodological quality 
Figure 2 and Figure 3 summarise the risk of bias and applicability assessment of the included studies   
for the detection of pulmonary TB. We judged one study (3.7%) to have a high risk of bias in the patient 
selection domain for including only the participants with presumptive TB after triaging them with 
sputum AFB smear and found to be smear negative.  We judged one study (3.7%) to have an unclear 
risk of bias in the patient selection domain due to lack of clarity about participant recruitment. Five 
studies (18.5%) did not blind the reference standard and were judged to have an unclear risk of bias 
in the reference standard domain. One study (3.7%) was judged to have an unclear risk of bias in the 
flow and timing domain for not stating the time interval and sequence of the tests. Regarding 
applicability, in the patient selection domain, we judged high concern for two studies (7.4%) because 
either the participants were evaluated at tertiary care centres or the test was done using stored 
samples. Three studies (11.1%) had unclear applicability concerns in the index test domain due to lack 
of information on the study procedure and one study (3.7%) lacked information about speciation and 
was judged unclear concern in the reference standard domain.
