Studies evaluating Xpert Ultra on gastric aspirate specimens (12 studies) 
In the patient selection domain, for MRS, we judged the risk of bias as low for most studies (10/12, 
83.3%) and high-risk two studies (16.6%) because the manner of participant selection was a 
retrospective convenience sample and due to unclear inclusion criteria in the other. Regarding 
applicability, we judged low concern for most studies (9/12, 75%), unclear for one study (1/12, 8.3%) 
because the setting was undefined, and high for two studies (2/12, 16.7%) because only inpatients 
were enrolled.  
  
In the index test domain, we judged the risk of bias as low for all studies (100%). Regarding 
applicability, we judged low concern for all studies (100%) owing to the use of the standard method 
for processing Xpert Ultra in gastric specimens.  
  
In the reference standard domain, for MRS, we judged the risk of bias as low for all studies (100%) 
because most studies used multiple cultures to verify pulmonary tuberculosis. Regarding applicability, 
we judged low concern for most studies (10/12, 83.3%) and unclear concern for two studies, because 
in one study cultures were performed on sputum or gastric aspirate specimens, and in the other, the 
specimen type for culture was not specified. For CRS, we judged the risk of bias as low for 2/7 studies 
(28.6%); and unclear for 1/7 studies (14.3%) because it was unclear if Xpert Ultra on gastric aspirate 
was used in the CRS; and high for 4/7 studies (57.1%), because Xpert Ultra on gastric aspirate was 
used in the CRS.  
  
In the flow and timing domain, for MRS, we judged a low risk of bias for most studies (11/12, 91.7%) 
and a high risk of bias for one study (1/12, 8.3%) because in this study children were mostly excluded 
from the analysis.  
 
Studies evaluating Xpert Ultra on nasopharyngeal specimens (7 studies) 
In the patient selection domain, we judged the risk of bias as low for most studies (6/7, 85.7%) and 
high-risk one study (14.3%) because participant selection was by a retrospective convenience sample 
and children were mostly excluded from the analysis. Regarding applicability, we judged low concern 
for all 7 studies (100%). 
  
In the index test domain, we judged the risk of bias as low for all studies (100%) because Xpert Ultra 
test results are automatically generated, and the user is provided with printable test results (thus there 
is no room for subjective interpretation of test results) and the threshold for positivity is pre-specified. 
Regarding applicability, we judged this as low concern for all studies (100%) because all performed 
Xpert Ultra following product recommendations. 
 
In the reference standard domain, for MRS, we judged the risk of bias as low for all studies (100%). 
Regarding applicability, we judged low concern for most studies (6/7, 85.7%) and high concern for 
one study.  For CRS, we judged the risk of bias as low for one study (1/4, 25%); and unclear for three 
studies (3/4, 75%) because it was unclear if Xpert Ultra on NPA was used in the CRS.  
 
In the flow and timing domain, we judged a low risk of bias for all studies (100%).
