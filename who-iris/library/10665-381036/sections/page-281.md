All studies for the purpose of this review used a paired diagnostic accuracy design.  
In the index test domain, we judged five studies (83.3%) to have low risk of bias as the risk of bias for 
each index test was judged low in QUADAS-2, while we could not tell for one study (16.7%; [Chipinduro 
2017]), and thus judged unclear risk of bias.  
In the reference standard domain, we judged all studies based on a MRS to have low risk of bias as the 
studies avoided incorporating LC-aNAAT as part of the MRS. For CRS, we judged five studies (83.3%) to 
have high risk of bias because they included LC-aNAAT as part of their reference standard [Chipinduro 
2017; Jaganath 2024; LaCourse 2018; Olbrich 2024; Orikiriza 2021]. One study (16.7%) had unclear risk 
of bias [Hanrahan 2018].  
In the flow and timing domain, we judged all studies (100%) to have low risk of bias [Chipinduro 2017; 
Hanrahan 2018; Jaganath 2024; LaCourse 2018; Olbrich 2024; Orikiriza 2021] for the comparison.  
4.3 Findings 
The key study characteristics are summarized by study (Appendix 8: Characteristics of included studies). 
For MRS, ten studies involved 2,798 participants, 216 (7.7%) with tuberculosis disease. Eight of the 
studies evaluated diagnostic accuracy for the parallel use of LC-aNAATs on a respiratory sample and 
stool compared to each LC-aNAAT alone (involving 2,145 participant, tuberculosis prevalence 8.1% 
[173/2,145]). Six studies evaluated diagnostic accuracy for the parallel use of LC-aNAATs on a respiratory 
sample and stool and LF-LAM on urine (involving 653 participant, tuberculosis prevalence 6.6% 
[43/653]). 
For CRS, 14 studies involved 4,253 participants, 1,750 (14.1%) with tuberculosis disease. 12 of the 
studies evaluated diagnostic accuracy for the parallel use of LC-aNAATs on a respiratory sample and 
stool compared to each LC-aNAAT alone (involving 3,579 participant, tuberculosis prevalence 40.9% 
[1,464/3,579]). Six studies evaluated diagnostic accuracy for the parallel use of LC-aNAATs on a 
respiratory sample and stool and LF-LAM on urine against a MRS (involving 674 participant, tuberculosis 
prevalence 42.4% [286/674]). 
The studies were conducted in 14 different countries, five (41.7%) were conducted in low-income 
countries, eight (66.7%) in lower middle-income countries and one (8.3%) in an upper-income country.
