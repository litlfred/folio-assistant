4.5.5 Non‐determinate results 
Five studies reported invalid results for either LC-aNAAT on respiratory samples or stool and LF-LAM 
on urine [Chipinduro 2017; Hanrahan 2018; LaCourse 2018; Olbrich 2024; Orikiriza 2021].  
LC-aNAAT on respiratory samples 
Three studies [Hanrahan 2018; LaCourse 2018; Orikiriza 2021] reported invalid results, ranging from 
0.2% (1/424; [Babo 2023]) to 1.3% (2/160; [Hanrahan 2018]). Two studies observed zero invalid 
results [Olbrich 2024; Chipinduro 2017]. 
LC-aNAAT on stool 
Two studies [Hanrahan 2018; Olbrich 2024] reported invalid results, ranging from 2.3% (18/757; 
[Olbrich 2024]) to 13.2% (15/114; [Hanrahan 2018]). Two studies observed zero invalid results 
[Chipinduro 2017; Orikiriza 2021]. We could not tell for one study if results of LC-aNAAT stool were 
missing or invalid test results (12.6%, 22/174, [LaCourse 2018]). 
LF-LAM on urine 
Only one study [LaCourse 2018] reported invalid results, being 2.2% (3/135). Four studies observed 
zero invalid results [Olbrich 2024; Hanrahan 2018; Orikiriza 2021; Chipinduro 2017]. 
4.5.6 Sensitivity analyses 
When excluding studies at high or unclear risk of bias (QUADAS-C) and with high or unclear concerns 
regarding applicability (QUADAS-2); 4 studies, 474 participants, the pooled difference in sensitivity 
comparing the accuracy of parallel testing to LC-aNAAT combined alone was 3.9% (95% CrI 0.3 to 
17.7) and pooled difference in specificity to -11.2 (95% CrI -23.0 to -5.2), compared to the primary 
analysis with a pooled difference in sensitivity of 6.9% (95% CrI 1.5 to 20.1) and specificity of −3.4%
