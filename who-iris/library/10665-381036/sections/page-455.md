The WHO End TB strategy aims at having 90% of TB patients diagnosed with WHO recommended 
molecular rapid diagnostics by 2025,3 but the use of these rapid tests is still minimal. Of the 6.4 million 
people newly diagnosed with TB in 2021, only 38 % were diagnosed with a WHO recommended rapid 
diagnostic test with a large variation between countries.1 A recent mixed methods review of 117 articles 
and 13 operational research reports examined implementation solutions to increase the uptake and 
impact of WHO recommended rapid diagnostic tests.10 This review informed the WHO report on universal 
access to rapid TB diagnostics10 by reporting several barriers, enablers and implementation strategies 
across different levels; patient level, provider level, health facility level and data management and health 
systems levels. It concluded that person-centered TB approaches coupled with multicomponent strategies 
are needed to improve the uptake and access to molecular rapid diagnostic tests. This review had limited 
data on molecular rapid diagnostic tests besides Xpert and it did not delineate the user perspectives about 
the rapid tests across classes of NAATs. It also did not provide evidence on implementing parallel TB 
testing. 
 
A previously published Cochrane qualitative review11 synthesized findings about provider and recipient 
views on low complexity NAATs from 32 articles and informed the WHO consolidated guidelines on 
tuberculosis: module 3: diagnosis: rapid diagnostics for tuberculosis detection.5  However this review 
mostly included evidence about perspectives on standard Xpert MTB/RIF tests ((referred to as Xpert 
MTB/RIF here forth) which have been superseded by Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra (referred to as Xpert Ultra here 
forth) and other new rapid NAATs.   Our review builds on this previous qualitative review by examining 
user perspectives of three classes of NAATs (low complexity manual, low complexity automated and 
moderate complexity NAATs) and parallel testing of NAATs in children and People living with HIV (PLHIV) 
in preparation for the upcoming WHO guideline development group meeting in May 2024.
