Incremental accuracy of parallel testing versus LF-LAM alone based on the 
microbiological reference standard  
The study-specific estimates of difference in accuracy between the parallel use of respiratory LC-aNAAT 
and urine LF-LAM versus LF-LAM alone for tuberculosis in adults and adolescents with HIV, based on the 
microbiological reference standard, are shown in Appendix 15. The pooled difference in sensitivity and

4.4.2 Comparative diagnostic accuracy based on the composite reference standard 
Twenty-three studies compared the accuracy of the parallel use of LC-aNAAT and LF-LAM versus LC-
aNAAT alone using a composite reference standard involving 11,109 participants, 3,723 (33.5%) with 
tuberculosis.  
Tuberculosis prevalence according to the composite reference standard ranged from 9% [Bonnet 2023] 
to 89% [Boloko 2022]. The definition of the composite reference standard differed between studies and 
was based on different combinations of microbiological tests, histology, radiographic findings, clinical 
assessment, or decision to initiate tuberculosis treatment. For 20 of 23 studies, the composite reference 
standard included initiation of tuberculosis treatment. The index test was incorporated in the reference 
standard in 21 of 23 studies: LC-aNAAT in 21 studies and LF-LAM in 11 studies [Åhsberg 2023; Bonnet 
2023; Gupta-Wright 2018; Huerga 2017a; Huerga 2021; Huerga 2019; Huerga 2020; J.G. Peter 2016; 
Spooner 2022; Stead 2023; Szekely 2022]. See Appendix 9: Characteristics of individual studies. 
Diagnostic accuracy of parallel use of respiratory LC-aNAAT and urine LF-LAM 
based on the composite reference standard  
See Appendix 16 for forest plots of the diagnostic accuracy of parallel testing, LC-aNAAT alone, and LF-
LAM alone based on a composite reference standard.  
The pooled sensitivity of parallel testing and urine LF-LAM was 67.6% (95% CrI 59.9 to 74.6) and 
specificity 96.2% (95% CrI 92.8 to 98.1), (23 studies; 11,109 participants, 3,723 (33.5%) with 
tuberculosis).  
Incremental accuracy of parallel testing vs LC-aNAAT alone based on the 
composite reference standard  
Within studies, incremental difference in sensitivity for the parallel use of respiratory LC-aNAAT and 
urine LF-LAM versus respiratory LC-aNAAT alone ranged from 2% [Benjamin 2019; Broger-Cohort4 2020]  
to 55% [Åhsberg 2023; Gupta-Wright 2018]. The difference in specificity for parallel testing versus LC-
aNAAT alone ranged from 0% [Broger-Cohort4 2020; de Vasconcellos 2021; Gupta-Wright 2018; 
Spooner 2022] to -36% [Huerga 2019]. The study-specific estimates of difference in accuracy between 
the parallel use of LC-aNAAT and LF-LAM versus LC-aNAAT alone based on the composite reference 
standard in shown in Appendix 17.  
The pooled difference in sensitivity and pooled difference in specificity between parallel testing versus 
LC-aNAAT alone when using a composited reference standard was 16.0% (95% CrI 10.7 to 22.9; 95% PI 
2.3 to 60.3) and -3.5% (95% CrI -6.6 to -1.7; 95% PI -47.2 to -0.1). See Figure 13.
