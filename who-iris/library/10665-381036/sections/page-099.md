Methods 
 
Search methods 
 
Electronic searches 
The Cochrane Infectious Diseases Group Information Specialist (Vittoria Lutje (VL)) 
performed literature searches between 2nd and 3rd October 2023, without language 
restrictions, in the following databases, using the search terms described in Appendix 1: 
Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), included in the Cochrane 
Library, issue 10, October 2023; MEDLINE (Ovid); Embase (Ovid); Science Citation Index 
and Biosis previews (ISI Web of Knowledge); WHO Global Index Medicus; and SCOPUS 
(Elsevier). VL also searched ClinicalTrials.gov (clinicaltrials.gov/) and the WHO Clinical 
Trials Registry platform (www.who.int/trialsearch) to identify ongoing trials.  
 
Searching other resources 
The review authors examined the reference lists of included articles and relevant review 
articles identified through the electronic searches. VL also searched for relevant dissertation 
in ProQuest Dissertations & Theses A&I.  The review authors searched information on 
ongoing and unpublished studies from manufacturers and researchers through a WHO public 
call; and from experts working on new diagnostics for TB such as STOP TB Partnership’s 
New Diagnostic Working Group and FIND (the global alliance for diagnostics). 
 
Selection criteria 
We included cross-sectional and cohort studies using non-respiratory specimens to evaluate 
Xpert Ultra against a microbiological or composite reference standard for tuberculosis and 
culture-based drug susceptibility testing or whole genome sequencing for rifampicin resistance. 
We included the following common forms of extrapulmonary tuberculosis: tuberculous 
meningitis and pleural, lymph node, bone or joint, genitourinary, peritoneal, pericardial, and 
disseminated tuberculosis. We excluded studies that evaluated the index tests in gastric fluid, 
as this specimen is used most often to investigate pulmonary tuberculosis in children. We also 
excluded stool specimens because tuberculosis bacteria may be swallowed and passed into 
stool as a marker of pulmonary tuberculosis.
