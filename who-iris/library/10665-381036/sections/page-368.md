5.3 Parallel testing - differences to previous Cochrane systematic review  
Systematic reviews and meta-analyses typically assess tuberculosis diagnostic accuracy by individual 
tests, or may compare diagnostic accuracy review of two or more test typically when a new version of a 
test has emerged [Penn-Nicholson 2021; Zifodya 2021]. However, tests may be used in combination in 
clinical practice, motivating this systematic review on parallel testing. We included 31 fully paired 
studies in this review. Our findings are consistent with the original review of LF-LAM where sensitivity of 
LF-LAM and sputum Xpert MTB/RIF combined (either test positive) was 75% (61% to 87%), representing 
a difference of 13% (1% to 37%) compared to Xpert MTB/RIF alone (three studies, 909 participants, 36% 
with tuberculosis) [Shah 2016b]. Pooled specificity was 93% (81% to 97%), a 4% (1% to 16%) decrease 
from Xpert MTB/RIF alone (three studies, 909 participants, 36% with tuberculosis). Notably, although we 
did not do a single test systematic review, estimates for LF-LAM individual accuracy was consistent with 
the previous review update on LF-LAM where sensitivity and specificity (95% CrI) was 42% (31-55) and 
91% (85-95), (8 studies, 3449 participants, 37% with tuberculosis)[Bjerrum 2019b].  
5.4 Strengths and weaknesses of the review  
The findings in this review are based on comprehensive searching, strict inclusion criteria, and 
standardized data extraction. The strength of our review is a large comparative accuracy study of the 
role of parallel testing with respiratory LC-aNAAT and urine LF-LAM versus that of a single test alone. For 
the microbiological reference standard, we carefully harmonized the case definition across the 31 
studies to not include the index tests. We used the composite reference standard to include also 
clinically diagnosed tuberculosis and explore the influence on specificity and comparative diagnostic 
accuracy. However, both index tests are recommended tests for tuberculosis diagnosis and were often 
incorporated in the reference standard in primary studies which may have resulted in exclusions due to 
insufficient data for the microbiological reference standard. The sample size is large, and we 
communicated extensively with authors of primary studies to obtain unpublished data when it could be 
re-analysed for the purpose of this review. However, this also may be a cause of concern for errors in 
analyses and results have not been peer-reviewed. We found considerable heterogeneity across studies 
with prediction intervals for both sensitivity and specificity being wider than the CrI around the pooled 
estimates. The review was further limited by the number of studies that used a lower quality reference 
standard and the high risk of selection bias in several studies owing to exclusion of patients unable to 
produce sputum. While we included all commercially available LC-aNAATs assays in our search, only 
studies evaluating Xpert MTB/RIF and Ultra in combination with LF-LAM were identified. This may limit 
the applicability of our results to settings where Xpert MTB/RIF and Ultra are not available.  Overall, we 
had low concern about the applicability of the included studies to our review question as assessed by 
QUADAS-2. However, studies using frozen samples for LF-LAM testing had unclear applicability concern. 
Last, only one study included young adolescents aged 10-15 years [Olbrich 2023]. Several studies 
included participants aged 15 or 16 years and above [Huerga 2023a; Huerga 2017a; Huerga 2019; Kasaro 
2020; Kebede 2021; Shapiro 2022], although median age suggested few adolescents included. See 
Appendix 9. Characteristics of individual studies. This may reduce generalizability to adolescents with 
HIV that are at high risk of tuberculosis, but often neglected [Snow 2020] [Swanepoel 2022].
