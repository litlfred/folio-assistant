4.7.4 Impact of respiratory LC-aNAATs and urine LF-LAM on time to treatment 
and proportion treated for tuberculosis in adult inpatients with HIV 
Two trials evaluated time to diagnosis, reporting median and interquartile ranges, which precluded 
meta-analysis of mean differences. Using bedside urine LF-LAM and study directed treatment 
recommendation, Peter 2016 reported that the time from enrolment to treatment was lower for the 
intervention group compared to the standard-of-care groups: median 0 days (IQR 0 to 2) versus 1 day 
(IQR 0 to 3) (p <0.0001). 
Using lab-based, study-directed parallel testing, Gupta-Wright 2018 reported that time from diagnosis to 
treatment was short (median 1 day, IQR 0 to 1) and was similar in both groups.  
Using clinician directed urine LF-LAM testing and interpretation, Åhsberg 2023 reported that of those 
patients with a per-protocol tuberculosis diagnosis, 67/105 (63.8%) initiated tuberculosis treatment 
during follow-up, after a median of 3 (IQR, 1 to 8) days, with no difference between study groups.  
Gupta-Wright 2018 reported the proportion of participants treated for tuberculosis was higher in the 
intervention group that included use of respiratory and urine LC-aNAAT (Xpert MTB/RIF) and LF-LAM 
compared to the standard-of-care group (268/1287, 21% versus 182/1287, 14%; adjusted risk difference 
-3.1% [-4.9 to -1.4]; p=0.0004). Åhsberg reported 34/173 (19.7%) of the intervention group, that 
included use of LC-aNAAT (Xpert MTB/RIF) and LF-LAM, were treated for tuberculosis compared to the 
standard-of-care group (33/246, 13.4%) (p=0.086).   
The pooled RR was 1.47 (95% CI 1.25 to 1.73), indicating that participants in the intervention group were 
1.47 times more likely to be treated for tuberculosis compared to those in the standard-of-care group 
(Figure 18).
