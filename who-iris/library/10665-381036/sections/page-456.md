Review objectives  
• 
To synthesize provider and end user perspectives on low complexity and moderate complexity 
NAATs for diagnosis of tuberculosis and tuberculosis drug resistance (DR-TB). 
• 
To synthesize provider and end user perspectives on parallel use of NAATs or of different 
specimens on NAATs in children and PLHIV. 
 
Methods 
The methods for study inclusion, data collection and analysis were based on the Cochrane Guidance for 
Qualitative Research.12 We built on the methodology of our previously published Cochrane qualitative 
review on user perspectives of low complexity NAATs and broadened the eligibility criteria to include 
moderate complexity NAATs and parallel testing as well.11 Where applicable we reported our findings in 
accordance with the reporting guidelines; Enhancing transparency in reporting the synthesis of qualitative 
research (ENTREQ).13  
In anticipation of limited qualitative data on NAATs besides Xpert MTB/RIF, we followed guidance by 
Murad et al on strategies for improving the utility of evidence synthesis when dealing with insufficient 
evidence.14 We followed this guidance by summarizing evidence on similar interventions (e.g. Xpert 
MTB/RIF). Moreover, we assumed that results from standard Xpert MTB/RIF would apply to Xpert Ultra 
as the same platform and procedures are used and Cartridges though different have similar 
functionality.15  
Inclusion Criteria 
 
Types of studies 
We included any study with a qualitative methodology or study design related to the application of low 
complexity and moderate complexity NAATs for TB and DR-TB. Such qualitative designs included 
ethnography, grounded theory, phenomenological study designs and case studies. We considered studies 
about pathways from diagnosis to treatment, operational research, mixed methods studies, feasibility and 
acceptability assessments which contain qualitative data or findings. In this update, we included 
additional eligible studies in our updated search and combined them with studies about Xpert MTB/RIF 
that were already included in our previous Cochrane review on low complexity-NAATs11.
