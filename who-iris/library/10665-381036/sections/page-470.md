The strengths of our review included utilizing a robust study search and selection process and building on 
a previously utilized framework for our thematic analysis. Studies were screened and appraised in 
duplicate to minimize bias. We also strengthened the limited evidence on Xpert Ultra by adding evidence 
about the similar standard Xpert MTB/RIF test.14 
Although Xpert MTB/RIF has been superseded by newer rapid NAATs, most of the available qualitative 
evidence is still mostly about single testing with Xpert MTB/RIF. Our review was mainly limited by the 
paucity of direct evidence on newer NAATs, not identifying other tests apart from Xpert Ultra. Similarities 
between the two low complexity testing platforms can be used to infer perspectives about the newer 
NAATs being introduced. Moreover, the testing platform, the steps used and infrastructural requirements 
are the same and the turnaround time minimally different .15 We did not identify any qualitative evidence 
on moderate complexity NAATs or parallel testing. The included studies about Xpert Ultra were from three 
countries: Kenya, Swaziland and Belarus30 31 limiting the generalizability of the findings. 
Uncertainties and future research 
There is a need for more high quality qualitative studies examining perspectives on NAATs apart from 
Xpert MTB/RIF  and other classes of NAATs. Such studies would give less biased information compared to 
studies about Xpert MTB/RIF which are being phased out of use as initial diagnostic tests for TB. More 
qualitative studies on the acceptability, feasibility and equity concerns of parallel testing would guide 
better implementation of such strategies. Parallel testing can be costly as it requires the use of two tests8 
9 hence its added value has to be justified to enable better uptake of this approach. More studies about 
perceptions of the use of such tests in hard to diagnose patients such as children, people living with HIV, 
EPTB would boost evidence of the value of such tests in disadvantaged populations. More operational 
research identifying gaps in pathways in supplies as well as solutions will enable the effective 
implementation of such tests. Furthermore, qualitative evidence on perspectives of users and providers 
on parallel testing would guide better implementation of diagnostic algorithms comprising multiple tests 
commonly recommended in guidelines. 
Conclusions 
Although Xpert MTB/RIF has been superseded by Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra and other rapid NAATs, qualitative 
evidence for newer NAATs is limited.  While low complexity NAATs in general are valued for their accuracy,
