3.1.4. Target condition 
For children with and without HIV, the target condition was pulmonary tuberculosis disease. We 
included both drug-susceptible and drug-resistant tuberculosis disease. 
3.1.5 Reference standards 
Included studies required at least one of the following two reference standards for pulmonary 
tuberculosis disease:  
3.1.5.1. Microbiological Reference Standard (MRS):  
For a microbiological reference standard (MRS) 
• 
Presence of tuberculosis disease was defined as a positive Mycobacterium tuberculosis culture on 
any respiratory sample type  
• 
Absence of tuberculosis disease was defined as a negative Mycobacterium tuberculosis culture on 
any respiratory sample type  
Both automated liquid culture methods such as liquid and solid culture methods were included in the 
reference standard. A positive result of either respiratory sample liquid culture or solid culture alone or 
in combination was accepted as a diagnosis of pulmonary tuberculosis disease; a negative result in all 
cultures indicated no pulmonary tuberculosis disease.  
In children with paucibacillary disease, tuberculosis is verified by culture in only 15% to 50% of cases, 
depending on disease severity, challenges of obtaining specimens, and resources [Graham 2015]. 
Evaluation of multiple respiratory specimens, of the same or different types, may increase the yield of 
culture for tuberculosis disease [Cruz 2013; Zar 2012]. Therefore, when more than one respiratory 
specimen is used to confirm pulmonary tuberculosis disease, we considered the reference standard to 
be of higher-quality. When only one respiratory specimen was used for tuberculosis diagnosis, we 
considered the reference standard to be of lower quality. The quality of the reference standard was 
addressed in the reference standard domain of the revised Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy 
Studies (QUADAS-2) tool (Appendix 2).
