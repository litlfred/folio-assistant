Should LC-aNAATs on respiratory samples be used to diagnose rifampicin 
resistance in PLHIV (adults and adolescents) with signs and symptoms or 
screened positive for pulmonary TB against a microbiological reference 
standard? 
We identified no studies on this topic that met inclusion criteria. Several studies in Figure 14 either did 
not include PLHIV or did not report participants’ HIV status (Aguilera-Alonso 2022, Chakravorty 2017, 
Chien 2020, Gomathi 2020, Li 2023, Piersimoni 2019, Wang 2019, Wang 2022), did not have rifampicin 
resistant isolates in PLHIV (Park 2019), or did not provide results by HIV status (Dorman 2018, MacLean 
2023, Penn-Nicholson 2021). This specific question was not addressed in the 2019 GDG report, for 
either Xpert Ultra or Xpert MTB/RIF. 
 
Rifampicin resistance in adults and adolescents by smear status 
Of the six (1693 participants) studies that evaluated smear-positive sputum specimens, sensitivity was 
not estimable for one study (Appendix 3, Figure 11). The sensitivities in the remaining five studies (1692 
participants) included in the meta-analysis were between 57% and 100% and the specificities were 
between 97% and 100%. The summary sensitivity (95% CI) was 92.3% (75.8 to 97.9) and the summary 
specificity was 97.8% (96.7 to 98.5) (Table 3).  
 
For the six (559 participants) studies that evaluated smear-negative sputum specimens, sensitivity was 
not estimable for one study (Appendix 3, Figure 11). The sensitivities in the remaining five studies (550 
participants) included in the meta-analysis were between 17% and 100% and the specificities were 
between 94% and 100%. The summary sensitivity (95% CI) was 91.5% (38.4 to 99.5) and the summary 
specificity was 99.6% (90.7 to 100.0) (Table 3).  
 
Discussion 
We found LC-aNAATs to be sensitive and specific for diagnosing pulmonary tuberculosis and 
rifampicin resistance, consistent with findings reported previously.  
● 
We found LC-aNAATs to be sensitive and specific for diagnosing PTB and rifampicin resistance. 
LC-aNAATs have a summary sensitivity (95% CI) and specificity (95% CI) of 90.4% (88.0 to 92.4) 
and 94.9% (93.0 to 96.3) against MRS, with similar performance between assays (14840 
participants; high certainty of evidence)     
● 
Among PLHIV, 12 studies contributed to LC-aNAAT summary sensitivity of 87.4% (83.8 to 90.3) 
and summary specificity of 95.2% (92.7 to 96.9). (2016 participants; high certainty of evidence) 
● 
LC-aNAAT accuracy in testing smear-negative respiratory specimens was lower than overall 
accuracy based on 18 studies: summary sensitivity 79.8% (74.9 to 84.0) and specificity 94.3% 
(92.0 to 96.0). 
● 
LC-aNAAT accuracy in testing respiratory specimens from persons with a prior history of TB was 
lower than overall accuracy based on 11 studies: summary sensitivity was 79.8% (74.9 to 84.0) 
and summary specificity 94.3% (92.0 to 96.0) 
●
