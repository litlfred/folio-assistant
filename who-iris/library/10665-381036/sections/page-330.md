Previous reviews have traditionally assessed diagnostic accuracy of LC-aNAATs and LF-LAM each in 
isolation for detection of tuberculosis including Xpert MTB/RIF and Xpert Ultra assays (Cepheid, 
Sunnyvale, USA) for pulmonary tuberculosis [Zifodya 2021; Shapiro 2021] and extrapulmonary 
tuberculosis [Kohli 2021a] and urine LF-LAM for people with HIV [Bjerrum 2019c]. Some studies have 
found that urine LF-LAM and respiratory LC-aNAAT may be complementary for the diagnosis of 
tuberculosis disease in people with HIV [Shah 2014; Barr 2020; Lawn 2017b; Broger 2023a]. In the 
2016 Cochrane systematic review of the diagnostic accuracy of LF-LAM, sensitivity increased by 13% 
when combining LF-LAM and sputum Xpert MTB/RIF compared to sputum Xpert alone while the 
specificity decreased by 4% [Shah 2016b]. However, results were based on few studies and analyses 
restricted to participants able to produce sputum.  
In this systematic review, we evaluate the incremental diagnostic accuracy of using LC-aNAAT on 
respiratory samples in parallel with urine LF-LAM (i.e. LF-LAM in combination with LC-aNAAT in 
which positivity was defined as either test positive) for diagnosis of any tuberculosis disease (without 
distinction of specific site of disease) among people with HIV. The focus is on diagnosis of 
tuberculosis disease in adults and adolescents with HIV. This review does not include data on drug 
resistance. 
1.2 Index tests 
We assessed two index tests: LC-aNAATs conducted on respiratory samples and LF-LAM test 
conducted on urine. LC-aNAATs include the Xpert MTB/RIF and Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra (Cepheid, 
Sunnyvale, USA); Truenat MTB and MTB Plus (Molbio Diagnostics, Goa, India); STANDARD M10 (SD 
Biosensor, Republic of Korea); and IRON-qPCR (BIONEER, Republic of Korea). Determine TB LAM Ag 
(Abbott, Chicago, USA) is currently the only commercially available LF-LAM test. 
1.2.1 Index test, LF-LAM 
LF-LAM detects lipoarabinomannan (LAM), a lipopolysaccharide in mycobacterial cell walls, which is 
released from metabolically active or degenerating bacterial cells during tuberculosis disease [Briken 
2004]. LAM is found in the bloodstream of persons with tuberculosis disease and subsequently 
filtered across the glomerular basement membrane of the kidneys into urine [Bulterys 2019]. To 
note, LF-LAM detection in urine is not site specific, meaning a positive LF-LAM test alone does not 
tell us whether the patient has pulmonary or extrapulmonary tuberculosis, or both [Kerkhoff 2020]. 
Evidence suggests that concentrations of LAM in urine increase progressively with degree of 
immunosuppression, probably due to higher rate of mycobacterial dissemination or overall 
mycobacterial burden [Shah 2010; Broger 2020]. Renal M. tuberculosis disease may also contribute 
to the presence of LAM in urine, as evidenced by autopsy studies [Cox 2015], but urinary tract 
disease is not necessary for LAM antigenuria.  
LF-LAM testing is performed manually by applying 60 μL of unprocessed urine to the sample pad of 
the Determine TB LAM Ag test and leave the strip test to incubate at room temperature for 25 
minutes [Abbott. 2022]. The strip is then inspected by eye. The intensity of any visible band is graded 
by comparing it with the intensities of the bands on a manufacturer-supplied reference scale card. 
Of note, the reference scale was revised in January 2014. Prior to January 2014, the reference scale 
card included five bands (grade 1 representing a very low intensity band to grade 5 representing a 
high/dark intensity band). After January 2014, the manufacturer revised the reference scale card to 
have four reference bands, such that the band intensity for the new grade 1 corresponded to the 
band intensity for the previous grade 2. Under the current manufacturer recommendations (using 
the revised four bands reference card), only bands that are grade 1 or higher are considered positive 
[Abbott. 2022].  
A 2019 Cochrane Review assessed the accuracy of a single LF-LAM test for the diagnosis of 
tuberculosis disease in people with HIV [Bjerrum 2019c]. Among participants with symptoms, the LF-
LAM pooled sensitivity was 42% (95% credible interval (CrI) 31 to 55) and pooled specificity was 91% 
(CrI 85 to 95) [Bjerrum 2019c]. Among unselected participants not assessed for signs and symptoms 
of tuberculosis, LF-LAM pooled sensitivity was 35% (CrI 22 to 50), and pooled specificity was 95% (CrI
