1. 
BACKGROUND 
 
In 2022, Tuberculosis (TB) was the second leading cause of death from a single infectious agent after 
COVID-19 globally(1).  TB resulted in 1.3 million deaths in 2022 with more than 10.6 million people who 
fell ill with TB, and of the 6.2 million people diagnosed with pulmonary TB worldwide, 63% were 
bacteriologically confirmed. The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends using rapid molecular 
diagnostic tests as the initial diagnostic test in individuals with signs and symptoms of TB. Molecular-based 
testing offers rapid identification of the Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB) complex, determination of 
drug resistance, and is endorsed by WHO for detection screening in individuals with symptoms consistent 
with TB (2,3). However, evidence shows that in 2022, molecular WHO-recommended rapid diagnostic 
tests (mWRDs) were only used as the initial test for 47% (3.5 million) of the 7.5 million people newly 
diagnosed with TB, albeit an increase from previous years (1). The most prevalent form of drug-resistant 
TB is multi-drug-resistant TB (MDR-TB), caused by bacteria that do not respond to treatment with isoniazid 
and rifampicin, the two most effective first-line TB drugs. The diagnosis and treatment of MDR-TB is 
complex and expensive; with only 2 in 5 people with drug-resistant TB accessing treatment in 2022. 
  
There is a need to scale up capacity for accurate diagnosis of TB and drug resistance testing.  With 
advances in the development and scaling up of MDR-TB case detection and treatment (4), the coverage 
of mWRDs would need to more than double to reach the new target of 100% by 2027 set at 2023 UN high-
level meeting on TB(1).  In addition, accurate and timely drug susceptibility tests (DST) are critical for early 
and appropriate clinical management and surveillance of drug-resistant TB(5,6). Many countries rely on 
the conventional phenotypic DST method in a solid medium, which is affordable and reliable. However, 
this method can introduce delays as generating the result may take 28-42 days(6). Culture in a liquid 
medium can reduce the delays in initiating appropriate treatment but comes with drawbacks and still 
takes a minimum of ten days (7,8).  
 
Nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs) are molecular based diagnostics for TB and drug resistance that 
show high diagnostic accuracy, ensuring rapid drug sensitivity testing to at least rifampicin, isoniazid, and 
fluoroquinolones. However, despite the WHO endorsement, routine access to molecular testing in high 
TB-burden areas remains limited (9). Implementing molecular diagnostic testing is challenging in resource-
constrained settings due to high costs, the need for laboratory infrastructure and maintenance, and the 
scarcity of trained personnel (10).
