3.3.7 Sensitivity analysis 
We performed the following sensitivity analyses by limiting inclusion in the meta-analysis based on the 
following criteria: 
• 
Only participants screening positive for tuberculosis using CXR  
• 
Exclusion of studies at high or unclear risk of bias (QUADAS-C) and high concerns regarding 
applicability (QUADAS-2) in at least one domain  
• 
Alterative prior distributions which are less informative: increasing the variance of the normal 
distributions over the summary log odds of the sensitivity or specificity to 100 and using a uniform 
distribution ranging from zero to three over the between-study standard deviation on the log 
odds scale  
3.3.9 Assessment of reporting bias 
We did not carry out formal assessment of publication bias using methods such as funnel plots or 
regression tests because such techniques have not been helpful for diagnostic test accuracy studies 
[Takwoingi Y 2022].  
3.3.10 Assessment of the certainty of the evidence  
We assessed the certainty of the evidence using the GRADE approach for comparative test accuracy 
questions [Yang 2021b], which involves rating risk of bias, indirectness, inconsistency, imprecision, and 
risk of publication bias across the body of evidence. We assessed the certainty that the true difference in 
sensitivity and true difference in specificity lie within the bounds of the 95% CrI (a minimally 
contextualized rating). We used the QUADAS-2 and QUADAS-C judgments for rating risk of bias and 
indirectness. We examined variability in study-specific estimates (of difference in sensitivity or 
specificity) and overlap in confidence intervals, and rated down for inconsistency when point estimates 
of individual studies were outside our target of certainty rating. We did not rate down for imprecision, 
as our target of certainty rating were defined by the 95% CrIs of summary estimates.  
3.3.11 Methods for selecting, appraising, and synthesizing evidence about impact 
on patient-important outcomes 
We included randomized controlled trials that assessed the effect of test strategies on the following 
patient-important outcomes: cure, mortality, loss-to-follow-up, time to diagnosis, and time to treatment 
initiation. Trials needed to include participants as described under section 3.1.2 and allocated 
participants to one of the test strategies described in Table 1. In eligible trials, participants' subsequent 
management decisions should be influenced by the results of these tests. We assessed risk of bias in 
randomized trials using the RoB 2 tool [Sterne 2019] with the aim of estimating the effect of assignment 
to intervention. Trial selection, data extraction, and risk-of-bias assessment were performed in 
duplicate. Trial results were summarized as relative risk (for binary outcomes) or mean difference (for 
continuous outcomes) with 95% confidence intervals. If at least three studies were available for a given 
analysis, we used frequentist DerSimonian-Laird random-effects meta-analysis to combine trial results. 
We assessed the certainty of evidence using the GRADE approach for therapeutic evidence [Guyatt 
2011].
