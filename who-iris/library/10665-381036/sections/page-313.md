5.5 Strengths and weaknesses of the review  
This review represents the first estimations of incremental accuracy using parallel- compared to 
single-test strategies in children, based on comprehensive searching, strict inclusion, and 
standardized data extraction. For both review questions, sample sizes were large and comparative 
accuracy study analyses were based on a number of studies. We communicated extensively with the 
authors of the primary studies and were able to retrieve comprehensive data.  
However, the results presented here need to be interpreted with caution, as this review also has 
several weaknesses. Firstly, we re-analysed data provided by the study authors directly, none of 
which had been published or underwent peer review. Furthermore, of the 36 eligible studies 
identified by our search, less than half (i.e., 14) contributed data to this analysis. It remains unclear if 
these studies yielded similar diagnostic accuracy estimates and how these would contribute to the 
pooled estimates presented in this review. Additionally, this review focused on pulmonary 
tuberculosis, not all forms of the disease, where adding an assay like LF-LAM might have a more 
pronounced effect.  
While this is not a specific weakness of this review, diagnostic accuracy estimations, including those 
presented in this review, are hampered by definition as childhood tuberculosis diagnosis remains 
difficult to ascertain. In this review, we defined two reference standards to reflect different degrees 
of certainty. The MRS was carefully harmonized and based on the current gold standard, being 
culture, to not include any of the index tests. While specificity is considered close to 100%, 
sensitivity ranges from 7-40% [DiNardo 2016; Thomas 2019; Mark P Nicol 2020; Zar 2019]. Thus, 
estimations based on MRS might underestimate sensitivity and bias towards more severe 
tuberculosis disease. Additionally, low specificity might be related to false-positives or false-
negatives, if index test identifies true tuberculosis cases that are culture negative. In this review, For 
the primary analysis with the MRS, there was an overall low risk of bias. While the studies may have 
originally defined microbiologically confirmed tuberculosis based on both LC-aNAAT and culture, 
authors in the included studies provided disaggregated data to support a culture-based reference 
standard. Clinically, most childhood tuberculosis diagnoses are made primarily on clinical grounds 
and therefore the use of a CRS remains crucial in diagnostic accuracy evaluations, despite reflecting 
less degree of certainty. Due to the non-specificity of the clinical presentation of childhood 
tuberculosis CRS potentially overdiagnoses disease while simultaneously identifying a number of 
children with true disease. In our review, following similar published analyses [Kohli 2021; A.W. Kay 
2022; Shah 2016b], we followed the definition of the respective primary study, thus resulting in 
varying definitions potentially causing heterogeneity in results. In addition, most studies included 
the index tests in their CRS definition. As reflected in the QUADAS-C reference standard domain, the 
CRS had a high risk of bias as most studies incorporated index test results in the clinical diagnosis of 
TB. The consequence may be overestimates of the individual test accuracy, and thus smaller 
differences when comparing to parallel testing.  
Lastly, while the primary outcomes of this systematic review focused on diagnostic accuracy, we 
carried out efforts to identify data on impact; however, we did not identify any study.  
5.6 Parallel testing - differences to previous systematic reviews  
Systematic reviews and meta-analyses typically assess tuberculosis diagnostic accuracy by individual 
tests or may compare diagnostic accuracy review of two or more test typically when a new version 
of a test has emerged [Penn-Nicholson 2021; Zifodya 2021]. There was one Cochrane review on the 
parallel use of LC-aNAAT on sputum and LF-LAM in adults living with HIV reporting a pooled 
sensitivity of 75%, representing a difference of 13% compared to LC-aNAAT alone, and a pooled 
specificity of 93%, representing a 4% decrease from LC-aNAAT alone [Shah 2016a]. For children, no 
similar systematic reviews have been published.
