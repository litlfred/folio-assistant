Why it was important to do this review 
A Cochrane Review assessed the impact of diagnostic strategies using Xpert MTB/RIF 
compared to strategies using smear microscopy on people-important outcomes (21). The 
evidence from the review informed the 2021 update of the WHO policy guideline on tests for 
TB detection (WHO consolidated guidelines on tuberculosis: module 3: diagnosis: rapid 
diagnostics for tuberculosis detection). 
 
Xpert MTB/RIF has been superseded by Xpert Ultra and other rapid NAATs have become 
available. Therefore, in preparation for a Guideline Development Group (GDG) meeting 
“Nucleic acid amplification tests to detect TB and DR-TB” scheduled for 6-10 May 2024, there 
was a need to summarize currently available evidence of the impact on patient outcomes of 
the following classes/modes of use of NAATs: 
1. Low complexity automated NAATs (LC-aNAATs) for detection of TB and drug 
resistance; 
2. Low complexity manual (LC-mNAATs) NAATs for the detection of TB; 
3. Moderate complexity automated NAATs (MC-aNAATs) for detection of pre-XDR-
TB/XDR-TB. 
 
We anticipated a paucity of data for the 2024 GDG meeting, therefore, only one systematic 
review was conducted across the classes and populations. This review assessed whether the 
use of these tests impacts people-important outcomes, such as all-cause mortality, 
proportion of LTFU, cure, time to diagnosis and treatment initiation.
