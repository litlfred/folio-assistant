Detection of pulmonary TB 
Should LC-aNAATs on respiratory samples be used to diagnose pulmonary TB 
in adults and adolescents with signs and symptoms or screened positive for 
pulmonary TB, against a microbiological reference standard? 
 
Thirty-five studies (14845 participants) assessed diagnostic accuracy using sputum specimens but one 
study had no TB cases (Zar 2019) and so sensitivity was not estimable. The sensitivities in the 34 studies 
(14840 participants) included in the meta-analysis were between 54% and 100% and the specificities 
were between 71% and 100% (Figure 6). The summary sensitivity (95% CI) was 90.4% (88.0 to 92.4) and 
the summary specificity was 94.9 % (93.0 to 96.3). The certainty of evidence for both sensitivity and 
specificity were graded as “High”. 
 
Based on the primary analysis, for a population of 1000 adults and adolescents where 100 have 
pulmonary tuberculosis on culture, 136 would be LC-aNAAT-positive and 46 (34%) would not have 
tuberculosis (false-positives); 864 would be LC-aNAAT-negative and 10 (1%) would have tuberculosis 
(false-negatives).  
Several studies demonstrated lower than expected sensitivity or specificity. 
Description of studies with lower sensitivity: 
Aguilera-Alonso 2022 - Accuracy estimates are based on the result from the first Xpert Ultra test, 
whereas the reference test used up to 3 samples (solid and liquid culture) to determine TB status. There 
were only 3 adolescents with microbiologically diagnosed TB (sensitivity 67%). Overall Xpert Ultra 
sensitivity for all participants in study was 81.0% based on MRS. 
 
Chien 2020: This was a study of bronchoscopic diagnosis in participants with smear-negative sputum 
who were suspected of having TB. 94% of the BAL specimens were smear-negative among persons who 
had MRS diagnosis of TB. Culture used both solid and liquid media.
