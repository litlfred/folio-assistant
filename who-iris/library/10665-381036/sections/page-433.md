In conclusion, the collective findings suggest that TB-LAMP holds promise as a cost-effective and efficient 
diagnostic tool for tuberculosis when integrated into broader diagnostic algorithms, particularly in 
resource-constrained settings. The results of this study have important implications for policymakers and 
healthcare professionals involved in the diagnosis and management of tuberculosis. 
 
MC-aNAAT discussion: 
This systematic review is the first of its kind to investigate the economic aspects of using a new technology 
called MC-aNAATS for diagnosing drug-resistant tuberculosis (DR-TB). This finding indicates the limited 
availability of relevant studies addressing the cost of automated NAATs for TB diagnosis in resource-
limited settings. Out of 108 records identified, only one study met the inclusion criteria, which examined 
the costs associated with both low-throughput (LT) and high-throughput (HT) NAAT systems.  
 
The study by Malhotra et al. estimated costs for both LT and HT NAAT systems in a hypothetical laboratory 
setting in a resource-limited low- or middle-income country (LMIC) (85). Per-test costs were higher for LT 
NAAT ($22.48) compared to HT NAAT ($18.66), primarily due to differences in test kit costs. Sensitivity 
analysis revealed factors such as testing frequency, equipment price, durability, and TB-specific workload 
significantly influenced per-test costs. HT NAAT became more cost-effective beyond an annual testing 
volume of 8,000. However, if LT NAAT equipment costs were substantially lower, LT NAAT could be 
cheaper, especially at lower testing volumes.  
 
The findings underscore the importance of considering disease burden, health infrastructure, and other 
contextual factors in placement decisions. Factors such as testing frequency, equipment costs, and TB 
workload should be carefully considered to optimize cost-effectiveness and diagnostic efficiency. While 
HT NAATs offer advantages at higher testing volumes, LT NAATs could be viable options under specific 
cost-saving scenarios. As this study is done in hypothetical setting future research should validate these 
findings across diverse LMIC contexts and explore long-term cost-effectiveness, ensuring successful 
integration and improved diagnosis rates for drug-susceptible and drug-resistant tuberculosis. The fact 
that we found only one study underscores the urgency for more research in this area to guide healthcare 
decisions better.
