4.5. PICO 2: What is the incremental diagnostic accuracy of parallel use of LC-
aNAATs on a respiratory sample and stool and LF-LAM on urine for diagnosis of 
pulmonary tuberculosis disease in children with HIV with signs and symptoms 
or screened positive for pulmonary tuberculosis, compared to any of the tests 
(either LC-aNAATs combined or LF-LAM) alone? 
4.5.1 Comparative diagnostic accuracy against a microbiological reference 
standard  
Description of included studies 
We identified six studies that compared a parallel strategy of LC-aNAAT on respiratory specimen plus LC-
aNAAT on stool (LC-aNAATs combined) and LF-LAM versus LC-aNAATs combined alone against a MRS 
involved a total of 653 participants, 43 (6.6%) of which had tuberculosis disease. 
For one study, the reference standard was exclusively based on one single respiratory sample 
[Chipinduro 2017], while the other five studies performed at least two cultures on respiratory specimens 
[Hanrahan 2018; Olbrich 2024; Orikiriza 2021; Jaganath 2024; LaCourse 2018]. 
Two of the studies (33.3%) were conducted exclusively in an inpatient [LaCourse 2018; Orikiriza 2021]), 
two of studies (33.3%) exclusively in an outpatient [Hanrahan 2018; Chipinduro 2017], and two of 
studies (33.3%) in both settings [Olbrich 2024; Jaganath 2024]. See Appendix 8. Characteristics of 
included studies. 
Diagnostic accuracy of parallel use of LC-aNAAT combined or urine LF-LAM based 
on the microbiological reference standard  
The sensitivity of the parallel use of LC-aNAATs combined and LF-LAM ranged from 64.3 [Olbrich 2024] 
to 88.9% [Chipinduro 2017] and the specificity from 63.2% [Jaganath 2024] to 92.4% [LaCourse 2018] 
between studies. See Figure 15.  The pooled sensitivity and specificity (95% CrI) of the parallel use of LC-
aNAATs combined and LF-LAM were 77.8% (59.9 to 89.8) and 83.9% (73.9 to 90.4).
