Concerns for access and equity  
 
Summary  
Findings about Xpert MTB/RIF demonstrated concerns about sustainable funding, restrictions by donors, 
maintenance of the testing platforms, poor referral systems and dependence on outreach workers 
limiting access. Also protracted diagnostic delays, test underutilization, unavailability of community TB 
diagnostic facilities and too many eligibility restrictions limited access to testing. 
Similar to Xpert MTB/RIF, the reported concerns about equity for Xpert Ultra were its limited availability 
in health facilities as well as high costs incurred by patients and health facilities for its utility.  
 
Detailed review findings for Xpert Ultra 
Summary of Qualitative Findings table: Findings 8-9, Annex 6 
 
Finding 8: Staff and managers voiced concerns regarding high costs, sustainability of funding and 
maintenance which negatively affects creating equitable access to automated NAATs of low 
complexity (High confidence; Sereda 202231).  
Hospital managers belabored the high costs of using Xpert and its limitations on access.  
“I just want to have a clear official document regulating who should be tested. GeneXpert is not cheap. 
There are financial constraints” (Sereda 2022) 
Finding 9: Lack of Xpert testing in surrounding health facilities hamper referrals and access to prompt 
and accurate testing and treatment particularly for vulnerable groups (High confidence; Sereda 
202231). 
The lack of testing, infrastructure and limited logistics in the hospital and surrounding health facilities 
limited referrals and access. 
“Not all hospitals have GeneXpert” (Sereda 2022) 
 
Discussion 
This review aimed to synthesize qualitative research on end user and provider perspectives on low and 
moderate complexity NAATs but only identified studies on low complexity automated NAATs. Only two 
studies about newer NAATs (Xpert Ultra)30 31 were identified and 47 about Xpert MTB/RIF or rapid NAATs. 
All included studies were about low complexity automated NAATs for detection of TB and DR-TB and 
conducted in high TB burden settings in Africa, Asia and Eastern Europe. The studies presented 
perspectives on single testing and did not include perspectives on parallel testing for NAATs.
