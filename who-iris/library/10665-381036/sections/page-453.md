Concerns for access and equity 
Findings about Xpert MTB/RIF demonstrated concerns about sustainable funding, restrictions by donors, 
maintenance of the testing platforms, poor referral systems and dependence on outreach workers limiting 
access. Also protracted diagnostic delays, test underutilization, unavailability of community TB diagnostic 
facilities and too many eligibility restrictions limited access to testing. 
Similar to Xpert MTB/RIF, the reported concerns about equity for Xpert Ultra were its limited availability 
in health facilities, as well as high costs incurred by patients and health facilities for its utility.  
 
Conclusion 
Although standard Xpert MTB/RIF has been superseded by Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra and other rapid NAATs, 
qualitative evidence for the latter NAATs is limited.  While low complexity NAATs in general are valued for 
their accuracy, ease of use and potential to reduce time to diagnosis, latter NAATs such as Xpert Ultra are 
valued for their higher accuracy in hard to diagnose patients, ease of implementation on existing 
GeneXpert platforms and their ease in integrating with rapid testing for other diseases. We had high 
confidence in the evidence contributing to review findings. Challenges limiting the realization of these 
values for latter NAATs are similar to standard Xpert MTB/RIF and comprise weak infrastructure, 
fragmented systems, heavy workload and limited availability of NAATs and their supplies. We recommend 
the conduct of qualitative studies about the perspectives of parallel testing of NAATs.
