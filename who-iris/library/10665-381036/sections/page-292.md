Forest plots of individual test accuracy for LC-aNAATs on respiratory sample and stool, CRS (PICO 1) 
In CHIV, the sensitivity of LC-aNAAT on respiratory samples alone ranged from 0.0% ([Hanrahan 2018]) 
to 100.0% [Donkeng 2022] between studies, and specificity was 100.0% for all studies except for two: 
79.0% [Jaganath 2024] and 99.0% [Chipinduro 2017]. The pooled sensitivity of LC-aNAAT on respiratory 
samples was 14.6% (95% CrI 6.8 to 29.2) and the specificity 98.3% (95% CrI 94.9 to 99.6). The sensitivity 
of LC-aNAAT on stool alone ranged from 0.0% [Hanrahan 2018] to 100.0% [Donkeng 2022] and 
specificity from 88.0% [Olbrich 2024] to 100% [Hanrahan 2018; Orikiriza 2021; LaCourse 2018] between 
studies. The pooled sensitivity of LC-aNAAT on stool was 12.6% (95% CrI 5.8 to 26.7) and the specificity 
96.8% (95% CrI 93.1 to 98.7; seven studies; 718 participants, 287 [40.0%] with tuberculosis). 
Incremental accuracy of the parallel use of LC-aNAATs combined versus LC-
aNAAT on respiratory samples alone based on the composite reference standard 
In children without HIV or HIV status unknown, incremental difference in sensitivity for parallel use of 
LC-aNAATs versus LC-aNAAT on respiratory samples alone ranged from 0.0% [Ainan 2021; Hanrahan 
2018] to 36.0% [Kabir 2021] between studies. The difference in specificity ranged from 0% [Ainan 2021; 
Chabala 2024; Donkeng 2022; Hanrahan 2018; Kabir 2021; Orikiriza 2021; Wobudeya 2024]) to -6.0% 
[M. P. Nicol 2013; Olbrich 2024]. See Figure 13. The pooled difference in accuracy of the parallel use of 
LC-aNAATs versus LC-aNAAT on respiratory samples alone was 4.7% (95% CrI 2.1 to 8.9; PI 0.5 to 30.4) 
for sensitivity and −0.5% (95% CrI -1.4 to -0.1; PI -10.6 to 0) for specificity. See Figure 9.
