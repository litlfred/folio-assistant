The overall aim of the present review is to identify, evaluate, and summarize the findings of available 
economic evidence on three classes of NAATs: 
• Low complexity automated nucleic acid amplification tests (LC-aNAATs) for TB 
diagnosis and detection of drug resistance.   
• Low-complexity manual NAAT (LC-mNAAT) TB-LAMP for TB diagnosis. 
• Moderate complexity automated NAATs (MC-aNAATs).  
 
2. 
METHODS 
 
This systematic review protocol has been developed according to the recommendations from the 
Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) 2015 statement 
and will be reported following the PRISMA 2020 guidelines for conducting and reporting systematic 
reviews (15,16). We seek to understand the costs, cost-effectiveness, and affordability of the three 
diagnostic classes of NAATs mentioned above. 
 
2.1. Study Selection:  
Our search strategies were developed to capture any form of economic study or evaluation. The search 
was not limited by study design or epidemiologic or economic outcome. Examples of study types include 
economic analyses, cost analyses, cost-effectiveness analyses, and cost-benefit analyses.  
 
In the case of LC-aNAATs, this review builds upon a previous systematic review done in 2019 to inform 
the WHO guideline group meeting and accompanying policy on using LC-NAATs.  Twenty-eight studies 
were included in that review, with no studies identified assessing the cost-effectiveness of Xpert Ultra and 
only one study evaluating the cost-effectiveness of TrueNat (11). Since that review was completed, there 
has been a proliferation of economic studies assessing LC-aNAATs. Therefore, this review will focus on 
studies published since the 2019 review. Given the key questions of interest for the upcoming GDG on 
NAATs, we will include only studies where LC-aNAATs are used as the initial test to diagnose active TB in 
individuals with signs and symptoms of TB. Consequently, we will exclude studies assessing active case-
finding strategies using the older Xpert assay and conducted outside healthcare facilities, including but 
not limited to general population screening, household or social contact tracing, door-to-door screening, 
and mass screening in prisons. Studies assessing triage approaches prior to diagnostic testing (i.e., in the 
absence of symptoms of TB) will also be excluded.
