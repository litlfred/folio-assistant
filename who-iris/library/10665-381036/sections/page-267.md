study authors on request were the final data. We included data provided by manufacturers and 
investigators in response to a public call for data by the WHO Global Tuberculosis Programme.

3.1.3. Index tests 
The principal focus of this review is in estimating the diagnostic accuracy of the combined (parallel) use 
of diagnostic tests for pulmonary tuberculosis in children. Here, we defined parallel use as tests 
conducted simultaneously on the same person, and a positive result on either test implies a positive 
result for the combination. The index tests include 1) the parallel use of respiratory samples and stool 
LC-aNAAT for children who are HIV negative or with unknown HIV status, and 2) the parallel use of 
urinary LF-LAM and LC-aNAAT (on both respiratory and stool samples) among CHIV. In clinical practice, 
urinary LF-LAM may be performed first, and the LF-LAM result is known to those interpreting the LC-
aNAAT. Other index tests are urinary LF-LAM alone. 
LC-aNAAT platforms included were Xpert MTB/RIF and Xpert Ultra, Truenat MTB, MTB Plus, STANDARD 
M10, and IRON-qPCR. LC-aNAATs were performed on respiratory samples (i.e.,  sputum, induced 
sputum, tracheal aspiration, fluid from bronchial alveolar, and gastric lavage). For LC-aNAAT performed 
on stool, any published stool processing protocol was considered. However, if multiple stool processing 
methods were used, we prioritized SOS over SPK and OSF as this is the approach currently in clinical use.
