4.4. PICO 1: What is the incremental diagnostic accuracy of parallel use of LC-
aNAATs on a respiratory sample and stool for diagnosis of pulmonary 
tuberculosis disease in children that are HIV negative or unknown HIV status 
with signs and symptoms or screened positive for pulmonary tuberculosis, 
compared to use of the LC-aNAAT on one sample type (either respiratory or 
stool)?   
4.4.1 Comparative diagnostic accuracy against a microbiological reference 
standard  
Summary of Findings Tables for the incremental diagnostic accuracy of the parallel use of LC-aNAATs 
combined versus each LC-aNAAT alone using the MRS can be found in Appendix 9. 
Description of included studies 
We identified eight studies that compared the accuracy of parallel use of respiratory and stool LC-
aNAATs (LC-aNAATs combined) versus LC-aNAAT on one sample type (either respiratory of stool against 
a MRS, involving 2,145 participants without HIV, 173 (8.1%) of which had tuberculosis disease, and 
seven studies involving 697 participants with HIV, 44 (6.3%) of which had tuberculosis disease. 
For children without HIV and MRS, one study [Hasan 2017] performed culture exclusively on a single 
respiratory sample (12.5%), while five studies [Hanrahan 2018; M. P. Nicol 2013; Olbrich 2024; Orikiriza 
2021; Jaganath 2024] used at least two respiratory samples (62.5%); for two studies ([Chabala 2024; 
Donkeng 2022] it was unclear how many cultures were performed (16.7%). Two of studies (25.0%) were 
conducted exclusively in an inpatient setting [Chabala 2024; Orikiriza 2021], two studies (25.0%) 
exclusively in an outpatient setting [Hasan 2017; Hanrahan 2018], and four of studies (50.0%) in both 
settings [Donkeng 2022; M. P. Nicol 2013; Olbrich 2024; Jaganath 2024]. See Appendix 8: Characteristics 
of included studies. 
Diagnostic accuracy of parallel use of LC-aNAAT combined based on the 
microbiological reference standard  
In children without HIV or HIV status unknown, the sensitivity of the parallel use of LC-aNAATs combined 
ranged from 60.0% [Chabala 2024] to 100% [Hanrahan 2018; Hasan 2017], and specificity from 76.0% 
[Jaganath 2024] to 100% [Donkeng 2022]. See Figure 7. The pooled sensitivity and specificity of the 
parallel use of LC-aNAATs were 79.9% (95% CrI 67.8 to 89.8) and 93.4% (95% CrI 87.2 to 97.0), (eight 
studies; 2,145 participants, 173 [8.1%] with tuberculosis). 
For CHIV, the pooled sensitivity and specificity of the parallel use of LC-aNAATs were 70.2% (95% CrI 
51.1 to 84.7) and 95.4% (95% CrI 91.7 to 97.8), (seven studies; 697 participants, 44 [6.3%] with 
tuberculosis).
