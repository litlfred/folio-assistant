Figure 5. Risk of bias and applicability for each study: pulmonary TB detection in 
PLHIV 
 
The sensitivities in the eight studies (2991 participants) included in the meta-analysis ranged 
between 52% and 100% and the specificities between 27% and 100% (Figure 6). The summary 
sensitivity (95% CI) was 77.1% (60.8 to 87.9) and the summary specificity was 95.9% (84.9 to 99.0); 
low certainty (sensitivity) and moderate certainty (specificity) evidence. 
 
For a population of 1000 adults and adolescents PLHIV where 100 have pulmonary TB on culture, 114 
would be LC-mNAAT-positive and 37 (32%) would not have TB (false-positives); 886 would be LC-
mNAAT-negative and 23 (3%) would have TB (false-negatives). 
 
 
Figure 6. Forest plot of LC-mNAATs for detection of pulmonary TB: PLHIV and 
respiratory samples  
Studies are sorted on the plot by sensitivity, specificity and study. FN: false negative; FP: false positive; TN: true 
negative; TP: true positive. 
 
Should LC-mNAATs on respiratory samples be used to diagnose pulmonary TB in 
PLHIV with CD4 count <200, with signs and symptoms of pulmonary TB, against a 
microbiological reference standard? 
For PLHIV, one study (Spooner 2022) with overall low risk of bias and low applicability concern 
(Figure 5) reported CD4 count. Of the 690 participants with CD4 count data, 235 (34%) had CD4 <200 
(Figure 7). The sensitivity (95% CI) was 60.9% (38.5 to 80.3) and the specificity (95% CI) was 96.2% 
(92.7 to 98.4) ;  very low certainty (sensitivity) and moderate certainty (specificity) evidence.
