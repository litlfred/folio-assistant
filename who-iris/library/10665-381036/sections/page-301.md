Diagnostic accuracy of the component tests: LC-aNAATs combined or LF-LAM 
based on the composite reference standard  
The sensitivity of LC-aNAATs combined alone ranged from 0.0% [Hanrahan 2018] to 54.2% [Chipinduro 
2017] and specificity from 78.9% [Jaganath 2024] to 100% [Hanrahan 2018; LaCourse 2018; Orikiriza 
2021] between studies. Pooled sensitivity and specificity for LC-aNAATs combined alone were 14.8% 
(95% CrI 6.8 to 27.8) and 95.5% (95% CrI 88.6 to 98.4), (six studies, 653 participants, 43 [6.6%] of which 
had tuberculosis). 
The sensitivity of LF-LAM alone ranged from 3.0% ([Olbrich 2024]) to 54.2% [Chipinduro 2017] and 
specificity from 25.0% [Hanrahan 2018] to 95.3% [Olbrich 2024]. Pooled sensitivity was 17.7% (95% CrI 
7.6 to 35.4) and specificity 88.7% (95% CrI 76.4 to 94.1), (six studies, 653 participants, 43 [6.6%] of which 
had tuberculosis). See Appendix 21: Point estimates by study for individual index tests in CHIV, CRS 
(PICO 2). 
Incremental diagnostic accuracy of LC-aNAATs combined and LF-LAM versus LC-
aNAATs combined alone based on the composite reference standard 
Incremental difference in sensitivity for parallel use of LC-aNAATs combined and LF-LAM ranged from 
0% [Olbrich 2024] to 33.3% [Chipinduro 2017] between studies. The difference in specificity ranged from 
-0.5% [Jaganath 2024; Olbrich 2024] to -75.0% [Hanrahan 2018]. See Figure 19. 
The pooled difference in accuracy of the parallel use of LC-aNAATs combined and LF-LAM versus LC-
aNAATs combined alone was 11.5% (95% CrI 3.8 to 26.7; PI 0.7 to 65.5) for sensitivity and −10.1% (95% 
CrI −21.6 to −4.9; PI -44.7 to 10.3) for specificity. See Figure 17.
