4.5.2 Comparative diagnostic accuracy against a composite reference standard 
Description of included studies 
We identified six studies that compared the accuracy of parallel use of LC-aNAAT on respiratory samples 
plus LC-aNAAT on stool (LC-aNAATs combined) and LF-LAM versus LC-aNAATs combined alone against a 
CRS involving 674 participants, 286 (42.4%) with tuberculosis disease.  
The tuberculosis prevalence as confirmed by a CRS ranged from 10.9% [Chipinduro 2017] to 80.9% 
[Hanrahan 2018]. The definition of the CRS differed between studies and was based on different 
combinations of microbiological tests, radiographic findings, clinical assessment, or decision to initiate 
tuberculosis treatment. LC-aNAAT and/or LF-LAM results were part of the reference standard in five 
studies (83.3%). See Appendix 8. Characteristics of included studies. 
Diagnostic accuracy of parallel use of LC-aNAATs combined and LF-LAM on urine 
based on the composite reference standard  
The sensitivity of the parallel use of LC-aNAATs combined and LF-LAM ranged from 5.9% [Hanrahan 
2018] to 87.5% [Chipinduro 2017] and the specificity ranged from 25.0% [Hanrahan 2018] to 93.1% 
[LaCourse 2018]. See Figure 18. The pooled sensitivity was 30.1% (95% CrI 13.2 to 54.9) and specificity 
83.3% (95% CrI 69.6 to 90.2), (six studies, 653 participants, 43 [6.6%] of which had tuberculosis).
