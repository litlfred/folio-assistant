There was no evidence that the difference in sensitivity among participants with CD4 ≤ 200 cells per µL 
exceeds that of participants with CD4 > 200 cells per µL (probability that between-subgroup difference > 
0 was 52%). Regarding difference in specificity, there was also no evidence of between-subgroup 
difference (probability that between-subgroup difference < 0 was 51%).  
Comparative accuracy stratified by inpatient versus outpatient  
We identified 10 studies that reported data for inpatients involving 3,951 participants, 641 (16%) with 
tuberculosis. Among inpatients, the pooled sensitivity of parallel testing was 77.7% (95% CrI 68.5 to 
85.9) and specificity 87.2% (95%CrI 81.3 to 91.6), respectively (Table 11). The pooled difference in 
sensitivity and pooled difference in specificity between parallel testing and LC-aNAAT was 11.7% (95% 
CrI 4.6 to 23.0; 95% PI 0.9 to 61.5) and -9.5% (95% CrI -14.5 to -5.9; 95% PI -32.3 to -2.2).  
Eleven studies reported data for outpatients, involving 5,892 participants, 752 (13%) with tuberculosis. 
Among outpatients, the pooled sensitivity of parallel testing was 76.0% (95% CrI 69.8 to 81.7) and 
specificity 92.0% (95% CrI 86.2 to 95.4%) respectively (Table 11). The pooled difference in sensitivity and 
pooled difference in specificity between parallel testing and LC-aNAAT was 3.6% (95% CrI 1.9 to 5.8; 95% 
PI 0.8 to 13.0) and -4.4% (95% CrI -8.2 to -2.3; 95% PI -29.3 to -0.5).
