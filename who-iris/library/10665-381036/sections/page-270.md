3.3 Data collection and analysis 
3.3.1 Selection of studies 
We uploaded titles and abstracts retrieved from the electronic literature search to EndNote to remove 
duplicates. Subsequently, we uploaded them to Covidence for study selection [Veritas Health Innovation 
2023]. Two review authors independently screened all titles and abstracts to determine potentially 
eligible studies. The same review authors independently assessed full-text articles. We resolved 
disagreements through discussion and, if necessary, involved a third review author as arbiter. We listed 
reasons for exclusion. Review authors who were involved in any of the identified studies did not 
participate in assessment of eligibility of their own study. We illustrated the study selection process in a 
PRISMA diagram.   
3.3.2 Data extraction and management 
Using a pre‐developed data extraction form (Appendix 5), we extracted the following characteristics: 
1. Study Characteristics: first author; publication year; country where the study was performed; 
economic classification of the country according to the World Bank [World Bank 2022], study 
setting (outpatient or inpatient setting in peripheral health institutions, peripheral or 
intermediate laboratories); study design; method of participant allocation; total number of 
children enrolled, excluded, and included for data analysis; study funding.  
2. Study participants: age, sex, symptom status, chest x-ray classification, HIV status, CD4 cell 
count/percentage, WHO HIV clinical stage, serious illness status 
3. Target condition: pulmonary tuberculosis  
4. Reference standards: number of cultures performed on respiratory specimens for each 
participant, either culture results alone or CRS. Solid culture or automated liquid culture, or both.  
5. Index tests: Details of the conduct of each assay, and the positivity threshold 
6. Specimen type: number of specimens collected (one, multiple, unknown, or unclear); specimen 
type (expectorated sputum, induced sputum, nasopharyngeal aspirate, gastric aspirate, broncho‐
alveolar aspirate, stool, urine); and specimen condition (frozen, freshly collected, or both). 
7. Study results: two-by-two table data (true positives, false negatives, false positives, and true 
negatives).  
8. Indeterminate and non‐determinate results: number of indeterminate or invalid index test 
results in accordance with manufacturer’s recommendations and number of non‐determinate 
results, if applicable.  
9. Participant health outcomes: cure; mortality; loss-to-follow-up; time to diagnosis; time to 
treatment initiation.  
For studies where we could not extract these data directly from the publications, we reached out to 
study authors for data. Data were reported back in the format of three-by-three-by-two tables designed 
for the purpose of our review. We also contacted study authors for missing data and clarifications. See 
Appendix 6: Data reporting form for primary study authors. Some study authors shared individual-
participant data (IPD).
