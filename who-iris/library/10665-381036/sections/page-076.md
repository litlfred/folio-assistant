SUMMARY OF MAIN RESULTS 
  
● For sputum, sensitivity was 75.3% (95% CI 68.9 to 80.8) and specificity was 95.9% (95% CI 
92.3 to 97.9) in children 0 to 9 years, against MRS. 
● For sputum, sensitivity was 70.4% (95% CI 56.0 to 81.7) and specificity was 95.6%  (95% CI 
89.9 to 98.2) in children 0 to 9 years living with HIV, against MRS. 
● For gastric aspirate, sensitivity was 69.6% (95% CI 60.3 to 77.6) and specificity was 91.0% 
(95% CI 82.5 to 95.6) in children 0 to 9 years, against MRS.  
● For nasopharyngeal aspirate, sensitivity was 46.2% (95% CI 34.9 to 57.9) and specificity was 
97.5% (95% CI 95.1 to 98.7) in children 0 to 9 years, against MRS.  
● For stool, sensitivity was 68.0% (50.3 to 81.7) and specificity was 98.1% (95% CI 96.3 to 
99.1)  in children 0 to 9 years, against MRS.  
● Sensitivity estimates against a CRS were lower for all specimen types, ranging from 12.7% 
for nasopharyngeal aspirate to 48.8% for gastric aspirate specimens. 
● Three studies (76 participants) estimated accuracy for rifampicin resistance using a mix of 
specimen types and the sensitivities were all 100% (only two cases in total) and specificities 
all 100%. 
● Four studies assessed extrapulmonary tuberculosis. The largest population had CSF tested by 
Xpert Ultra (215 participants) with sensitivities of 67-100% and specificities of 91-100% 
against MRS. Due to the small sample size, we were unable to conclude the diagnostic 
accuracy of Xpert Ultra for extrapulmonary tuberculosis in children.
