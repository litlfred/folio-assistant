Searching other resources 
We examined the reference lists of included articles and relevant review articles identified 
through the electronic searches. VL also searched for relevant dissertations in ProQuest 
Dissertations & Theses A&I.  We searched for ongoing and unpublished studies from 
manufacturers and researchers through a WHO public call; and from experts working on new 
diagnostics for TB such as STOP TB Partnership's New Diagnostic Working Group and FIND 
(the global alliance for diagnostics). 
 
Selection criteria 
We used Covidence, a systematic review management software, to select studies (26). Two 
review authors (JO and LW) independently screened study titles and abstracts for eligibility. 
Any disagreements were resolved by discussion between the reviewers or by consulting a 
senior review author (EO). Afterwards, we retrieved the full‐text articles and two review 
authors (JO and LW) independently assessed their eligibility. Any disagreements were 
resolved similarly to the first screening stage. We contacted study authors for clarification of 
methods and other information as needed. None of the eligible studies was authored by any 
of the review authors.  
 
Data collection  
Reviewers worked with the statisticians, (YT and KS), to develop a standardised, pre-designed 
data extraction form, to be used in Covidence (26). Two review authors (JO & LW) 
independently piloted the form on one eligible study. We modified the form based on the 
outcomes of the piloting process after which, all review authors approved its usage. Two 
review authors (JO and LW) independently extracted data and discussed inconsistencies to 
achieve consensus. A senior review author (EO) was consulted where necessary.
