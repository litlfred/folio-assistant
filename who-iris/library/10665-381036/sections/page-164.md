Executive Summary 
 
Background 
WHO recommends Rapid nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs) as the initial diagnostic test 
for tuberculosis (TB), some of which can detect drug resistance simultaneously. They have 
higher diagnostic accuracy than smear microscopy and have fast turnaround times that may 
lead to a positive impact on patient outcomes. 
 
Objective 
To assess the impact of low complexity (LC) and moderate complexity (MC) NAATs compared 
to smear microscopy or culture on patient outcomes in people investigated for TB. 
 
Methods 
We searched several electronic databases from inception: CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase, 
Science Citation Index, Biosis Previews, WHO Global Index Medicus, SCOPUS, 
ClinicalTrials.gov, and the WHO Clinical Trials Registry platform. Reference lists of included 
articles and relevant reviews were also checked. Two review authors independently used 
Covidence for study selection and data extraction, resolving discrepancies through consensus 
or senior author consultations. We assessed the risk of bias using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool 
and rated the certainty of evidence with the GRADE approach. We did not conduct a meta-
analysis due to limited data but summarised the evidence narratively. 
 
Results 
The search yielded 1,068 potential studies and two studies assessing the impact of Xpert 
Ultra, a LC-automated NAAT, were included: one individually randomised and one cluster 
randomised trial. No study reported on any of our primary outcomes (all-cause mortality and 
cure). Both studies were single-country trials conducted in Africa that involved adults (18 
years and above). The adjusted geometric mean ratios for time to diagnosis and time to 
treatment (1 trial, 10,644 participants) were 0.49 (95% CI: 0.39-0.62) and 0.35 (95% CI: 0.21-
0.56), respectively. The hazard ratio for time to treatment was 3.0 (95% CI: 1.2-7.7, 584 
participants), with some concerns about the risk of bias. The certainty of evidence was 
moderate for all outcomes.
