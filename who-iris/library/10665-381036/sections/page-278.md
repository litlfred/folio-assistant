All studies for the purpose of this review used a paired diagnostic accuracy design.  
In the patient selection domain, we judged eleven studies (91.7%) for studies in children without HIV 
and five (71.4%) for CHIV to have low risk of bias [Chipinduro 2017; Jaganath 2024; LaCourse 2018; 
Olbrich 2024; Orikiriza 2021]. One study (8.3%,[Donkeng 2022]) for children without HIV and two (28.6% 
[Donkeng 2022; Hanrahan 2018]) for CHIV had unclear risk of bias, as the risk of patient selection bias 
for both index tests was judged unclear per QUADAS-2 due to the patient recruitment not being 
consecutive.  
In the index test domain, we judged all studies (100%) for children without HIV and six (85.7%, [Donkeng 
2022; Hanrahan 2018; Jaganath 2024; LaCourse 2018; Olbrich 2024; Orikiriza 2021]) to have low risk of 
bias as the risk of bias for each index test was judged low per QUADAS-2. We found it unlikely that the 
conduct and interpretation of one index test would have unwanted effects on or advantages above the 
other index tests.  
In the reference standard domain, we judged seven (87.5%) out of eight studies based on MRS for 
children without HIV to have low risk of bias. One study (12.5%) had unclear risk of bias for the 
comparison, as it was unclear if the reference standard was likely to classify the participants correctly for 
both index tests in QUADAS-2 [Hasan 2017]. For CHIV, all (100%) studies were judged at low risk of bias. 
All studies avoided incorporating LC-aNAAT as part of the reference standard.
