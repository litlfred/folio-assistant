Figure 30. Risk of bias and applicability summary for extrapulmonary tuberculosis using lymph node 
tissue and aspirates and a composite reference standard  
Two studies (58 participants) were included with sensitivities of 50% to 805 and a sensitivity of 100% 
(Figure 31). Meta-analysis was not performed due to the small number of studies and participants. 
For a population of 1,000 children where 200 have tuberculosis on CRS, 100 to 168 would be Xpert 
Ultra positive; of these, 100 to 160  would have lymph node tuberculosis (true positives) and 0 to 8 
would not have lymph node tuberculosis (false positives); 832 to 900  would be Xpert Ultra negative; 
of these 792 to 800 would not have lymph node tuberculosis (true negatives) and 40 to 100 would 
have lymph node tuberculosis (false-negatives).  
 
Figure 31. LC-aNAATs for extrapulmonary tuberculosis using LN and a composite reference 
standard. FN: false negative; FP: false positive; TN: true negative; TP: true positive. 
 
16. Should LC-aNAATs on other specimen types be used to diagnose 
extrapulmonary tuberculosis in children with signs and symptoms of 
extrapulmonary tuberculosis, against a microbiological reference 
standard? 
 
Three studies (37 participants) evaluated pleural specimens. Three studies (9 participants) evaluated 
bone and joint specimens. One study evaluated urine (1 participant). One study evaluated tissue 
biopsies (10 participants). Sensitivities ranged from 50% to 100% (most were not estimable), and 
specificities ranged from 98% to 100% (Figure 32). Meta-analysis was not performed due to the small 
number of cases and studies.
