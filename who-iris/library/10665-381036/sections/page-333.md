2. Review questions  
We addressed the following PICO (population, intervention, comparison, outcome) questions. See 
Appendix 1: PICO questions. 
1. What is the incremental diagnostic accuracy of parallel use of respiratory LC-aNAATs and urine 
LF-LAM for diagnosis of tuberculosis disease in adults and adolescents with HIV who present 
with presumptive tuberculosis, compared to any of the tests alone?  
In addition, we will estimate incremental accuracy in the following subgroups: 
• in patients with signs or symptoms of tuberculosis versus asymptomatic  
• in inpatient settings versus outpatient settings  
• in patients with CD4 ≤ 200  
• in patients screening positive for tuberculosis (e.g. chest X-ray, mWRD, or CRP)  
• in patients with advanced HIV 
• in patients seriously ill 
‘Presumptive tuberculosis’ refers to a person with signs or symptoms suggestive of tuberculosis 
disease or who has a positive or abnormal screening test for tuberculosis. For people with HIV, WHO 
recommends using tools like the WHO four-symptoms screen, chest X-ray, CRP and mWRD for 
tuberculosis screening [WHO 2021]. When mWRD is used as an option for screening, WHO highlight 
the importance of following up with a diagnostic assessment including LF-LAM and a repeat mWRD 
to prevent the potential harm of over diagnosing and overtreatment [WHO 2021]. People with HIV 
are also recommended further tuberculosis testing if: seriously ill (i.e. any of the following danger 
signs: respiratory rate ≥ 30 breaths per minute, heart rate ≥ 120 beats per minute, unable to walk 
unaided, or temperature ≥ 39°C); if with advanced HIV (i.e. defined as CD4 cell count < 200 
cells/mm3 or a WHO stage 3 or 4 condition); or with low CD4 cell count regardless of symptoms (i.e. 
CD4 ≤ 200 cells/mm3 for inpatients and CD4 ≤ 100 cells/mm3 for outpatients) [World Health 
Organization. 2021a]. 
  
2. What is the effect of parallel use of respiratory LC-aNAATs and urine LF-LAM on patient-
important outcomes in adults and adolescents with HIV who present with presumptive 
tuberculosis, compared to respiratory LC-aNAAT alone?   
We will assess the effect of parallel testing on the following patient-important outcomes: 
• Mortality  
• Cure 
• Loss-to-follow up 
• Time to tuberculosis diagnosis 
• Time to tuberculosis treatment initiation 
 
Throughout the report, we combined data for adolescents defined as age 10 years and older with 
data on adults. Data on children below 10 years of age will be reported in a separate systematic 
review [Olbrich 2024].
