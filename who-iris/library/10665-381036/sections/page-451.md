Executive Summary 
 
Background 
The WHO has introduced different classes of Nucleic Acid Amplification Tests (NAATs) depending on their 
infrastructure needs and mode of conduct. Latter NAATs are replacing standard Xpert MTB/RIF as initial 
diagnostic tests for Tuberculosis (TB) due to their high accuracy, rapid results and impact on patient 
important outcomes. Evidence on perspectives on the use, feasibility, acceptability and equity of newer 
NAATs in particular will inform an update to the WHO consolidated guidelines on TB diagnosis. 
Objectives 
To synthesize provider and end user perspectives on low complexity and moderate complexity NAATs for 
diagnosis of tuberculosis and tuberculosis drug resistance (DR-TB). 
To synthesize provider and end user perspectives on parallel use of NAATs or of different specimens on 
NAATs in children and People living with HIV (PLHIV).  
Methods 
We updated and expanded the scope of an existing Cochrane review that examined user perspectives 
views on low complexity NAATs. We searched multiple databases, from inception: Medline, Cochrane 
Library, LILACS, SCOPUS, Web of Science and WHO Library databases (Globus Index Medicus) to identify 
any study with a qualitative methodology or design related to the application of low complexity manual, 
low complexity automated and moderate complexity NAATs for TB and DR-TB among providers and end 
users. Providers were health care providers (clinicians, nurses, laboratory technicians), TB programme 
officers or managers or other staff involved in ordering, maintaining or acting on results of TB diagnostic 
tests and end users were people with presumptive or confirmed TB or DR-TB. We defined parallel testing 
as simultaneously testing samples using two different assays. Two authors conducted study selection and 
data extraction independently with disagreements resolved through consensus.  We used the Evidence 
for Policy and Practice Information and coordinating Centre tool (EPPI-Centre tool to assess the 
methodological quality of included studies. We used a thematic analysis approach for data synthesis, 
building on a previous coding framework. We used the GRADE-CERQual method to assess the certainty of 
evidence. We had high confidence in the evidence from both standard Xpert MTB/RIF and Xpert Ultra.  
Main Results 
This review includes 49 qualitative studies of which 17 were identified in the updated search. All studies 
about low complexity automated NAATs for detection of TB and DR-TB were conducted in high TB burden
