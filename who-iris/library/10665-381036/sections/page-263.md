1.2.1 Index test, LC-aNAATs 
LC-aNAATs are a subgroup of mWRD used for tuberculosis diagnosis and detection of resistance to 
Isoniazid/Rifampicin. A number of commercial LC-aNAATs are available for tuberculosis testing, 
including the platforms GeneXpert by Cepheid (Sunnyvale, California, U.S.), TrueNat (MolBio Diagnostics 
Pvt Ltd Goa), STANDARD M10 (SD Biosensor, Republic of Korea), and IRON-qPCR (BIONEER, Republic of 
Korea). Since January 2020, WHO endorses both GeneXpert and TrueNat for initial diagnostic test for 
pulmonary tuberculosis in patients of all ages and rifampicin resistance [M. P. Nicol 2011]. 
In their policy update in 2013, WHO recommended testing with GeneXpert MTB/RIF as initial diagnostic 
test in all adults and children suspected of having tuberculosis, including MDR-TB or HIV-associated 
tuberculosis [World Health Organization 2013]. The first diagnostic accuracy study using GeneXpert 
MTB/RIF in children published by Nicol et al. using bio-banked specimens estimated a sensitivity of 74% 
[M. P. Nicol 2011]. The recently developed GeneXpert MTB/RIF Ultra (hereafter referred to as “Xpert 
Ultra”) cartridge is substantially more sensitive than GeneXpert MTB/RIF and showed an increased 
sensitivity in children of more than 10%, while maintaining an acceptable specificity [Sabi 2018]. A 
recent Cochrane review of Xpert Ultra in children found a sensitivity of 75% on sputum, 70% for gastric 
aspirate, 56% for stool, and 44% for nasopharyngeal aspirate (NPA), compared to culture while 
specificity ranged from 94% to 98% [A.W. Kay 2022].  
Data on TrueNat in children are limited. A recent study compared the diagnostic accuracy of both 
TrueNat and GeneXpert MTB/RIF using gastric aspirate, induced sputum, and broncho-alveolar lavage 
samples. The cohort consisted of 612 children <18 years, sensitivity and specificity of the Truenat MTB-
RIF Dx compared with liquid culture was 59% and 88% while that of GeneXpert MTB/RIF was 56% and 
91% [Singh 2023].  
STANDARD M10 and IRON-qPCR are both validated as fully automated platforms with all-in-one 
cartridge for nucleic acid extraction and amplification with results within one hour. IRON-qPCR 
technology includes targets for M tuberculosis complex (and resistance mutations for isoniazid, 
rifampicin and fluroquinolones) to be run on sputum or on isolates (culture growth) [Bioneer]. 
STANDARD M10 requires a simple sputum pretreatment process but can include cartridges for 
simultaneous detection of M tuberculosis and non-tuberculous mycobacteria or M tuberculosis and 
drug-resistance against rifampicin and isoniazid [Biosensor]. 
Stool is considered a pulmonary specimen if tested for mycobacterial DNA. Bacteria in the lungs are 
transferred to the pharynx by normal physiologic mucociliary activity or expectorated prior to being 
swallowed into the digestive tract, allowing bacteria or their particles to be detected by NAATs 
[MacLean 2019]. Stool specimen collection is non-invasive, and while it often can’t be collected on 
demand in a health facility, its potential benefit is increasingly being recognized. Different methods have 
been described to facilitate processing of stool, requiring varying degrees of processing steps and 
infrastructure, but implementation has more recently trended toward low-complexity methods. 
The Simple One Step (SOS) stool processing method does not require any additional equipment or 
consumables [de Haas 2021]. Following inactivation of MTB using the sample reagent provided in the 
Xpert Ultra kit, sedimentation by gravity precedes aspiration of the supernatant and loading of the Xpert 
Ultra cartridge [A.W. Kay 2022]. An optimised sucrose flotation (OSF) method was also proposed that 
solely requires stool transfer supplies, a scale, sample preparation tubes, and Sheather’s solution
