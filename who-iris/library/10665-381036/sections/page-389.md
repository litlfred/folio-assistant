General characteristics of included full economic evaluation studies.  
 
 
 
Xpert cost-effectiveness studies (n=10) 
Ten cost-effectiveness analyses evaluated Xpert (19–28). These were conducted across various settings 
covering all income levels, including one study from the USA, one each from Brazil and South Africa, two 
from Thailand, and Uganda and one each from Columbia, Egypt, and Ethiopia (Table 1).  
 
Most of the studies included adult populations, with only one study including both adults and children 
(29). Three studies included HIV-infected adults (21,23,26).  With one study including both PLHIV and 
non-PLHIV (22). Two studies included only hospitalized participants, otherwise populations were a mix 
of inpatients and outpatients. Inpatients.(30,31)  
 
Three studies focused on DS TB (23,24,28), one on DR TB (22), and 5 included both DS and DR TB 
populations (19,20,25–27). 
 
One study included a selection of respiratory samples, including sputum, bronchioalveolar lavage, 
tracheal aspirate, and biopsy (20), while the remaining 9 studies examined sputum samples (19,21–28). 
In addition, one study assessed urine samples (26). In three studies, the diagnostic comparison was 
smear microscopy (24,25,28). In two studies, the comparator was smear microscopy and culture (20,27), 
and in another, it was only culture (22). One study compared Xpert only or concurrently with LAM to 
immediate empiric TB treatment or TB-LAM alone (26). In another study, Xpert was evaluated as the 
initial diagnostic test in all patients or as a secondary diagnostic for smear-negative patients compared 
to smear and culture, TB-LAMP as an initial test or TB-LAMP as a follow-on test to smear microscopy 
instead of culture in smear-negative patients (19). One study compared the GeneXpert Omni to Xpert 
(23). One study evaluated the concurrent use of Xpert and LAM, sequential Xpert followed by LAM, or 
sequential LAM followed by Xpert compared to Xpert alone and LAM alone, using culture as a reference 
standard (21).  
 
Ultra cost-effectiveness studies (n=6) 
The six Ultra studies with full economic evaluations (32–37) were conducted in a variety of countries 
across all four income groups including 2 in South Africa, one each in Brazil, Indonesia, Uganda, Ethiopia, 
Lao PDR, Nigeria, and England (38) (Table 1). 
 
Only one study included children (39), and one study included HIV-infected adults (32). Three studies 
focused on the diagnosis of drug-sensitive (DS) TB (34,35,37), and two studies focused on DS and drug-
resistant (DR) TB (32,36), while one study did not report on the type of TB disease (33). Most used 
respiratory samples (32,33,35,36), while the study in children included stool (37), and another study 
employed pleural fluid (34). 
 
The diagnostic comparators are presented in Table 1. All the studies compared Ultra as a single testing 
strategy to one or more diagnostic tests as comparators, including smear microscopy and Xpert. In 
addition, one study assessed the parallel use of Ultra with lipoarabinomannan (LAM) (32), while another 
evaluated parallel testing using Ultra and whole-genome sequencing (WGS) (36).
