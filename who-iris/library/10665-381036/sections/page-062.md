3. Should LC-aNAATs on respiratory samples be used to diagnose 
pulmonary tuberculosis in children living with HIV (CLHIV) with signs 
and symptoms or screened positive for pulmonary tuberculosis, against a 
microbiological reference standard? 
 
Figure 6. Risk of bias and applicability summary for pulmonary tuberculosis in CLHIV: sputum 
specimen and microbiological reference standard  
 
Eleven studies (445 participants) evaluating sputum against a MRS in CLHIV were included with 
sensitivities between 40% and 100% and specificities between 77% and 100% (Figure 7). Seven 
studies (400 participants) were included in the meta-analysis. The summary sensitivity (95% CI) was 
70.4% (56.0 to 81.7) and the summary specificity (95% CI) was 95.6% (89.9 to 98.2) (moderate-
certainty (sensitivity) and high-certainty (specificity) evidence). 
For a population of 1,000 CLHIV where 400 have tuberculosis on culture, 308 would be Xpert Ultra 
positive; of these, 282 would have pulmonary tuberculosis (true positives) and 26 would not have 
pulmonary tuberculosis (false positives); 692 would be Xpert Ultra negative; of these 574 would not 
have pulmonary tuberculosis (true negatives) and 118 would have pulmonary tuberculosis (false-
negatives).
