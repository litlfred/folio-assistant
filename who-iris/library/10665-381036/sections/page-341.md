We included randomized controlled trials that assessed the effect of test strategies on the following 
patient important outcomes: cure, mortality, loss-to-follow-up, time to diagnosis, and time to 
treatment initiation. Trials that included participants as described under section 3.1.2 and allocated 
participants to one of the test strategies described in Table 8, i.e. a test strategy of LC-aNAAT and LF-
LAM used in parallel versus a test strategy with LF-LAM alone, or a test strategy with LC-aNAAT 
alone. In eligible trials, participants' subsequent management decisions were required to be 
influenced by the results of these tests.  
To evaluate impact data, we developed a standardized data extraction form (see Appendix 8) and 
piloted this on two of the included trials prior to finalizing the form. Subsequently, one review 
author (RRN) extracted data from each included trial on the following characteristics using Excel to 
collect and manage study data.  
• Author, publication year, trial design, country(ies), clinical setting (outpatient or 
inpatient), number enrolled and analysed 
• Participants: age, HIV-status, CD4 cell count 
• Mode of mortality assessment, type of mortality (all-cause versus tuberculosis-
related), timing of mortality assessment 
• Mortality analysis metrics used (absolute risk reduction (ARR), (adjusted) Hazard 
Ratio (HR) or Kaplan Meier, (adjusted) odds ratio) 
• Comparator groups analysed 
• Mortality in the intervention group and control group (for randomized controlled 
trials) 
• Mortality in LF-LAM positive, mortality in LF-LAM negative 
• Mortality in LF-LAM positive patients with confirmed tuberculosis, mortality in LF-
LAM negative patients with confirmed tuberculosis 
• Mortality in LF-LAM positive patients with inconclusive or non-tuberculosis 
diagnosis, mortality in LF-LAM negative patients with inconclusive or non-
tuberculosis diagnosis 
• Mortality data stratified by CD4 count 
• Time to diagnosis, time to treatment 
• Proportion diagnosed with TB, proportion with confirmed TB diagnosis 
• Proportion treated for TB 
• Other outcomes assessed in the trial 
We assessed risk of bias in randomized trials using the Cochrane Risk-of-Bias Tool (2011) [Higgins 
2011]. Then we narratively described the effect of implementation of the parallel testing strategy 
(that is, use of LC-aNAAT and LF-LAM) versus use of LC-aNAAT alone on patient important outcomes: 
mortality, cure, loss-to-follow up, time to diagnosis, proportion diagnosed, time to treatment, and 
proportion treated. In one of the included trials, Peter 2016, the Zimbabwe study sites did not have 
Xpert MTB/RIF testing, thus data for this study was derived from the South Africa, Zambia, and 
Tanzania sites. For the outcomes, mortality, proportion diagnosed, and proportion treated, we 
performed a Mantel-Haenszel random-effects meta-analysis to estimate pooled risk ratios with 95% 
confidence intervals using Review Manager 5.
