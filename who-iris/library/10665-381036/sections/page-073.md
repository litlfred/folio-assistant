13. Should LC-aNAATs on CSF be used to diagnose tuberculous 
meningitis in children with signs and symptoms of tuberculous meningitis, 
against a composite reference standard? 
 
 
Figure 26. Risk of bias and applicability summary for extrapulmonary tuberculosis using CSF and a 
composite reference standard  
Two studies (67 participants) were included with sensitivities of 12% to 19% and all had a specificity 
of 100% (Figure 27). Meta-analysis was not performed due to the small number of studies. 
For a population of 1,000 children where 200 have tuberculosis on CRS, 24 to 46 would be Xpert 
Ultra positive; of these, 24 to 38  would have tuberculosis meningitis (true positives) and 0 to 8 would 
not have tuberculosis meningitis (false positives); 792 to 976 would be Xpert Ultra negative; of these 
792 to 800 would not have tuberculosis meningitis (true negatives) and 162 to 176 would have 
tuberculosis meningitis (false-negatives).  
 
Figure 27. LC-aNAATs for extrapulmonary tuberculosis using CSF and a composite reference 
standard. FN: false negative; FP: false positive; TN: true negative; TP: true positive.
