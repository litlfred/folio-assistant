Should LC-aNAATs on respiratory samples be used to diagnose pulmonary TB 
in HIV negative adults and adolescents with signs and symptoms or screened 
positive for pulmonary TB, against a microbiological reference standard? 
Figure 11 summarises the results of the risk of bias and applicability assessment for each of the six 
included studies. As previously noted, there are applicability concerns in patient selection for Park 2019 
based on recruitment of inpatients exclusively from a tertiary referral hospital.  
Figure 11. Risk of bias and applicability concern for each study: sputum specimen and 
microbiological reference standard in HIV negative adults and adolescents 
 
Six studies (1700 participants) evaluated sputum specimens from HIV negative adults and adolescents 
(Figure 12). The sensitivities ranged between 88% and 97% and the specificities between 91% and 99%. 
The summary sensitivity (95% CI) was 92.2% (87.6 to 95.2) and the summary specificity was 95.0% (92.3 
to 96.9). 
 
For a population of 1000 HIV negative adults and adolescents where 100 have pulmonary tuberculosis 
on culture, 137 would be LC-aNAAT-positive and 45 (33%) would not have tuberculosis (false-positives); 
863 would be LC-aNAAT-negative and 8 (1%) would have tuberculosis (false-negatives).
