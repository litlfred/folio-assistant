4. 
DISCUSSION 
 
LC-aNAAT discussion: 
Through a systematic review of published literature on LC aNAATs, we identified 29 studies that met our 
inclusion criteria. These included seventeen cost-effectiveness analyses and twelve cost analyses. Of the 
seventeen cost-effectiveness analyses, ten were on Xpert, six on Ultra and one on TrueNat. All twelve cost 
analyses were on Xpert. We did not find any cost analyses for Ultra or TrueNat.  
The studies included in our review were diverse, conducted across various settings and covered all income 
levels. This broad spectrum of research provides a comprehensive view of the economic evidence on LC 
aNAATs, with a particular focus on adults. Only three cost-effectiveness analyses included children. There 
were various comparators, including smear and culture. For studies evaluating Xpert, two studies assessed 
the cost-effectiveness of parallel testing with LAM in people living with HIV. Two Ultra studies also 
assessed parallel testing, one in combination with LAM and the other with WGS. Most of the studies 
included sputum specimens. Six of the ten full economic evaluation studies on Xpert presented results in 
natural units (additional case detected), and the other four presented utility outcomes (QALYs and DALYs).  
The findings from the cost-effectiveness analyses revealed that Xpert was generally cost-effective across 
the included studies when compared to smear or culture, with the exception of one study from Thailand, 
where TB LAMP was the dominant strategy. In contrast, for Ultra, there was more heterogeneity in the 
methodology used in the cost-effectiveness studies and the findings from the various studies. showed 
that Ultra vs. SSM, the ICER ranged from $72.72 to $160.23 per DALY averted. In the only study on 
TrueNat, it was cost-effectiveness in children in India compared with Xpert, with an ICER of $94.72 per 
DALY averted. We only found a few studies that assessed parallel testing, but when assessed, parallel 
testing was the preferred strategy among PLHIV in South Africa, with & Xpert and LAM being the preferred 
strategy compared with stand-alone testing despite the marginally higher ICER of $1626 per additional 
case diagnosed. In another study done in South Africa in PLHIV, Ultra & FujiLAM was the preferred strategy 
given the willingness to pay threshold and the most DALYs averted, with an ICER of $689.39 per DALY 
averted. 
Similar to the previous review done for the previous guideline development group meeting in 2019, this 
review found substantial economic evidence on Xpert. However, in contrast to that review, which did 
not find any studies on Ultra, this review yielded six cost-effectiveness analyses, notwithstanding the 
heterogeneity in comparators, outcomes, and summary measures of cost-effectiveness. We only found 
one study on TrueNat, which highlights a gap in the literature.
