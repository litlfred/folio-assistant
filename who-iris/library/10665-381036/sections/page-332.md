1.3 Role of the tests 
The WHO operational handbook on tuberculosis states that “Urine LF-LAM should be used as an 
add-on to clinical judgement in combination with other tests. It should not be used as a replacement 
or triage test” [World Health Organization. 2021c]. For LC-aNAAT, the suggested role is an initial test 
replacing smear microscopy for the diagnosis of tuberculosis [World Health Organization. 2021a]. LF-
LAM and LC-aNAAT may be performed in parallel (i.e. LF-LAM in combination with LC-aNAAT) where 
a positive result on either test implies a positive result for the combination of tests [World Health 
Organization. 2021c]. 
1.4 Rationale 
Several systematic reviews have been conducted to assess and compare the diagnostic accuracy of 
individual diagnostic tests for detection of tuberculosis including Xpert MTB/RIF and Xpert Ultra for 
pulmonary tuberculosis [Zifodya 2021; Shapiro 2021] and extrapulmonary tuberculosis samples 
[Kohli 2021b] and LF-LAM for people with HIV [Bjerrum 2019a]. Respiratory samples may be difficult 
to obtain particularly in people with HIV and this population may further have paucibacillary or 
extrapulmonary disease. A systematic review and meta-analysis using individual participant data 
found that while nearly all participants were able to produce a urine sample for testing, one in five 
participant was not able to produce sputum [Broger 2023b]. From clinical studies, we expect that 
there could be an incremental value in combining  LC-aNAAT from a respiratory sample and urine LF-
LAM to increase diagnostic yield for tuberculosis in people with HIV [J.G. Peter 2016; Broger 2019; 
Gupta-Wright 2018; Huerga 2017b; Thit 2017; Lawn 2017a]. In the meta-analysis by Broger et al. 
[Broger 2023b], in hospitalized people with HIV, the yield for microbiological diagnosis of 
tuberculosis using LF-LAM (Determine TB LAM) and Xpert assays were similar (51% versus 47%) 
while the tests in combination had a yield of 71%, supporting the implementation of combined 
testing strategies. 
This systematic review will estimate the change in diagnostic accuracy (i.e., sensitivity and 
specificity) when using respiratory LC-aNAAT and LF-LAM in parallel to diagnose tuberculosis disease 
among people with HIV, compared to using each of the tests alone. The findings will inform an 
upcoming WHO guideline development.
