Findings 
We included 36 studies for Xpert Ultra and three studies for Truenat MTB plus. We found no 
eligible studies for the other index tests. For a class level analysis for each specimen type 
across different technologies, we could only include studies from Xpert Ultra based on the 
criteria in Box 1. Therefore, the summary estimates in Table 2 were obtained from only 
studies of Xpert Ultra. We present the findings in detail below for each specimen type 
together with statements about the certainty of the evidence. Appendix 2 contains the 
summary of findings tables from the assessment done using the GRADE approach. 
 
Detection of extrapulmonary tuberculosis using LC-aNAATs 
For detection of extrapulmonary tuberculosis, we included 36 studies evaluating Xpert Ultra, 
Figure 2a.   
 
Of the 36 studies, 30 studies (83.3%) took place in high tuberculosis burden and 22 (59.4%) in 
high tuberculosis/HIV burden countries. We judged risk of bias to be low in the patient 
selection, index test, and flow and timing domains and high or unclear in the reference standard 
domain because many studies decontaminated sterile specimens before culture inoculation. 
Regarding applicability, in the patient selection domain, we judged high or unclear concern for 
most studies because either the participants were evaluated exclusively as inpatients at tertiary 
care centers (for any form of extrapulmonary tuberculosis, other than tuberculous meningitis), 
or we were not sure about the clinical settings.  
 
We included three studies that evaluated Truenat MTB Plus. These studies were conducted in 
high tuberculosis and high tuberculosis/HIV burden countries (Figure 2b). In the reference 
standard domain, we judged risk of bias to be high for one study as the reference standard was 
not blinded and unclear risk of bias for two studies as the decontamination process was not 
mentioned. We judged unclear applicability concern for the two studies since details regarding 
speciation were unknown.
