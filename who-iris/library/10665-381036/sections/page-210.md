Background 
Tuberculosis (TB) is one of the deadliest diseases with high mortality and morbidity. Globally, in 2022, 
7.5 million people were newly diagnosed with TB and 1.3 million died due to TB. (WHO Global TB 
Report 2023). One of the major obstacles to the elimination of TB is the delay in accurate diagnosis. 
Over the years, various rapid molecular tests have been recommended by WHO (mWRD) as the initial 
diagnostic test for TB. Based on the technology, performance and complexity of implementation, the 
WHO categorized these tests into different classes (low, moderate, and high complexity).  
Low complexity manual nucleic acid amplification tests (LC-mNAATs) require an initial manual 
specimen treatment step followed by three distinct steps: DNA extraction, PCR amplification and 
results visualization. The reagents used in these tests are enclosed in multiple disposable sealed 
containers not requiring special storage conditions. It does not require advanced skills to perform the 
tests. Also, it requires the same biosafety measures and infrastructure as smear microscopy. Because 
of its merits, LC-mNAATs can be used as a replacement for smear microscopy in resource-limited 
settings. Hence WHO recommended replacing smear microscopy with mWRD wherever possible. This 
systematic review aims to assess the diagnostic accuracy of LC-mNAATs for detecting TB in adolescents 
and adults.  
Target condition being diagnosed 
M. tuberculosis is the bacteria that causes TB, an infectious disease that spreads through the air via 
respiratory droplets from an infected individual. M. tuberculosis can cause pulmonary and 
extrapulmonary TB. Pulmonary TB refers to the TB disease that exclusively affects the lungs. When TB 
affects organs of the body except the lungs, it is referred to as extrapulmonary TB. Pulmonary TB is 
the most prevalent form of TB. Loss of appetite, loss of weight, lethargy, fever, chills, night sweats, 
cough, and haemoptysis are common symptoms of pulmonary TB. 
Index test  
The index test is LC-mNAATs and the most common LC-mNAAT is TB-LAMP (loop-mediated isothermal 
amplification). TB-LAMP has been developed and marketed by Eiken Chemicals (Tokyo, Japan). TB-
LAMP is based on an amplification process that uses four primers that match six locations of the target 
gene and a strand displacement reaction at 65°C for 15 to 60 minutes. An improved amplification 
process of 109 to 1010 times can be achieved by using loop primers in TB-LAMP assay (WHO 2016). 
Since this assay is insensitive to the buildup of DNA and pyrophosphate salt by-products, it can 
continue until a substantial amount of amplicons has been produced. This also makes the 
amplification process visible to the naked eye using double-stranded DNA dyes, such as SYBR Green. 
The assay can be completed within one hour and consists of three steps: sample preparation (10 to 
20 minutes), amplification (40 minutes), and visual detection of fluorescence using ultraviolet light 
(0.5 to 1 minute). Studies of adults with TB have reported higher sensitivity and specificity for TB‐
LAMP than those of smear microscopy (Iwamoto 2003; Joon 2017). TB-LAMP gives negative, positive, 
or indeterminate/invalid results according to manufacturer recommendations. TB-LAMP does not 
provide any information on rifampicin resistance or resistance to other TB drugs. 
Objectives  
1. To assess the diagnostic accuracy of LC-mNAATs for detection of pulmonary and 
extrapulmonary TB by the site of disease in adults and adolescents with presumptive TB.  
2. To investigate potential sources of heterogeneity. For detection of pulmonary TB, we will 
investigate the effect of smear microscopy status (smear positive/smear negative), HIV 
status (positive/negative), and the history of TB on test accuracy. 
 
Methods 
Methods are described in detail in the generic protocol for six diagnostic accuracy reviews that will 
provide evidence to inform updates to WHO policy guidelines on tests for TB detection. The protocol 
is available on the Open Source Repository.
