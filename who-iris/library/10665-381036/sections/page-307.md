5. DISCUSSION  
This systematic review summarizes the evidence on the parallel use of LC-aNAAT on respiratory 
samples and LC-aNAAT on stool in children without HIV with presumptive pulmonary tuberculosis 
disease, compared to each test alone, based on 12 fully paired comparative diagnostic accuracy 
studies. Furthermore, it summarises the evidence on the parallel use of LC-aNAAT on respiratory 
samples plus LC-aNAAT on stool and LF-LAM on urine in children with HIV with presumptive 
pulmonary tuberculosis disease, compared to each test alone, based on six fully paired comparative 
diagnostic accuracy studies

Based on eight studies included in this meta-analysis in children without HIV or unclear HIV status 
for a microbiological reference standard (2,145 participant, tuberculosis prevalence 8.1% 
[173/2,145]), the estimated diagnostic accuracy of the parallel use of LC-aNAAT on respiratory 
samples and stool is 
• 
Pooled sensitivity: 79.9% (67.9 to 89.8) | Pooled specificity: 93.4% (87.2 to 97.0) 
➔ For a population of 1,000 children where 100 have tuberculosis on culture, 139 would test 
positive using the parallel testing strategy; of these, 80 would have pulmonary tuberculosis 
(true positives) and 59 would not have pulmonary tuberculosis (false positives); 861 would test 
negative by the parallel testing strategy; of these 841 would not have pulmonary tuberculosis 
(true negatives) and 20 would have pulmonary tuberculosis (false-negatives). 
Compared to LC-aNAAT on respiratory samples alone, difference in accuracy is: 
• 
Pooled difference in sensitivity: 7.1% (3.2 to 13.4)  
• 
Pooled difference in specificity: -1.7% (-3.8 to -0.6) 
➔ For a population of 1,000 children where 100 have tuberculosis on culture, compared to 
testing with LC-aNAAT on respiratory sample alone, 23 children would additionally test 
positive with the parallel strategy; of these, 7 would have pulmonary tuberculosis (true 
positives) and 16 would not have pulmonary tuberculosis (false positives); compared to 
testing with LC-aNAAT on respiratory sample alone, 23 children less would test negative; 
of these, 16 would not have pulmonary tuberculosis (true negatives) and 7 would have 
pulmonary tuberculosis (false-negatives). 
Compared to LC-aNAAT on stool alone, difference in accuracy is: 
• 
Pooled difference in sensitivity: 22.1% (13.7 to 32.7)  
• 
Pooled difference in specificity: -4.1% (-8.0 to -1.7) 
➔ For a population of 1,000 children where 100 have tuberculosis on culture,   
compared to testing with LC-aNAAT on stool alone, 59 children would additionally test 
positive with the parallel strategy; of these, 22 would have pulmonary tuberculosis (true 
positives) and 37 would not have pulmonary tuberculosis (false positives); compared to 
testing with LC-aNAAT on stool alone, 59 children less would test negative; of these, 37 
would not have pulmonary tuberculosis (true negatives) and 22 would have pulmonary 
tuberculosis (false-negatives).
