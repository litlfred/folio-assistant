Italics are not, however, used for:

allo bis cyclo epi
iso nor tris.

A lower-case italic prefix at the beginning of a line or after a full stop
is not given an initial capital; the next letter becomes a capital instead
(e.g. trans-2-Butene). A capitalized italic prefix does not serve as the
capital marking the beginning of a sentence; the first letter of the name
itselfshould therefore become a capital (e.g. N, N-Dimethyltryptamine).
Italicized syllables are not “counted” in alphabetization.

Diseases

As far as possible the International Nomenclature of Diseases should
be followed for disease names and their spelling; otherwise standard
dictionaries (e.g. Stedman’s or Dorland’s) should be consulted. It
should be noted that the International Classification of Diseases
(ICD-10) is a statistical classification and is not intended to be a
reference for nomenclature.

Drugs and pesticides

For pharmaceuticals the International Nonproprietary Names (INNs)
should be used, and for pesticides the common names adopted by the
International Organization for Standardization (see International Stan-
dard ISO 1750:1981, and subsequent addenda and amendments). When
no internationally approved name exists, an approved national name
may be used. Proprietary names should be avoided whenever possible.
