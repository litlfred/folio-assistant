WHO Editorial Style Monval

Nucleosides
The symbols used for ribonucleoside residues (radicals) are:
A. adenosine C cytidine
G_ guanosine T  ribosylthymine
I inosine U_ uridine
X  xanthosine wy 5-ribosyluracil (pseudouridine).

The prefix “t” (for ribo) may be used for emphasis or clarity.

The 2'-deoxyribonucleosides can be designated by the same symbols
preceded by “d”, for example:

dA 2'-deoxyribosyladenine
dT 2'-deoxyribosylthymine (thymidine).

Sugars
Symbols may be used for representing polymers or sequences and in
figures and tables:

Ara — arabinose Man mannose

dRib 2-deoxyribose Rib ribose

Fru fructose Neu neuraminic acid
Fuc fucose Rha _ rhamnose

Gal galactose Xyl xylose.

Gle glucose

chemistry

Chemical names should follow the IUPAC tules, as interpreted by
the American Chemical Society (see Chemical abstracts). Note that
“sulfur” should always be used rather than “sulphur”, but that the Brit-
ish spellings “aluminium” and “caesium” are preferted to “aluminum”
and “cesium”.

General

Chemical names should be spelt out in text unless the symbolic
formula is graphically useful. In one-line formulae used to represent
molecules, dashes may be used to represent bonds in the main chain,
for example:

HN — [CH,], — CH(NH,) — CO.H.

If there is no need to show linkages, the formula may be written
without bond symbols, for example:

(CH,),CHCH,CH(NH,)COOH.
