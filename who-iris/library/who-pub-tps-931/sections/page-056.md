14. Scientific terminology

The scientific terminology used in WHO publications should be in
accordance with the recommendations of the relevant international
bodies. Guidance on correct usage can be obtained from CTT.

Anatomy

In general, the anglicized versions of Latin anatomical terms should be
used, as found in the standard medical dictionaries. If, for a specific
reason, Latin terms are preferred in a given context, they should not be
italicized.

Animals, plonts, bacteria and viruses
The relevant codes of international nomenclature should be used.

The name of a genus is set in italic with an initial capital (e.g.
Staphylococcus); it refers to all species and subspecies within the genus
and should not be used as a substitute for the name of a single species.
The name of a species consists of the genus name and the specific
epithet, both italicized (e.g. Escherichia coli). The name of the person
who originally classified the species sometimes follows the binomen in
roman (e.g. Schistosoma mansoni Sambon). A genus name may be
abbreviated to its initial letter after the first mention, unless this leads
to ambiguity.

Common names (not italicized) also exist for certain genera and
species, for example:

pseudomonad
salmonella
blackfly
gorilla.

Both one-word and two-word common names are in use for viruses
(e.g. poliovirus, papillomavirus, poxvirus, herpesvirus; but influenza
virus, cowpox virus, rubella virus, mumps virus).
