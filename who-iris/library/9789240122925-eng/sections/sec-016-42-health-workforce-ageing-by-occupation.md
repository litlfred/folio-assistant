The age distribution of health workers is linked to the age distribution of the total population, 
showing similarly younger health workers in LICs and older health workers in HICs. However, 
notable differences can be seen when comparing specific occupations, with medical doctors 
being older on average than nursing personnel (Fig. 17 (a) and (b)). This may be attributed to 
the differing education and career pathways for the health occupations.

4. Thematic analysis – population and health workforce ageing
Health workers currently aged 55 years and above are expected to retire, and leave the health 
labour market within the next 10 years. Table 6 shows the variation in the share of health 
workers aged 55 years and above by occupation and by income group. As noted above, medical 
doctors have the highest proportion of workers aged 55 years and above – nearly one out of 
four. Compared to LICs, HICs have a higher share of health workers aged 55 years and above, 
with nearly one out of three medical doctors expected to retire within the next 10 years in HICs.

It is of interest to compare the share of health workers aged 55 years and above, i.e. leaving 
the health labour market in the next 10 years, with the share of those aged 35 years and 
below, i.e. those who approximately entered the health labour market in the last 10 years. The 
comparison of these shares can identify countries for which the exit from retirement is faster 
than entrance at a young age. The pattern of ageing workforce is marked in HICs and to a lower 
extent in upper middle-income countries (UMICs), as evidence indicates for medical doctors 
and nursing personnel (Fig. 18 (a) and (b)).
