Methodology to estimate health workforce need
Age-standardization of the health workforce need
In the assessment of the impact of population ageing on the need, an age-standardization 
approach was used. The standardization was a double standardization on population age 
structure and on age-specific medical needs. As a result, the gap between the projected HWF 
by 2030 and the need based on 2020 HWF density was assessed while accounting for a change 
in the density due to change in the age structure of the underlying population.
To illustrate the computation, a country example is provided. In this example, the country has 
56 292 health workers in 2020 and is projected to have 61 533 health workers by 2030 (refer to 
(18) for further details on methods). Its population is projected to grow from 5.9 million in 2020 
to 6.3 million in 2030 and the share of those aged 65 years and above is projected to rise from 
13.4% in 2020 to 22.5% by 2030. In this example, the HWF density is projected to increase from 
96.2 per 10 000 population in 2020 to 98.3 in 2030 (Table A3.1).

a.	 Population statistics should be extracted by age group for 2020 and 2030, here from

b.	 A standard curve should be identified illustrating different needs according to age. 
Here, data on total costs per capita and by age were extracted from a study from 
Kalseth and Halvorsen (47), Figure 2) and the values were standardized to 100. It 
suggests that individuals aged 65 years and above would need about 3 times more 
health workers than those aged 25–34 years of age.

c.	 Age-specific weights can be computed multiplying population in 2020 by the standard 
curve value. This corresponds to a double weighting by age and by standard curve of 
needs. Column ‘c1’ represents absolute weights, and column ‘c2’ represents relative 
weights.
d.	 The stock is distributed by age group from multiplying the total stock observed in 2020, 
i.e., 56 292 health workers, by age-specific weights and dividing by the sum of weights. 
This enables to redistribute the total HWF size by age group. 
e.	 The age-specific HWF density can be estimated based on the stock redistributed (d) 
and the population in 2020. Note that if no standard curve (b) is used (or a flat curve 
with equal weight by age group), the redistribution of health workers is only influenced 
by population structure and assumes a unique age-specific density similar to the total 
density (96.2 health workers per 10 000 population) for each age group.
f.	
Lastly, the number of health workers needed in 2030 to maintain the age-specific HWF 
density (e) in 2020 is estimated by multiplying the 2020 age-specific HWF density by 
the population in 2030.

This analysis shows that when accounting for the change in population age structure, the HWF 
size in 2030 should be 68 380 to maintain the age-specific HWF density estimated for the year 
2020, because older population have greater needs.
It must be noted that the projected stock by 2030 already factors in the ageing of the health 
workers in a stock and flow model. As a result, the comparison of the projected HWF stock and 
the age-standardization of HWF density by 2030 depicts both the phenomenon of ageing of 
the caregivers as well as that of ageing of the recipients of care. This analysis shows that the 
unadjusted positive increase observed in Table A3.1 above from 56 292 to 61 533 is actually 
not sufficient to cover the age-adjusted needs of 68 380. And a density of 68 380 / 6 262 466 
= 109.2 health workers per 10 000 population would be needed instead of the projected 98.3 
per 10 000 population. The HWF gap between the age-specific need and the projected size can 
be estimated as 68 380 – 61 533 = 6 847. When presented as percentage increase, this analysis 
shows that the currently projected stock increase of 9% (61 533/56 292 – 1) should be raised to 
21% (68 380/56 292 – 1) to maintain by 2030 the age-specific HWF density observed in 2020.
