health workforce ageing 2024–2030 
The global HWF stock is projected to increase faster from 2024 to 2030 (16% increase) than the 
global population during the same period (5% increase). In the European Region and the Region 
of the Americas, where the combined HWF size is higher than other regions, the estimated 
increase of the HWF is smaller (6% in the Region of the Americas and 5% in the European 
Region). Looking at the change in density of health workers between 2024 and the projection 
to 2030 shows that 45 countries are projected to have a lower density by 2030, with 17 of these 
countries being from the European Region.
The rapid increase in the older population, aged 65 years and above, described earlier would 
result in an increase in medical needs as this population age group usually requires more access 
to health services (47). Consequently, in ageing populations, the maintenance of the national 
level of HWF density to 2030 would probably not be sufficient to cover the population’s need 
for health workers. Accounting for this increased health needs in older age group, the HWF 
stock needs to be standardized. We therefore conducted a double standardization analysis of 
the HWF stock using (i) the change in age structure from 2024 to 2030, and (ii) an age-specific 
curve of health needs (see Annex 3 for further details). The standard curve of health needs 
was selected based on averaging several curves showing health costs per capita by age. This 
double standardization enabled to estimate the age-standardized density of health workers 
needed by 2030 while accounting for the ageing population and the fact that older individuals 
have more medical needs.
Comparing the age-adjusted increase in HWF need to the projected HWF in 2030, it was estimated 
that 67 countries would be impacted, i.e., they would not have a sufficient HWF to maintain 
the age-adjusted need of their population (Table 7). This amounts to 22 additional countries to 
the already identified 45 countries projected to observe a decrease in their age-standardized 
HWF density, i.e. not able to provide the same coverage to their population as in 2024. These 67 
countries will experience an overall gap of 832 000 health workers, representing 4.6% of their 
projected stock by 2030. This age-induced gap assessment is different from the HWF shortage 
of 11 million health workers projected by 2030 (45), but because it is mostly affecting HICs, this 
gap can be considered as an additional shortage of health workers by 2030, and this might 
trigger an increased reliance on foreign health workers in these countries.
