This section presents key results based primarily on the 2025 NHWA data release and some 
trends that depict the improvement in HWF data availability over time.
i) Overall data reporting status:
178 countries (92%) reported at least one new data point on the NHWA data platform during 
the latest year (2025) while all 194 countries have shared data during the last five years (2021–
2025). 
ii) Overall data availability status:
Fig. 3 maps each  country by the latest year for which data was available. 116 countries (60%) 
have at least one data point available for any indicator or for any occupation on the NHWA data 
platform for the most recent year (2024) while 192 (99%) countries have data available for the 
last five years (2020–2024).

2. NHWA implementation
vii) Trend in stock data availability for SDG 3.c occupations over time:
The frequency of countries with stock data reported for the SDG 3.c.1 occupations (medical 
doctors, nursing and midwifery personnel, dentists and pharmacists) per year from 1990 
onwards was compared in the 2025 NHWA data release (containing data for the period 1990–
2024) vis-à-vis the 2018 Global Health Workforce Statistics (GHWS) dataset, published in the 
GHO (containing data for the period 1990–2016), which can be considered as the “pre-NHWA” or 
baseline dataset. Spikes in data reporting tend to coincide with the preparation or publication 
of earlier landmark HWF reports. For instance, the spike in the number of countries reporting 
stock data for 2004 in the GHWS 2018 data release corresponds to the preparatory phase of 
the World Health Report 2006 (2). Similarly, the spike in countries reporting stock data for 
nursing and midwifery personnel during the period 2013–2018 in the NHWA 2023 data release 
aligns with the preparation of the State of the World’s Nursing 2020 (20) and the State of the 
World’s Midwifery 2021 (21). In contrast, the progressive implementation of the National Health 
Workforce Accounts (NHWA) across countries has led to a more sustained level of reporting and 
has largely avoided such spikes in recent HWF reports, such as the State of the World’s Nursing 
2025 (26). Overall, there is a drastic improvement in data availability for these occupations, 
from 4736 unique data points in 2018 to 13 755 unique data points in 2025 – an increase of 
2.9 times (Fig. 8). Significantly, even when the same time period of 1990–2016 is compared 
for both datasets, the number of unique data points has doubled from 4736 in 2018 to 9787 in 
2025, indicating that countries continue to update their retrospective data and improve their 
longitudinal time trends.

2. NHWA implementation
viii) Data availability for key HWF topics:
The progressive implementation of the NHWA by countries is also contributing to the 
diversification of HWF data to inform policies and plans. There is a high availability of data 
across key HWF topics, such as stock, activity level, graduates and HWF distribution by various 
parameters such as sex, age group, place of birth, place of training, facility ownership, facility 
type and subnational level, with most of them having some data for at least 100 countries 
(Fig. 9).
Fig. 9: Status of data availability for key HWF topics
ix) Trend of data volume for key HWF topics:
The number of data points across successive annual NHWA data releases were compared with 
the HWF dataset from the GHO as of 2018 to assess the increase in data availability for key 
HWF topics over the last seven years. Fig. 10 indicates an almost exponential increase in data 
availability over time (almost 20-fold), from 12 587 data points in 2018 to 248 873 in 2025. In 
addition, there has been a diversification of HWF data over time, with more data now available 
for key HWF topics, compared to the pre-NHWA dataset which only contained stock data.

In summary, there has been a tremendous improvement in the availability of HWF data over 
the last few years, across multiple occupations and various HWF topics. This is primarily due 
to the efforts by Member States to strengthen their HWF situation, develop robust HRHIS, 
improve HWF data and evidence, and use these data to inform evidence-based HWF plans 
and policies. The leadership of the NHWA focal points across the world at national level has 
been critical in catalyzing the progressive implementation of the NHWA in their respective 
countries and paving the way to enable the use of NHWA data to measure and monitor key 
characteristics of the HWF at global, regional and national levels, identify key issues facing the 
HWF, develop notable knowledge products and inform evidence-based planning and policy-
making processes at national, regional and global levels.
