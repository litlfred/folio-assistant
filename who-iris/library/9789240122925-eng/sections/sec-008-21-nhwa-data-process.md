2.1 NHWA data process
Prior to the NHWA process, the WHO Secretariat published annual updates on health workforce 
stock data by health occupations and limited demographics data to the global health workforce 
statistics database, through a data mining process. Currently, at the national level, NHWA focal 
points convene multi-stakeholder and multisectoral (e.g. education, finance, labour, private 
sector) working groups which oversee the collection, collation and analysis of HWF data, by 
following best practices for HRHIS such as diversification of data sources, triangulation of data, 
and data quality assessments (9). These focal points are in most cases senior authoritative 
officials in the Ministry of Health – usually directors of the HWF department or unit – or at 
specialized government agencies which are designated to collect health-related information 
and statistics. Focal points then share the HWF data on behalf of their country following the 
NHWA norms and standards annually (13). Data can be shared up to the penultimate year. In case 
the country has been able to gather more accurate data for previous years, then the focal point 
also provides retrospective updates. These data are then assessed by the NHWA Secretariat 
located within the Health Workforce Data and Learning Analytics Unit at WHO headquarters 
through data quality processes involving data analysis, triangulation of information, and other 
methods (44). In case any discrepancies are identified, requests are sent to focal points for 
consultation and clarification (see Annex 2 for more details on the NHWA data cycle). WHO 
provides technical support to focal points for the progressive implementation of the NHWA, 
through its three levels as well as in collaboration with partners. The country validated data is 
then published and disseminated as a global public health good through various channels such 
as the NHWA data portal, the Global Health Observatory (GHO), the World Health Data Hub 
(WHDH) and SDG 3.c.1 reports to the Secretary-General of the United Nations (UN), to name 
a few. The SDG 3.c.1 indicator reports on health worker density and distribution and includes 
medical doctors, nursing and midwifery personnel, dentists and pharmacists. Complementary 
to the data release, the validated data is used to develop knowledge products that cater to 
national, regional and global needs.
The present report draws on official country data shared thus far by WHO Member States on 
the NHWA platform (including the 2025 data reporting cycle) following data quality checks, 
which is subsequently referred to in the report as the 2025 NHWA data release. These data are 
also available on the NHWA data portal (12).
