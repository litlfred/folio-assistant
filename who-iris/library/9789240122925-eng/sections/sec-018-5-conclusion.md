Conclusion 
National health workforce accounts – health workforce levels and trends 2026
38
Over the last 20 years, data availability on various HWF characteristics have progressed 
substantially, in particular since the launch of the NHWA. More than 90% of WHO Member 
States have appointed focal points for annual reporting to the NHWA platform. Majority of 
countries have reported stock data for multiple occupations, have been reporting stock data 
periodically in the last 7 years, have reported data for various HWF key topics, and this has led 
to a substantial diversification of HWF data beyond stock since 2018. This progress has been 
driven by the leadership of NHWA focal points, who have been successful in strengthening the 
collection, analysis and use of HWF data at national level.
According to the latest available data, substantial disparities persist in HWF density, both by 
WHO region and by World Bank income group. From 2006 to 2025, there has been a substantial 
increase in HWF density at global level, with variations by WHO region and by occupation. 
The demographic impact of ageing affects both health workers and the population they 
serve. 67 countries would not be able to compensate for the retirement of health workers 
by employment of young health workers and have a projected 832 000 health workers gap 
to maintain the age-standardized HWF density as in 2024. This gap affects mostly HICs and 
is therefore adding to the 11 million projected global shortage by 2030. Thus, this analysis 
underscores the importance of understanding and analyzing the health labour market dynamics.
This report has showcased the improvement of evidence base on HWF statistics, thanks to 
the efforts by countries to follow the core principles of NHWA implementation to enable more 
robust monitoring of HWF stock, demographics, migration, subnational equity, education and 
financing. This has required – and continues to require – sustained investments by countries 
to improve their capability to collect, analyze and report HWF data and to strengthen the 
capacity of their HRHIS. NHWA is a global public health good that helps countries to track 
their efforts towards UHC, the SDGs and other health objectives. To this effect, WHO strives to 
support countries in their progressive implementation of the NHWA and continues to engage 
and collaborate with donors and partner organizations to align national efforts towards 
strengthening the collection, analysis and use of HWF data and evidence in countries.
