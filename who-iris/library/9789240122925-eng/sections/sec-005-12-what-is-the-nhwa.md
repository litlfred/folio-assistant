1.2 What is the NHWA?
The NHWA can be defined as a system by which countries progressively improve the availability, 
quality, and use of HWF data through monitoring of a set of indicators to support achievement 
of UHC, SDGs and other health objectives. 
Box 1   Core principles of the NHWA
The core principles of the NHWA are as follows:
•	 Multi-stakeholder and multi-sectoral governance mechanism of the national NHWA data 
•	 System strengthening approach 
•	 Progressive self-paced implementation
•	 Parsimonious approach to collecting validated indicators / proxies
•	 Inclusive country-owned and country-led (through the vital role of NHWA focal points) process in 
prioritising and reporting indicators and terminology at national level
•	 Continuous updating mechanism using valid, reliable and diverse data sources (no primary data 
collection)
•	 Annual data release
Source: (13)
Various technical tools and guidance have been developed to assist countries in NHWA 
implementation, such as the handbook containing a standardized set of HWF indicators and 
meta-data (8), the implementation guide (9), the NHWA online data platform for focal points to 
report HWF data on (10), and a job aid to map the national job titles of health and care workers 
(HCWs) with the appropriate HWF occupational group listed in the NHWA [(11), as available in 
the International Standard Classification of Occupations(ISCO-08)]. In addition, the NHWA data 
portal is openly accessible and contains the latest available global HWF data for key indicators, 
country profiles, occupation profiles and other visualizations and resources (12). In 2023, the 
NHWA was revised, based on an extensive assessment of five years of its implementation and 
feedback from NHWA focal points, regional offices and technical experts from various sectors, 
and an updated handbook containing an optimized set of indicators and various examples of 
NHWA data use was released (13).
The implementation of the National Health Workforce Accounts (NHWA) supports countries in 
carrying out both Workload Indicators of Staffing Need (WISN) assessments and Health Labour 
Market Analysis (HLMA) (14,15). By establishing NHWA, countries are able to strengthen their 
health information systems and enhance collaboration among multiple stakeholders. Many 
of the data elements typically collected for WISN exercises correspond closely with NHWA 
indicators. Countries that have already applied the WISN methodology cover NHWA indicators 
related to workforce stock and distribution, employment conditions, and remuneration.

1. Introduction
Conversely, countries that begin implementing NHWA often conduct an initial mapping of 
their national health information systems, generating the baseline data required for WISN 
studies. Both NHWA indicators and the WISN approach rely on common data components 
outlined in the WHO Health Labour Market Framework. Similarly, a HLMA can be performed 
more quickly and cost-effectively when NHWA has been implemented, thus ensuring that the 
country already has data on key indicators related to various aspects of the health labour 
market. Further, the conduct of a HLMA can provide additional relevant information on certain 
HWF topics that can be channelled back into the NHWA. In addition, there is a clear alignment 
of activities between NHWA implementation, the HLMA approach and the WISN process, 
such as engagement of stakeholders through formation of technical working groups and 
steering committees, compilation analysis and triangulation of HWF data, use of this data 
and information to facilitate the development of HWF policies and strategies as well as their 
monitoring and evaluation, and the broader efforts to strengthen information systems (16).
Since its launch in 2017, thanks to combined efforts of the three levels of WHO, over 500 HRH 
experts from more than 150 countries in all WHO regions benefited from NHWA trainings 
or country support. This has resulted in a harmonized understanding of the standardized 
measurement approach developed by WHO and a tremendous improvement in HWF data 
availability and quality. The NHWA has succeeded in its mandate of fostering an integrated 
approach for annual and timely collection of HWF information and defining core indicators in 
support of strategic workforce planning and monitoring at national, regional and global levels. 
Through the progressive implementation of the NHWA, countries have been able to strengthen 
their national human resources for health information systems (HRHIS) to collect and analyse 
reliable and up-to-date HWF data on stock, subnational distribution, distribution by sex, 
distribution by age group, distribution by place of training and/or place of birth, education and 
other topics, and utilize this data to drive evidence-based HWF planning and policy-making at 
national level (17). At the global and regional levels, the NHWA data has been used to measure 
key characteristics of the global HWF and has been instrumental in the development of various 
seminal knowledge products (18–33). In addition, the NHWA has become the official mechanism 
to monitor various global initiatives and frameworks (34–43).
