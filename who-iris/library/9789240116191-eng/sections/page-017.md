Introduction
How to use this handbook 
This handbook provides a stepwise approach to 
implementing standardized NPC and product master 
data management processes.
•	
Chapter 1 outlines guidance on establishing the 
necessary regulatory framework to support and 
promote product master data standardization.
•	
Chapter 2 addresses policies and considerations 
for product master data management.
•	
Chapter 3 presents technology specifications
and implementation considerations.
•	
Chapter 4 highlights the importance of robust change 
management and governance.
•	
Chapter 5 explores approaches for building human 
resource capacity to sustainably manage NPC.
•	
Chapter 6 provides details on WHO hosted instance 
of the open-source product catalogue management 
tool (PCMT).
•	
Chapter 7 explains how countries can share data 
as a global good to WHO’s hosted instance and 
benefit from aggregated product master data.
How this handbook was developed 
This handbook was developed from the experiences and 
feedback of a variety of experts compiled through:
•	
Stakeholder consultations were conducted through 
a series of in-country and virtual sessions with 
stakeholders who have supported DHSC 
implementations in countries such as Ethiopia, Malawi, 
Nigeria, Rwanda, and Zambia. These discussions 
focused on identifying challenges related to product 
master data management and data standardization, 
exploring country approaches to aligning with global 
standards, and capturing lessons learned from 
country experiences.
•	
DHSC Small Working Group (DHSC SWG): a subgroup 
under the Digital Health and Interoperability Working 
Group (DH&I WG). The DH&I WG is a volunteer 
community of practice committed to strengthening 
health systems through the appropriate and 
responsible use of digital health technologies. Its 
membership includes over 250 individuals from 
governments, donors, multilateral organizations, 
academia, the private sector, and Non-Governmental 
Organizations (NGOs). The DHSC SWG members met 
monthly to review the handbook and provide input 
as individual experts, collaboratively shaping its 
structure and content.  
•	
Insights from NPC Implementations: practical 
experiences of NPCs across different contexts were 
analysed and integrated.
•	
Alignment with Established Frameworks: the steps 
outlined in this handbook are informed by the 
Principles for Digital Development (Annex 1). Some 
methodologies and templates have been adapted from 
established sources such as:
-	
DIIG
-	
WHO/ITU National eHealth Strategy Toolkit
-	
Supply chain information systems maturity 
model (SCISMM)
-	
OpenHIE Framework
Decision-making was collaborative and consensus-based, 
with stakeholder feedback synthesized and validated through 
DHSC SWG meetings. Drafts were iteratively reviewed, revised, 
and agreed upon to ensure diverse perspectives were 
reflected and aligned with global frameworks.
