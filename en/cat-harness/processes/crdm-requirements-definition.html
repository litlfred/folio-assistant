CRDM Phases 2–4 — BPA and requirements

Process_CRDM_Requirements · strict · 6 step(s)

Map the current workflow, then define the requirements and the impact they carry, and loop until approved. Same shape as Phase 1 and for the same reason: an unapproved requirement is a proposal.

How it connects

  • Called by: CRDM requirements
  • Calls: Wireframe design review

Agentauthoring-agentOwns Phases 2 through 4a end to end, but the revise loop (GW_Reqs’ ‘revise’ branch) re-enters at A_DefineReqs rather than back at A_MapWorkflow — so mapping the current workflow is this lane’s one-time fact-finding, done once per phase run, while defining requirements and comparing options is what gets redone against BA feedback until Lane_Stakeholders approves. When a requirement changes a rendered surface, this lane also designs it before anything is built: GW_RenderedSurface sends it to Call_Wireframe, where the agent is the Designer of Process_WireframeDesignReview and posts its candidates for the BA to choose from. The call sits here rather than in Lane_BA because drawing and checking the candidates is the agent’s work; the BA’s part is the choice, made at BA_ReviewReqs.
StakeholdersstakeholderThe verdict GW_Reqs reads to decide between ending the phase and sending A_DefineReqs around again — a narrower approval than crdm-close.bpmn’s final feature sign-off, since what is being approved here is the REQUIREMENT model itself, not that the delivered feature satisfies it.

wireframe-design-review		Design use of wireframe-design-review, before implementation: a written design intent, at least two mid-fidelity candidates each with a web and a mobile layout, the mechanical checks, and a blind review. The candidates are committed as assets of the request’s proposal, under docs/proposals//wireframes/ beside docs/proposals/.md, on the feature branch so feature-staging publishes them, and shown where the requester will look, chosen from context — the conversation, the issue the request came from, the pull request, or several of these — each place carrying the same staging deep links and screenshots (staging-review, the deep-link rule, point 5: where it goes is a judgement). The requester or BA is the human reviewer and chooses one at BA_ReviewReqs. The owner asked for this on 2026-09-23 (methodologies/wiregen.md): a wireframe is part of the design process for adjudication.
The candidates ARE the options A_CompareOptions lays out, so this merges into it rather than producing a second comparison.
Phase 4a: Compare the viable options
A_CompareOptions		Agentdecision-comparisonPhase 4 often ends with a CHOICE rather than a plan,
and this is the step that hands it over. Per option: what it does, its pro, its
con, what it changes DOWNSTREAM, and how reversible it is — laid out where the
rows can be read against each other, then one recommendation and a stated
default.
A step of its own rather than a line inside A_DefineReqs, because it has a
different output and a different reader: the impact analysis is for the record,
the comparison is the thing the BA answers from. Folding it in is how it gets
skipped — the analysis feels finished, so the options go over as a list of
names.
When Call_Wireframe ran, its wireframe candidates are among the options: one row per candidate, with a link to its web and mobile screenshots.
Skipped legitimately when the analysis yields ONE viable approach. Do not
manufacture alternatives to fill a table.
