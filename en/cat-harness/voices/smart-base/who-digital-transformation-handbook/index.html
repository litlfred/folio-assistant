digitization-digitalization-transformation-are-three-stepsDigitization, digitalization and digital transformation are three different scopes
The handbooks define the three terms narrowly. Digitization is converting paper records into digital format; digitalization is automating and simplifying processes WITHIN individual health programmes; digital transformation is systems-level change across all programme areas. Whether they are also an ORDERED sequence is where the sources differ, and both readings are on record as alternatives (smart-base/findings/dth-terms.md; owner, 2026-10-03, #1984): the supply chain handbook adds that digitization is the initial step and digitalization the second, and the product catalogue handbook repeats it as steps 'towards' digital transformation, while the primary health care handbook defines the same terms without any order. So write the scope a term names, and do not assert the order as the handbooks' common position. In ordinary usage 'digitalization' is a loose synonym for going digital at any scale, and the draft DPI-H Reference Architecture itself quotes the World Bank calling DPI 'an approach to digitalization' — a broad, cross-sector sense the earlier handbooks would call digital transformation. The draft also writes 'digitalisation' and 'digitisation' alongside 'digitalization'; the published handbooks use -ization throughout. Because the draft may change, prefer the published handbooks' sense and spelling.		terminology · major
In the health context, the process of digitally automating and simplifying processes to streamline individual health programmes and reduce manual efforts for greater efficiency. Digitalization is the second step in digital transformation

register-is-not-registryA register is a list of service users; a registry is a governed master system
The primary health care handbook defines the two words separately and they are not variants. A register is a list of health service users compiled to track care, aggregate activity or find people needing an intervention — a paper ANC register is a register. A registry is a governed, authoritative, centralized information system holding the unique attributes and identifiers of facilities, health service users, products and/or the health workforce against a canonical minimum data set; the supply chain and product catalogue handbooks and the draft Reference Architecture carry the same registry definition. Note that the draft groups the terminology service, the logical information model repository and the lifelong health record under 'metadata registries and foundational components'; that heading does not make them registries in this sense.		terminology · major
In the context of health care, a list of health service users compiled for the purposes of tracking care, aggregating activities or health outcomes, or identifying health service users who need specific interventions.

dpi-h-is-not-dpiDPI-H is health-specific infrastructure built on foundational DPI, not DPI itself
Rests on the DRAFT Reference Architecture (V1.0, for public comment), which may change. DPI-H names digital resources built as a shared means for multiple HEALTH agency functions; it leverages and extends foundational DPI (identity, payments, data exchange — abbreviated DPI-F) and does not replicate it. The draft is explicit that DPI-H is not a collection of programme-specific tools or point-of-service applications. Write DPI-H with the hyphen. The draft is itself inconsistent on the expansion — its acronym list and definition give 'Health Digital Public Infrastructure', its cover 'Digital Public Infrastructure for Health', and one glossary entry writes 'HDPI' — so the abbreviation, not any one expansion, is the stable form.		terminology · major
Health Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI-H) refers to digital resources that are built as a shared means for fulfilling multiple health agency functions.
who-dpi-h-reference-architecture-draft-v1#sec-001-112-understanding-digital-public-infrastructure, p20
pcposs-is-a-category-of-systemsA PCPOSS is a category spanning several application types, not one product
The primary health care handbook's person-centred point of service system (PCPOSS) is an umbrella: community-based information systems, decision support systems, electronic medical or health record systems and personal health records are all PCPOSS. It is spelled 'person-centred' and, in that handbook, 'point of service' without hyphens (POS is point of service, never point of sale). Do not equate a PCPOSS with an EMR. The DRAFT Reference Architecture uses a different label, 'point-of-service applications' (glossary entry marked [Under review]), and places them outside DPI-H.		terminology · major
PCPOSS encompass various services and application types, including community-based information systems, decision support systems, electronic medical (or health) record systems and personal health records

master-data-excludes-instance-dataProduct master data excludes batch numbers, serial numbers and manufacturing dates
In the product catalogue handbook, product master data is the information that stays the same for every instance of a product: description, dosage, strength, manufacturer details, shelf life. Batch or lot numbers, manufacturing dates and serial numbers vary per instance and are transactional data. They are the identifiers a traceability discussion reaches for first, which is why they get mislabelled as master data.		terminology · major
In contrast, transactional data—such as batch numbers, manufacturing dates, and serial numbers—varies with each specific instance of the product.
9789240116191-eng#sec-003-introduction, p1 (PDF p. 10)
generic-product-means-no-manufacturer'Generic product' means manufacturer-independent information, not a generic medicine
The product catalogue handbook gives 'generic product', 'product' and 'product information' a handbook-specific meaning: the general identifying details of a health product, without any manufacturer-specific information. In pharmaceutical usage a 'generic' is an off-patent medicine from a non-originator manufacturer — a different idea. In catalogue prose, 'generic product' is the level above the trade item, not a market category.		terminology · minor
In this handbook, the terms ‘generic product,’ ‘product,’ or ‘product information’ pertain to the general identifying details of a health product, excluding any manufacturer-specific information.
9789240116191-eng#sec-002-glossary, pvii (PDF p. 8)
hscis-can-be-paperA health supply chain information system can be paper-based
The supply chain handbook defines a health supply chain information system (HSCIS) as a paper-based OR software tool, and defines an order management system the same way. 'Information system' therefore does not imply software, and an inventory of current HSCISs includes the paper forms. The handbook notes that the software tools are commonly called electronic logistics management information systems (eLMIS); eLMIS is the narrower term.		terminology · minor
A paper-based or software tool that supports the management and/or operations of the health supply chain.

national-product-catalogue-spelling'National product catalogue' in prose; 'catalog' survives in tool names and the 2024 handbook
The product catalogue handbook's title and body use 'catalogue' together with -ize spellings such as 'standardized' (the WHO pairing). Its own abbreviation list nevertheless expands NPC as 'National product catalog', and the earlier supply chain handbook writes 'National Product Catalog', capitalized, in its abbreviations and prose. The Product Catalog Management Tool (PCMT) is a proper name and keeps its spelling. In running prose write 'national product catalogue (NPC)', lower case, as the 2025 handbook does.		spelling · minor
This handbook complements the DIIG by providing a structured approach to implementing a national product catalogue (NPC) for standardized product master data management.

integration-is-not-interoperabilityIntegration is bespoke; interoperability is standards-based
Rests on the DRAFT Reference Architecture, whose glossary marks this entry [Under review]. The draft separates integration — a bespoke connection from one application component to another — from interoperability, which it defines as a standards-based capability of heterogeneous systems. The published handbooks define interoperability through shared application interfaces, value sets, concepts and standards, and do not define integration, so the contrast is the draft's. 'Integrated with' is not a synonym for 'interoperable with' in this register.		terminology · minor
Bespoke capability of connecting one application component to another [Under review]

refer-by-number-and-title-not-by-idPoint the reader to another part by its number or title, never by its machine id
The published handbooks send the reader to another part of the text by section number and, where it helps, its title: 'see section 3.2.1', 'see Table 4'. They never show the identifier a file or model gives that part. In a computable DTH every block, persona, process step and decision rule has such an id (a label like pers:health-worker or freq:FR-ID-01, a BPMN or DMN id like Task_4_1_SelectSoftwareAndVendor or Rule_DT02_01, a role code, a repository path). In reader text, write the title and link it to the part; leave the id in the link target and in the model files. Ids are fine where the reader needs them as data: a requirement code in a catalogue table, or a coded value a system stores. The same applies to tool notes such as hit policies or file names, which belong in the model, not the prose.		structure · major
Each user persona (generated previously – see section 3.2.1) should have a series of user stories
