cdisah-is-the-current-abbreviationThe current abbreviation is CDISAH
The second edition is the *Classification of Digital Interventions, Services and Applications in Health*, and the abbreviation it introduces itself with is **CDISAH**, with the leading C. Use CDISAH for the current classification and CDHI only when referring specifically to the 2018 first edition. Two things make this counterintuitive. CDHI is the abbreviation in wide circulation and in most secondary literature, so the familiar term is the wrong one. And the publication is not internally consistent: page 9 writes the bare form 'DISAH' once, while page 7 and the classification's own short link (bit.ly/CDISAH) use CDISAH. WHO's implementation authority settles it — `WorldHealthOrganization/smart-base` uses CDISAH throughout its FHIR artefacts (`input/fsh/codesystems/CDHIv2.fsh`, `input/fsh/conceptmaps/CDHIv1toCDHIv2.fsh`) and the bare form appears in no FSH or prose there. That divergence inside one publication is the plainest argument for this voice existing at all.		terminology · major

intervention-is-a-capability-not-softwareA digital health intervention is a capability, not a system
A digital health intervention is a discrete technology FUNCTIONALITY — a capability — such as decision support, targeted client communications or stock notification. The software that delivers it is a digital health application, or in the classification's terms a Service or Application type, such as an LMIS or an EMR. The two are different levels and an application typically delivers several interventions. Naming a product where an intervention belongs collapses the distinction the whole classification rests on.		terminology · critical
DIGITAL HEALTH INTERVENTION: A discrete technology functionality – or capability – designed to achieve a specific objective addressing a health system challenge. Examples of digital health interventions include decision support, targeted client communications, and stock notifications

bottleneck-is-specific-challenge-is-generalA bottleneck is specific; a health system challenge is general
A bottleneck is a gap or problem in the workflow of ONE health programme area, persona or process — DIIG's own example is health workers having difficulty tracking when pregnant women are due for an antenatal care visit. A health system challenge is the general form of that problem across any programme area, such as loss to follow-up. DIIG Chapter 3 maps the first onto the second, so treating them as synonyms destroys the step. The classification groups health system challenges into nine overarching categories: information, availability, quality, acceptability, utilization, efficiency, cost, accountability and equity.		terminology · major
BOTTLENECKS: Specific gaps or problems in the workflow of delivering health services specific to a health programme area, persona or process (for example, “health workers have difficulty keeping track of when pregnant women are due for an antenatal care visit”), in contrast to a health system challenge, which is a general representation of the problem across any health programme area (for example, “loss to follow-up”).

persons-is-the-grouping-term'Persons' is the classification's term, not 'patients'
The classification groups interventions by targeted primary user, and the group covering members of the public is *Persons* — potential or current users of health services, including prevention and wellness. The classification explicitly notes that 'patients', 'clients' and 'health service users' have been used for this group; they are prior terms, not the classification's. Use Persons when writing against the classification's groupings. Outside that context an ordinary word is fine; this rule is about the taxonomy, not about banning 'patient' from English.		terminology · minor
Interventions for Persons: Persons are members of the public who are potential or current users of health services, including health prevention and wellness activities. Other terms that have been used to describe individuals in this group include ‘patients’, ‘clients’ and ‘health service users’.

state-the-intervention-challenge-outcome-tripleAn intervention is stated against a challenge and an outcome
The classification's stated purpose is to let a planner express what they expect to achieve, and its worked form is a triple: one implements a digital health intervention IN ORDER TO address a health system challenge, TO ACHIEVE an outcome. Prose that names an intervention with no challenge and no outcome has described a capability and asserted nothing about why it was chosen — which is the gap DIIG Chapter 4 exists to close.		structure · major
one may implement a digital health intervention, such as “targeted communication to persons”, in order to address a health system challenge, such as “lack of service utilisation,” to achieve an overarching eHealth outcome of “improving individuals’ access to knowledge resources and support for better management of their health”

digital-health-supersedes-ehealth'Digital health' is the current term; 'eHealth' is its predecessor
DIIG defines digital health as the systematic application of information and communications technologies, computer science and data to support informed decision-making. Where its glossary carries the older term it marks it as an alternative — a digital health outcome 'may also be known as an eHealth outcome', a digital health strategy as an eHealth strategy. Prefer digital health; keep eHealth when quoting a source that uses it, or when naming a national strategy by its actual title.		terminology · minor
DIGITAL HEALTH OUTCOME: The desired change in the health system or services by using digital health interventions. May also be known as an eHealth outcome

implementation-investment-and-plan-are-distinctImplementation, investment and costed plan are three things
A digital health IMPLEMENTATION is the development and deployment of applications or platforms with its governance framework, operational plan and human resources. A digital health INVESTMENT is the financial and human resources directed towards implementations. A COSTED IMPLEMENTATION PLAN is the document describing challenges with a financially justified deployment and monitoring plan, and it is what DIIG exists to produce. Using them interchangeably makes it impossible to say whether a sentence is about work, money or a document.		terminology · major
accompanied by a governance framework, operational plan, human resources and related activities for its successful execution

first-edition-title-was-wrong-and-is-reissuedThe first edition's title was incorrect and is being reissued
WHO states that the first edition was published under the wrong title. It went out as *Classification of digital health interventions v1.0* under reference WHO/RHR/18.06 — the document ingested here as `who-rhr-1806-eng` — and is being reissued under the correct title, *Classification of digital interventions, services and applications in health*. So the two editions do not differ only by content: the earlier NAME is disowned by the publisher. When citing the first edition, cite it by its reference number and say which title you are using, because a reader who looks it up may find either. Do not silently modernise a v1 citation to the new title, and do not treat the v1 title as merely superseded — WHO calls it incorrect.		citation · major
The first edition of this publication was published under the following title “Classification of digital health interventions v1.0: a shared language to describe the uses of digital technology for health” with a WHO reference number - WHO/RHR/18.06. The first edition is being reissued under the correct title
