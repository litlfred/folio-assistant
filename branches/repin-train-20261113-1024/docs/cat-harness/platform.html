The platform

The platform half of folio-assistant: the skills, schemas, processes and MCP server an agent uses to author a folio. It holds no subject matter. A paper, a WHO SMART Guideline, an Implementation Guide — those live in their own repositories and layers, and this one is the machinery they run on.

One sentence, and every word in it is a declared object

An actor performs a task in a process as a role, using that role’s skills.

The five nouns are five different things:

what it iswhy it is not the one next to it
Actora concrete participant — a person, an agent session, a scheduled jobpersists across processes; a role does not
Rolethe BPMN swimlane — a persona an actor takes on for the duration of a lanenobody is a reviewer; somebody acts as one, here, now
Taskone activity in one diagramthe unit of work, not the ability to do it
Processthe diagram itself: lanes bind roles, activities name skills, gateways may compute a branchthe shape of the work, not an instance of it
Skillthe instructions the actor needs to perform the taskknowledge, not permission — what an actor may do is a W3C ODRL policy in policies/

The full model is the role-model skill.

Processes — every one is BPMN, and the diagrams are executable

The .bpmn file is the source of truth: workflow_start, workflow_next, workflow_gate and workflow_complete run it, workflow_complete refuses a step that holds no token, and the running instance is stored so a sibling session sees the same position. A diagram that disagrees with what the tools do is therefore a defect in the diagram.

Entry point or step inside another — the distinction is derived, not labelled. A process that no other diagram calls is somewhere work begins: you enter it deliberately. A process named by a callActivity somewhere else is a step inside that larger one, and entering it directly means starting in the middle of something. Nothing marks this in the file; it falls out of who calls whom, which is why it stays true as diagrams are added.

For example, Content acquisition is an entry point: nothing calls it. Adjudication is not — it is called from the Translation Workflow, from the Ingestion subprocess — the L1 completeness gate, and from Refresh materialized remote content, and is what one of those reaches when it needs a decision. Criterion adjudication is a level further in, called by Wireframe design review and Voice overlay review. A process whose title sounds like a verb is not evidence that you may enter it; ask the index who calls it.

Before you open one:

  • A lane is an accountability boundary. When a token crosses into another lane, who is answerable for the next step changes, which is why “say which process you are in” is a rule rather than a courtesy.
  • An activity names the skill that performs it. A step whose skill is missing is a finding rather than a blank.
  • A gateway may be computed. Where a branch is backed by a DMN table, the agent supplies the facts and the table returns the branch. A hand-supplied outcome is refused, so nobody talks their way past a gate.

Every process, with its lanes, its activities and the skills they name → That index is generated from the diagrams on every build, so it is what the repository contains rather than what this page remembers.

Roles, tasks and skills

Skills are knowledge-graph content, not a directory to memorise. An agent asks for one — skill_list for what exists, skill_fetch for a named skill’s instructions — and a layer inherits its dependencies’ skills through the same declaration, so what is reachable depends on where you are standing.

Every skill, by sub-graph →

Roles are declared in scenarios/roles.json and bound to lanes by the diagrams; what an actor may do is an ODRL policy in policies/, because it cross-cuts the lanes the actor acts in. bun run cat kg:audit checks one criterion per join between them and writes its findings as sidecars rather than console output, because a printed verdict is gone, and without it “unbound since it was drawn” and “broken in the commit under review” look the same.

Every task, by the process that draws it →

A role is as likely to be mechanical as human. Adjudicator and Business analyst are personas a person or an agent takes on; Build pipeline, Attestation service and Activity log are lanes held by something that is not a person at all, and they share one list because a swimlane does not care.

Tasks are the individual steps inside a diagram — Read the sidecar findings, Classify what changed, Accept, or send back, Authorise the extraction. There are an order of magnitude more tasks than processes, which is why the task index is a reference rather than a reading list. Open it when you want to know which diagram a step belongs to; the lane it sits in says who is answerable for it.

What the handler publishes

/cat-harness/ is a handler namespace. Every route under it is <base>/<handler>/<kind>/ — the platform rendering one declared graph — and the handler segment is read from the layer’s declared name, never composed. <base>/<instance>/ is a layer presenting itself; where the two rules meet at one path, the handler wins.

what it showsgenerated by
auto-docsderived indexes over a sub-graph — processes and skillsgen-auto-docs.ts
librarythe L1 corpus: what is ingested, how far, what references it, and what is still queuedgen-library-viz.ts
schemasthe schema graph — declarations and the edges between themgen-schema-viz.ts
voicesevery voice a layer declares, and the passage each rule was read fromgen-voices-viz.ts

Each is regenerated from the tree it describes, so a stale one is a build problem rather than an editing problem. This page is not: if you change how processes, roles or skills relate to each other, change it here too.
