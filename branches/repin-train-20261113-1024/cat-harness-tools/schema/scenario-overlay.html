Description

Roles, actors and capabilities EXTENDED BY ID from a higher layer's own scenarios/.

A role→skill edge naming a skill that belongs above the harness has to live in the higher layer, pointing down; otherwise the harness holds an UPWARD reference.

The rule — the dependent holds the pointer

As a story points at the role it is told as, a higher layer's scenarios/ may:

| what | how | what it may change | |---|---|---| | extend a ROLE | roles.json → extensions: [{ "role": "<id>", "skills": […] }] | adds skills; nothing else | | add a ROLE | roles.json → roles: […], a NEW id | its own role; may inherits a lower one | | extend an ACTOR | actors/<file>.json with "extends": "<actor id>" | adds roles and capabilities; nothing else | | add an ACTOR | actors/<file>.json without extends, a NEW id | its own actor | | extend a CAPABILITY | capabilities/<file>.json with "extends": "<capability id>" | adds requires; nothing else | | add a CAPABILITY | capabilities/<file>.json, a NEW id | its own probe | | add a POLICY | policies/<file>.jsonld, a NEW uid | its own ODRL rules, e.g. the grants of the actors it adds |

Id-matching is the same rule as a directory override and as inherits. Redeclaring a lower id is refused, not merged, so the lower layer's audit and the checkout's agree on what the role IS. An extension may only name something declared BELOW it.

The one exception: a move in flight

Moving a role, actor, capability or policy UP is two pull requests: the higher layer adds it, then the lower layer removes it. A redeclaration that only adds (onlyAdds) is therefore accepted with a warning, and the higher declaration is used: every field of the lower one is present unchanged (scalars equal, array members kept, nested objects recursively), and the higher may carry more. _-keys are not compared. The warning names the id and both layers and says to remove the lower copy. A redeclaration that changes or drops anything is refused.

Skill front matter does not carry roles:; the edge stays on the role graph, spread across layers.

Where the layers come from

The base is the layer asked about; the layers are the CHECKOUT's layers that depend on it (checkoutDependentsOf), deepest first. Resolved alone, a layer sees only its own graph.

Auditing ONE layer asks the other direction: which actors may its requirements and lanes name? Those are the platform's, every layer it needs, and its own (neededActors) — never a dependent's.

Policies travel with the actors they grant

An actor a higher layer adds holds its grants in THAT layer's policies/. checkoutPolicies and neededPolicies read the same layers as the actor overlay, by the same rule: a new uid is added, a redeclared one is refused unless it only adds. A policy may inheritFrom a lower one; it cannot edit it.
