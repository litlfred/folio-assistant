Description

A DRAWN figure, assembled and rendered — the step above vector-labels.json.

Where it sits

| | measurement, machine-derived | judgement, someone looked | |---|---|---| | raster | <entry>/images.json | <library>/image-verdicts.json | | vector labels | <entry>/vector-labels.json | <library>/figure-descriptions.json | | vector figures | <entry>/vector-figures.json + figures/*.png | <library>/image-verdicts.json, keyed vfig-pNNN |

The figures share the RASTER verdict file, because the call made about them is the raster call — figure or furniture, and if figure, what it shows. apply-image-verdicts.ts writes both through the same inspection basis.

The basis is ALWAYS present, and the extractor never assigns a role

Every entry carries a basis naming who or what looked:

  • assembly — pdf-vector-figures.py grouped the page and rendered it. It assigns no role: role is undetermined, since no coverage threshold separates a figure from furniture.
  • inspection — somebody looked at the render and said what it is (the raster schema's InspectionBasisSchema).

So undetermined ⇔ assembly, structurally: here it means "the machine looked and decided nothing", unlike images.json where it means no basis.

shows is how a figure is reached, and it is the inspector's to set

VectorFigureSchema.captionLabels are CANDIDATES: every line-start Fig. N on the page, cross-references included. Only an inspector can say which figure the render holds, so image-descriptions counts a declared figure as reached by this arm only through shows on an inspected entry.

The assembly is structure, not prose

Groups are connected components of touching drawing rectangles; boxes are closed shapes holding a label, with parent by containment; connectors are open strokes whose two ends land in labelled boxes. Every rule is a fact about the drawing — the only length used is the stroke's own width — and pdf-vector-figures.py carries each. A connector end that lands in no box is null, not snapped to the nearest.
