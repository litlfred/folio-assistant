Description

The positioned TEXT on a page that declares a figure: the labels of a figure that is DRAWN rather than placed.

Not images.json

pdf-images.py recovers the RASTER layer: image objects the PDF places. Conceptual figures — frameworks, maturity models, process flows — are drawn in path operators and text, with no image object. Forcing them into DocumentImageSchema would make role and basis mean two things (no rectangle, no xref, no pixel). So this is its own sidecar, vector-labels.json, joined to images by page number only.

What it refuses to do

It does not decide which labels belong to the figure. Intersection with a drawing is not a reliable test (labels, axis titles and legends often sit in the white space between boxes), so every line on a qualifying page is recorded, and VectorLabelSchema.intersectsDrawing is stored as a measurement, not used as a filter.

It does not group. A label is one MuPDF line, and nothing above that: MuPDF's blocks merge labels that are far apart. Boxes, layers and arrows are inference. A description from labels alone is safe as an INVENTORY and unsafe as an ACCOUNT.

It does not claim reading order. VectorFigurePageSchema.labels is ordered by top edge then left edge: a deterministic, stated convention so a re-run diffs cleanly.

It does not count. No ratio of labels to declared figures is computed; they are not commensurable.

Which pages qualify — over-inclusion is chosen

A page is recorded when it carries a line-start Fig./Fig/Figure + number that is not a table-of-contents entry, and has a vector layer at all. That is looser than declaredFigureCaptions (which also requires a period and whitespace): captions such as Fig. 13<tab>Example have no period, and a false positive costs a page of prose while a false negative loses a figure's labels.

The table-of-contents guard is structural. A figure-list entry carries a DOT LEADER — two periods separated by nothing but space. It is NOT "the line ends in a number", which would drop real captions ending in a year.

Known limit: a figure whose caption sits on the facing page is not recorded at all.
