Description

QA review — findings, decisions, and the audit notes that justify them.

Three entities, not one. Two vocabularies exist for what looks like a single act:

content-review skill output:  decision: approve | request-changes | reject
                              severity: blocking | suggestion | praise
QA sidecar entry:             result:   pass | fail | warn | n/a
                              severity: critical | major | minor

A finding is an observation; a decision is an act about findings

Neither determines the other. A critical mechanical finding can be overruled by someone with the standing to overrule it, and an approve can be issued over findings that are still open: a defect nobody is going to fix, and an approval granted in spite of one, both need a record.

The two severity vocabularies are two axes, not two spellings

FindingSeverity (critical | major | minor) says what kind of breakage this is: a broken reference, a missing join, a coverage gap. It is a property of the defect and a machine can assign it.

FindingWeight (blocking | suggestion | praise) says what the reviewer asks of the gate. It is a stance, not a measurement; praise has no equivalent on the machine axis.

So a finding carries whichever axis its reviewer can speak on, and checkReview requires the right one rather than defaulting a mechanical finding into a stance nobody took.

An overruled finding stays recorded

Overruling is not deletion. The Finding is immutable once raised; the Decision records the overrule, and every overrule of a mechanical finding carries an AuditNote saying why.

An audit note cites

AuditNote.cites carries Citations into the corpus — a witness, another finding, a QA report, a block, a file range, a bibliographic key, a workflow instance. Agents are expected to propose those citations from corpus evidence (AuditNote.proposed_by); a human confirming a proposed note is recorded as its author, because the reasoning is theirs.

Citation resolution has three outcomes, and one of them is not a pass

resolveCitations returns resolved, dangling or not-checked. not-checked is what you get when no corpus resolver was supplied, or when the resolver itself could not answer — a shallow clone, an absent submodule. It is never rendered as resolved. Same rule as check-ci-health.ts, readme-sections.ts and kg-qa.ts.
