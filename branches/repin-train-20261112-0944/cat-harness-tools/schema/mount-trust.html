Description

MOUNT TRUST — what must be true before a remote knowledge graph is mounted.

Rule H8 of skills/conduct/security/zero-trust-handover.md: a remote mount needs a trusted, signed provenance source or explicit user consent; staging needs neither. A mounted skill steers an agent and mounted code runs, so mounting is a supply-chain decision, not a fetch.

Three ways a mount may proceed, and one it may not

| basis | when | state | |---|---|---| | staging | the mount is for a staging preview | allowed, recorded as such | | consent | a person consented to THIS harness at THIS pin | allowed | | signature | a signature verifiable through a declared trust network | could not determine: no verifier exists yet, so a recorded signature is reported and NOT trusted | | none | unsigned and unconsented | refused |

Consent is scoped to the pin. A moved pin is different code, so it asks again — unless a declared STANDING consent covers it (below).

An unverified signature is not trust. Until a verifier for the declared network exists (GDHCN is the named example; its trust-list model belongs to a layer above this layer), a signature-only mount is could-not-determine, which never mounts.

What the lock keeps, and who may consent

  • The basis is recorded. Every locked layer carries the basis it was mounted on (MountTrustBasisSchema): staging (the caller's --staging flag) or consent with who and when. mount:remote:check re-runs mountTrust against the CURRENT declaration and reports a mount whose recorded basis no longer holds.
  • Who consented is checked when the layer says who may. A layer may declare mountApprovers; then consent.by must be one of them. With no list, the verdict says approver: "unverified", which callers report as its own state, never as clean.
  • A gitlink is not covered by its parent's consent. A layer reached through a gitlink needs consent of its own, given on the downstream's override for that layer.

Standing consent

Each site keeps its own lock. A site may say, once, per upstream layer, "keep me on <branch> of this upstream" — a StandingConsentSchema on that layer's trust.standing (the remote source's own trust, or overrides.<instance>.trust for a layer reached through a gitlink). It names the person who gave it and when.

A standing consent does not trust a pin by itself, and a hand-moved pin is still refused. It lets the repin tool, having resolved the followed branch's newest (or, with require: "green", newest green) commit, WRITE a per-pin consent for the new pin, attributed to the standing rule's person and marked consent.standing (withStandingConsent). mountTrust accepts that pin only while the rule it names is still declared, unchanged, on the same layer; withdraw or edit the rule and every pin recorded under it asks again.
