This page describes how a Personal Health Wallet handles consent. Consent follows IHE Privacy Consent on FHIR (PCF) 1.1.0. Each trust domain sets the consent policies that apply in it. The processes on this page are trust-anchor neutral: GDHCN is one possible trust anchor among others.

Two kinds of consent

Each consent records its kind in Consent.category with a code from HL7 v3 ActConsentDirective (CodeSystem v3-ActCode). HL7 defines every one of these codes; this guide adds none.

Explicit consentImplied consent
What it isThe holder agrees, or refuses, by their own act: on their wallet, or on a form a health worker records.The holder does nothing. The trust domain’s default policy applies.
Kind (Consent.category, HL7 ActConsentDirective)OPTIN (opt-in), OPTINR (opt-in with restrictions), OPTOUT (opt-out), OPTOUTE (opt-out with exceptions): PHW Explicit Consent KindIMPLIED (implied consent), IMPLIEDD (implied consent with opportunity to dissent), NOPP (notice of privacy practices): PHW Implied Consent Kind
How it is recordedA PHW Explicit Consent in the Consent Registry (process L, IHE PCF [ITI-108]).Not recorded per holder. A document may carry it as a PHW Implied Consent.
The policyConsent.policyRule: the trust domain’s consent code with its version, when the domain publishes consent codes. Otherwise Consent.policy.uri: the IHE PCF implicit policy the consent changes.Consent.policy.uri: the trust domain’s default policy, normally an IHE PCF implicit policy (Policy-basic-normal, Policy-all-normal, Policy-break-glass-only or Policy-deny).
The evidence (Consent.source[x])The holder’s recorded act: the wallet confirmation or the signed form.The trust domain’s published policy.
StateConsent.status (FHIR R4): proposed, active, rejected, inactive (withdrawn or expired), entered-in-error.The same.

IHE PCF does not allow Consent.verification, so the kind is carried in category. Both kinds are IHE PCF Intermediate Consents (PHW Consent).

An opt-in trust domain has Policy-deny as its default, so data is shared only with explicit consent. An opt-out trust domain has Policy-basic-normal or Policy-all-normal as its default, so data is shared unless the holder dissents.

The kind and ODRL

Consent is decided by translating it to W3C ODRL 2.2 (consentToOdrl() in fhir-harness), where each IHE PCF implicit policy is an ODRL Set. The kind says which of those Sets a consent builds on, through ODRL’s inheritFrom:

KindBuilds on (odrl:inheritFrom)What the consent adds
OPTIN, OPTINRPolicy-denyIts permissions (OPTINR: with the holder’s restrictions as prohibitions or constraints)
OPTOUTPolicy-denyNothing: the holder refuses
OPTOUTEPolicy-basic-normalIts prohibitions, with the holder’s exceptions as permissions
IMPLIED, IMPLIEDD, NOPPIts own Consent.policyNothing: the implied consent is its policy’s Set

So a receiver, or the Sharer’s decision point, can tell from the code alone what holds when no permission matches. Reading category this way is a proposed change to consentToOdrl(), which today reads policy and policyRule only.

Trust domains and their consent codes

A trust domain may name its own consent policies as a terms-of-use ValueSet (TrustDomain.termsOfUse in the GDHCN Trust IG). Each code’s definition is the text the holder is shown. An explicit consent names one of these codes in policyRule. A VHL Receiver’s credential names one in its termsOfUse (see process I). The Sharer finds the trust domain from the ValueSet that holds the code.

For the IPS Pilgrimage trust domain, the codes are IPS.HAJJ.CONSENT in the IPS Pilgrimage guide, for example CONSENT-MYS and CONSENT-KSA. The worked example shows the two Hajj consents as PilgrimConsentOriginRelease and PilgrimConsentHostUse.

Consent directives in shared and issued documents

A document that is shared or issued may carry the consent directives it is shared under, explicit or implied. Carrying them is optional: the trust domain, or the holder in the wallet, decides. Process M adds them before the document is signed. Process B calls it at step B5a and process J at step J2a.

  • Each directive is a consent entry of the document Bundle (IPS document that carries its consent directives).
  • The Composition’s Advance Directives section (LOINC 42348-3) lists them, with a summary that a person can read.
  • The copy in the document is evidence. The sharer enforces the copy in its own Consent Registry. A signed document cannot show a later withdrawal.

LinaSummaryWithConsent is an example. Lina’s patient summary carries her explicit consent, an OPTIN (LinaExplicitConsent), and the trust domain’s default, an IMPLIEDD under Policy-basic-normal (LinaImpliedConsent). Every person in the examples is fictional.

Deciding access

When a link is redeemed (process C) or a request comes under an agreement (processes F and I), the sharer reads the active Consents from its registry with [ITI-108]. It translates them to W3C ODRL 2.2 and decides permit, deny or unknown. A missing, expired or unreachable consent never gives permit. The decision is recorded as W3C PROV-O provenance that cites Consent.id and Consent.meta.versionId.
