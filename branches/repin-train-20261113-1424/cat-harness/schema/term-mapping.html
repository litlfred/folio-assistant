Description

Whether a glossary term the extractor MINTED already exists as a concept somebody else is authoritative for.

glossary-extract.ts mints a candidate from every knowledge-graph asset carrying a title and a description, asserted by the extraction and checked against nothing. This compares each one against existing terminology, using the model [vocabulary-authority](../skills/kg/kg-core/vocabulary-authority.md) defines: SKOS the hub, exactMatch / closeMatch / broadMatch / relatedMatch the predicates.

TWO targets, and they are not interchangeable

| target | authoritative for | resolved against | |---|---|---| | skos | what a term MEANS | SKOS concept schemes — the layer's own authored terms, plus any external scheme it declares | | fhir | a clinical code's OPERATIONAL semantics | the pinned published IG snapshot (cat-harness/external-schemas/who-smart-base.terminology.json), a published IG at a version, not a live collection |

A SKOS match and a FHIR match are different assertions with different authorities, so a result carries which target it came from and the two are never merged into one verdict. OCL is for FHIR only, never an authority on meaning.

Three states

mapped · unmapped · undetermined. The third is NEVER rendered as unmapped: a terminology that could not be reached has said nothing. The fhir half resolves offline against the pinned snapshot, so both targets return determined verdicts whenever the pin and snapshot are present and valid; an absent or unusable snapshot yields undetermined with its reason.

exact and concept are a PAIR

Both are always present, and the GAP between them is the finding. From [consensus-grounded-subject-evaluation](../methodologies/consensus-grounded-subject-evaluation.md): a high concept score with a low exact score means the right idea under the wrong authorised label — a different outcome from no match at all. The meanings are SKOS's own:

  • exact — the normalised prefLabel matches a concept's prefLabel.
  • concept — the normalised label matches that concept's prefLabel or any of its altLabels: the same concept reached by a different name.

Normalisation: NFC, lower-case, whitespace collapsed, a trailing period stripped.

Validated in memory, committed as a projection

The full file — two rows per candidate — is built and validated against this schema on every run (so an undetermined row with no reason is refused), then PROJECTED into test/results/term-mapping.qa-results.json, which keeps the per-target summaries and only the rows a reader can act on.

It reports and never grades

No ratio of mapped to total is computed: an unmapped candidate may be a term this corpus is right to coin, and a mapped one a coincidence of wording.
