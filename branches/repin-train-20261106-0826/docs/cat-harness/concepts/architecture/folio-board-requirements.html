What this is

The requirements agreed for the folio board, with their acceptance criteria and the reasons that constrain an implementation. The rules an agent follows live in the cat-harness skills this set produced — board-windows, board-diagram-interchange, harness-tiles — and the processes in the executable BPMN diagrams beside them. Where this page and a skill disagree, the skill wins.

The two constraints everything follows

  • The todos/ ↔ cat-harness split — the board’s content sits under todos/; the schema and behaviour of the folio board are in cat-harness, with tools kept distinct from schema.
  • Always collapsible to a linear rendering — an accessibility floor. A board that cannot be read linearly cannot be read by a screen reader, printed or translated.

Notes move around the board, attach to nothing or to content nodes by their relationships, and show on content (or its avatar) as an icon with a count badge when more than one.

R1 — the board renders content, not only notes

The board SHALL render content nodes alongside notes, and a note attached to a content node SHALL render at that node rather than at an independent position.

Accepted when a board with one note attached to a block and one attached to nothing renders both — the first at its block, the second at its declared position. Removing the attachment moves the note to its position rather than hiding it: “attached to nothing” is an existing declared state, not a new one.

R2 — semantic zoom, with a declared threshold

As the board shrinks, a card SHALL stop rendering its words and render its avatar instead. The threshold SHALL be declared data, not a literal in the renderer.

Accepted when the card renders text at a declared size and its avatar below it, a kind with no avatar takes GENERIC rather than rendering blank, and the behaviour is falsified in both directions — a test that only checks the shrunk case passes for a board that is always avatars.

Consumer burden: the threshold is emitted, never documented. “We don’t need to emit it — the renderer knows” is the smell this phase names.

R3 — targetLabel SHALL publish its parts

The published todo index SHALL emit a note’s page and node as separate fields, not only the composite targetLabel. The value is sec:<page>-<node>, page-qualified because a bare node id can exist on two pages; node ids contain -, so the composite cannot be split without the page list.

R4 — the linear floor

The board SHALL always be collapsible to a linear rendering, reachable without XSS: the linear rendering MAY fetch and build its content client-side, and MUST NOT be reachable by anything that executes markup it did not author. Movement SHALL be keyboard-operable; drag MAY be an accelerator but SHALL NOT be the only way in — this instance’s declared interaction profile is low-dexterity.

R4 binds the board. Its linear floor is the todo listing, asserted by the tools layer’s test/linear-floor.e2e.ts. The docs site is bound by ui-accessibility (WCAG 2.2 A/AA), which also states what a client-side rendering owes: print and PDF wait for load and render, and a load that fails says so rather than rendering as empty.

R5 to R7 — the badge

The badge SHALL show a count only when more than one note is attached (R5); the badge and its panel SHALL be two readings of one query rather than a number somebody maintains (R6); and an avatar carries the same badge from the same query as the card it replaced (R7). A badge cannot disagree with its own panel, by derivation rather than by discipline.

Cards start closed. A closed card shows its words above the declared threshold and its avatar below it, with the avatar present at every width as the control that opens the window (R2 with R9).

R17 — render safety

Assume assets in the KG are accessible, lazy-load, and render dynamically where possible, without XSS. The Url half is schemas/safe-url.ts, default-deny, with a source-level check that a renderer ignoring it fails a test. A Tool-output render vocabulary is not defined, because nothing renders a tool’s output: a safety property with no consumer to check it is not declared.

A content library is not a folio. The IRIS replica and the smart-* instances are things a reader browses; each may implement its own landing page and its own harness. What travels with the reader between them is the folio — their notes, materialised assets and links — and it looks and behaves the same in each because it belongs to cat-harness. Consistency is the requirement, not similarity: two harnesses that each build a good folio have failed R24.

An asset has three states, not two — R30

requirement
R30an asset SHALL have three distinguishable states: in the library (not the reader’s), in folio/ but not displayed (materialised, theirs, off the glass), and on the glass (displayed in the pulled-down folio). Closing it from the glass returns it to the second state, never the first; returning it to the first is a separate act performed from the library

A two-state model makes closing a sticky and un-materialising an asset the same gesture, so a reader tidying their glass silently discards work. The middle state makes closing safe, and the inverse of close is reachable from a different surface (the library) than the one that closed it.

A sticky’s face — R31 and R32

requirementin the requester’s words
R31a closed sticky SHALL show its condensed own text — whitespace, newlines, bullets and list markers stripped. No second string“no, not hidden label, that’s new data to maintain… just the condensend text”, “(strip whitesaplnce, newlines, bullets….)”
R32a sticky SHALL still be about 2.5 physical inches on a large MacBook screen“sticky should stilll be ~2.5" in large macbook screen”

R31: no second string. A hidden accessible label beside the visible summary is new data to maintain, and a label that disagrees with the visible text is a defect no reader can see.

R32 is a physical measurement, and CSS cannot express it. A CSS in is 96 CSS px, not an inch of glass. A 16” MacBook Pro presents 1728 CSS px across about 13.6” of width, so one physical inch is roughly 127 CSS px and 2.5” is about 318px: 20rem. 2.5in would render about 1.9”. The device is named in the code comment because the conversion is device-dependent: the same 20rem is about 3.7 physical inches on a 27” 1440p display, which is correct, since the size is chosen for the screen the requester uses.

R21 and R32 are a pair. “Small” without R32 shrinks the card until its text is unreadable; R32 without “small” leaves the dead space R18 was about.

R19 — tile or avatar

the rule
R19ba thing renders as a tile iff the harness declares it; as an avatar iff the folio holds it (a note, a document, a materialised asset in library/ or uploads/). A theme is how either one looks, never a third kind
R19ca tile is one of two kinds. A functional tile (a directory or sub-graph) is the visual interface to a skill, implemented by a tool chosen from potentially several — so it names the skill, never the tool. A content tile stands for a set of schema instances

R19c is a separate requirement because R19b stays true but is insufficient: who declared it answers tile or avatar and stops, and the two kinds of tile bind to different things, resolve different things when opened, and mean opposite things when empty:

functionalcontent
bound toa skilla schema
resolved on opena tool implementing itthe instances
empty meansa gap to report — a skill nothing implements is unreachablea real state — “nothing outstanding” is not “broken”

A tile names the skill, never the tool. schemas/tool-types.ts exists to make tools interchangeable, so a tile bound to a tool breaks the moment another is chosen. VisualiserDeclarationSchema takes one path or several visualisations, so a sub-graph may be rendered more than one way exactly as a skill may be implemented more than one way.

R30 puts avatars of materialised assets, including whole knowledge graphs on the glass, so size does not decide tile or avatar; declaration ownership does. The rule is preferred because it is already in the corpus: harness-tiles says a tile is the harness’s, not the node’s. The rule lives in harness-tiles, with a pointer from board-windows. There is no check: nothing classifies a folio item yet, so a gate would be a declared property whose check cannot answer its own claim.

R27 — where materialised assets live

The knowledge graph is static and modelled by reference; materialisation is a separate, gated, checked act that produces bytes:

the content core’s schemas/materialization.tsthree states — referenced (we know where, we hold no bytes), materialized (the bytes are here), unknown (we have not established which) — with no default, plus five gates, and localPath present iff materialized
cat-harness/processes/library/materialize-remote.bpmnthe act itself, as an executable STRICT process running five gates in a fixed order; unknown on any one keeps the node referenced
skills/kg/kg-core/directory-conventions.mdthe catalogue graph typology — “a remote catalogue modelled BY REFERENCE … Distinct from library: that is content which IS here, this is the shape of a collection of which almost none is”

Materialised bytes land in library/ and uploads/, not in a folio/ directory. The folio is the reader’s repository, of which library/ and uploads/ are declared parts, each marked as the reader’s own copy (the perInstance flag on the kind). Two reasons rule out folio/ itself: corpus-grep searches library/ only, so a node materialised elsewhere reads as absent, and folio/ is the renderable typology, so it would put large PDFs into the site build. The content core’s scripts/check-catalogue.ts checks that a materialized claim names bytes that exist, with local-path.ts’s three states (ok / missing / unknown).

Still open

The library view renders all three states. gen-iris-pages.ts emits materialized, referenced and unknown as distinct badges, defaulting to unknown, never to referenced. Catalogue pages are generated at build time, so check the published site, not the source tree. What is missing is one action: the way from a library row onto the glass (state 1 → 3). The glass (R25) and its pull-out action are one unit of work.

Folio surfaces on library pages are blocked on R25. A replica library page is complete standalone HTML from its own generator — no Jekyll, no head_custom.html — because it is a faithful replica. Loading docs-ui.js there is safe (every mount guards itself), and nothing mounts:

surfacerequireson a replica page
launchera sidebar headerabsent
board#main-content / .main-content / <main>, inserted at the topabsent, and the top is the IRIS content
language bar.main-contentabsent

Every visible folio surface is bound to just-the-docs page furniture, and giving a library page that furniture would make it look like folio-assistant — the one thing a replica exists not to do.

The delivery mechanism is an exported mount fragment plus a gate, as staging-banner.ts does, reusing bodyInsertionPoint(). A pull-down glass comes down over what is being browsed, so it is the only folio surface that does not need the host page to look like the harness; it comes first, because shipping the mount fragment before it would create a gate for a thing with no consumer.

A board is a diagram OF a folio

In the OMG sense that a BPMNDiagram is a diagram of a bpmn:process: the folio carries what is true; the layout layer carries where it was drawn; the arrow runs folio → board → position → note and never back. board-positions may not put x/y on a note, and a folio is complete with no board — deleting every board loses layout and no content.
