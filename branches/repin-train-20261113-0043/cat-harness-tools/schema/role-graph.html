Description

Actors, Roles and Skills — who does a BPMN task, and what they need to know.

  • Actor — a concrete participant. Human or agentic. litlfred is an actor; so is the ingestion engine. An actor exists independently of any process and persists across all of them.
  • Role — a persona an actor takes on because of the lane it is acting in. Reviewer is a role; nobody is a reviewer outside a process. A role carries a collection of Skills. A lane names the Role its tasks are performed in, not the actor.
  • Skill — a responsibility an actor carries out to complete a task: as instructions to follow, as a contract to be tested against, or both.

So: an actor performs a task in a process as a role, using that role's skills. Every word in that sentence is a distinct object here, and the audit in scripts/kg-audit.ts checks each join in it.

A lane names a role because the same actor acts in different lanes of different diagrams (Lane_Agent in crdm-requirements.bpmn, Lane_Sibling in bean-lifecycle.bpmn), and its skills differ with the lane.

Actors carry roles, not inherits

An actor names the roles it may take on in roles[]; the inherits lattice belongs to roles. readActors reports an actor carrying inherits via LoadedActor.looksLikeRole, and the actor-is-not-a-role criterion fails on one.

roles: [] and an absent roles are different: [] says the actor takes on no role (a read-only identity that never appears in a swimlane), while absent says nothing has been asserted.

Hierarchy: roles compose down a subprocess chain

A process calls a subprocess, and the actor keeps acting. It does not stop being the outer role; it additionally takes on the inner lane's role. So the skills available at a task are the union along the whole call path. resolveRoleStack computes that union and keeps the path, so a reader sees which role supplied each skill.

Two distinct compositions, deliberately not merged:

  • inherits — a role IS-A role. qc-reviewer inherits reviewer, and gets its skills, everywhere, in every process. Static.
  • the subprocess stack — a role is acting INSIDE another role's task. Dynamic, and only for the duration of that call.

Merging them would give reviewer every skill any caller ever had.

A lane binds its role explicitly

Lane names are free text, so a LANE names the role it binds — see laneRoleRef. The role does not list its lanes: a general node never names its users (data-modelling step 8). An unbound lane is a finding.

The binding is BPMN's own: the lane's partitionElementRef points at a top-level <bpmn:resource> whose id IS the role id. <bootstrap.processes:role ref> is still accepted, with a warning.

Roles played by mechanical actors are real roles

A build pipeline, a data store, a log or a registry plays a role, and that role's skills are its contract: the responsibilities it must perform, which a test can hold it to.

Three states

Absent declaration → undefined, and callers fall back to documented defaults. Present but unparseable → throws, because a role graph nobody can read leaves every consumer assigning tasks to nobody. An unknown actorKinds or a dangling inherits → rejected at read, not accepted and ignored.
