WHO publication design (Western Pacific Region)

who-publication-design · shipped by who-iris · who-iris/skills/voices/who-publication-design · every who-iris voice

IRI: https://litlfred.github.io/folio-assistant/who-iris/voice/who-publication-design · JSON-LD

The visual conventions around the prose rather than the prose itself: logo use, palette, typography, pagination and figure accessibility, as set out in the WHO Western Pacific Region publication and information products style guide. This is the one voice whose rules mostly do not touch a block's text, which is why it is a separate voice rather than more rules on who-editorial — a folio can want WHO's editorial register without WPRO's brand.

who-des-figure-colour-accessibilityNever red with green, never blue with yellow
The guide's one hard accessibility rule for figures, and the one that matters most for a generated chart: do not combine red and green, and do not combine blue and yellow. Both pairs are the classic confusion axes. It points onward to "Accessible Publishing at WHO" for the rest. Note this agrees with this repo's own `dataviz` skill and `docs/accessibility.md`, so a folio activating this voice is not taking on a conflicting rule.		accessibility · critical
• Recommended font size 10pts • Avoid combining red and green. Avoid combining blue and yellow. For further information please check "Accessible Publishing at WHO"
wpr-rdo-2020-003-eng#page-020, p18
who-des-figure-font-size10pt in graphs, figures and tables
The recommended font size inside a figure or a table is 10pt. Stated on both the figure page and the table page, so it is the guide's setting for data presentation generally rather than a one-off.		formatting · minor
Graphs and figures sample • Recommended font size 10pts … Table sample • Recommended font size 10pts

who-des-pagination-roman-then-arabicRoman numerals for the front matter, Arabic for the body
Preliminary pages take lower-case Roman numerals and the body takes Arabic. The guide also fixes the order of the front matter: title page; copyright, disclaimer and cataloguing behind the title page; table of contents listing chapters, figures and tables; foreword or preface; acknowledgements; list of contributors; list of abbreviations; executive summary. Then the body: chapters, references or bibliography, annexes.		structure · minor
PAGINATION Preliminary pages have lower-case Roman page numbers • Title page • Copyright + disclaimer + cataloging (behind title page) • Table of contents (list of chapters, figures and tables) • Foreword or Preface • Acknowledgments • List of contributors • List of abbreviations • Executive summary Main pages have Arabic page numbers • Chapters • References or bibliography • Annexes
wpr-rdo-2020-003-eng#page-023, p21
who-des-logo-integrityThe logo is placed, never redrawn
Do not alter, rotate or modify the logo, never recreate it, and do not obstruct it. Three versions exist — blue, black, and white on a dark background — and the logo may only appear on materials WPRO has endorsed. That last clause is a permission, not a style preference: a folio that is not a WPRO product must not carry it at all.		visual-identity · critical
The logo for the World Health Organization Regional Office for the Western Pacific (WPRO) should only be used on materials endorsed by WPRO. It should be presented as per guidelines here. This is essential to maintain a consistent presentation of our identity. Do not alter, rotate or modify the logo. It should never be recreated. Do not obstruct the logo. Three versions: The three versions of the WPRO logo are Blue, Black and White on a dark background
wpr-rdo-2020-003-eng#page-006, p4
who-des-logo-exclusion-zone1 cm of clear space, 3 cm minimum size
Leave an exclusion zone of at least 1 cm on every side of the logo, equal on all sides, and never reproduce it below 3 cm — the guide's stated floor for legibility in print and on screen. Where it sits beside another logo the clear space is no less than a quarter of the logo's width.		visual-identity · major
Leave "exclusion zones" around the logos (at least 1cm width distance) around the logos to ensure they maintain visual impact. Also the minimum logo size is 3cm (the smallest size at which the logo is still legible in print and on screen) For other sizes, no less than 1/4 width of logo. … Clear space should be equal on all sides.
wpr-rdo-2020-003-eng#page-007, p5-6
who-des-brand-blueThe regional blue is C95 M25 Y0 K0 / R0 G147 B213
The primary logo blue, given in both CMYK and RGB. A folio reproducing the brand takes the values from the guide rather than sampling them from an image — a sampled colour is a colour that drifted.		visual-identity · minor
C: 95 M: 25 Y: 0 K:0 / R: 0 G: 147 B: 213 / C: 0 M: 0 Y: 0 K: 100
wpr-rdo-2020-003-eng#page-006, p4
who-des-typeface-familyOne of four typefaces, for brand recognition rather than taste
News Gothic MT, DIN OT, PT Sans or Calibri, each with its stated weights; Times New Roman appears with a sample as well. The guide's reason is family resemblance across WHO products, which is why it is a rule rather than a suggestion, and why substituting a similar-looking font defeats it. Body samples are set at 10pt with leading of 12–13pt.		formatting · minor
Consistent fonts across our communications help brand recognition. This helps people recognize WHO publications and communications as part of the same family and gives our products a professional look and feel. News Gothic MT (bold, regular, italic) • DIN OT (bold, medium, regular, italic, condense, condense medium, light, light italic) PT Sans (bold, regular, italic, narrow, narrow regular) • Calibri (bold, regular, italic, light italic, light)
wpr-rdo-2020-003-eng#page-014, p12-13
who-des-photo-provenancePrefer WHO images, and credit them
Photographs should come from the Regional Office or the WHO photo library where possible, and carry credits. A folio's figure with no credit line is the finding here — the same provenance discipline the rest of this platform applies to a measurement or a quotation.		visual-identity · minor
Photos and credits It is best to use WHO images from the Regional Office or WHO photo library
