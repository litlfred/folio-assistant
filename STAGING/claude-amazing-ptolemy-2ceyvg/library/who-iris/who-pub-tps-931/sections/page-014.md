The following words and expressions do not normally require capitali-
zation:

commission, committee (unless specifically identified)

decision (e.g. ... in decision EB62(4) ...)

health for all

headquarters (WHO)

member (of the committee, etc.), but Member (State)

names of seasons

ordinal numbers referring to sessions of the Board (e.g. sixty-third
session of the Executive Board)

primary health care

programme (except those established by a World Health Assembly
resolution, e.g. Global Programme on AIDS, Expanded Pro-
gramme on Immunization)

region(s) (when used generically)

representatives

resolution (e.g. ...in resolution WHA41.26 ...)

session

specialized agency

strategy, strategies (e.g. health-for-all strategy)

twenty-first century

For further information on capitalization see Hart’s rules for compositors
and readers at the University Press Oxford, 39th ed. (Oxford, Oxford
University Press, 1983:8-14).

Words ending in -ize, -ise and -yse

Where there is a choice between using the suffix -ize or -ise (e.g.
organize or organise), -ize, derived from the Greek “-izo”, is preferred,
consistent with the first spelling of such words given in The concise
Oxford dictionary. For some words, such as surprise, comprise or impro-
vise, where -ise is not a suffix but part of the root of the word, there is
no choice and -ise must be used. Similarly, -yse must be used for words
derived from the Greek lusis (= lysis), as in analyse, dialyse and
hydrolyse.
