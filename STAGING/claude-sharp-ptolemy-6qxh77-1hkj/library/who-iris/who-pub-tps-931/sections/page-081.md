WHO Editorial Style Manual

References

References should be numbered consecutively throughout the text
and should be listed together at the end of the report, before any
annexes (which may have their own reference lists). Only if a single
reference is cited in the report should it be given in a footnote. The
bibliographic style to be followed is given in section 9.

Normally references to WHO unpublished documents should not be
included; any relevant information they contain should be summa-
rized in the report. However, ifan unrestricted document, available on
request to readers, contains important supplementary information
that cannot be incorporated in the report, a reference to it may be
included in the reference list, together with an indication as to how the
document can be obtained.

Bibliographies

A bibliography is intended to provide the reader with a list of
publications that may be consulted as background reading or for
supplementary information. It should be reasonably representative of
the relevant literature that exists in the languages of publication of the
Technical Report Series (always English, French and Spanish, and
sometimes Arabic, Chinese or Russian). If the list provided does not
seem to fulfil these criteria, it may need to be modified, removed or
retitled.

Annexes

Expert groups often annex supplementary information, details of
technical procedures, and summary lists and tables to their reports;
such material should have been approved by the group at the time of
the meeting. All annexes should be referred to in the body of the
report, with an indication of the reasons for their inclusion. Working
papers and signed contributions may not be included as annexes.
