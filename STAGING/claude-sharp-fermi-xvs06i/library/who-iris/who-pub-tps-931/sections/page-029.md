Chinese

The romanization system called pinyin, introduced by the Chinese in
the 1950s, has now largely supplanted the older Wade-Giles system.
Pinyin should be used in all WHO publications; accents are not
generally necessary, except in certain specific contexts (e.g. texts on
acupuncture). Pinyin spellings often differ markedly from the older
ones, and personal names are usually spelt without apostrophes. Given
names tend to be closed up rather than hyphenated in pinyin, but
current practice is variable. A list of Chinese place names, giving the
pinyin spelling, the Han character name, and the usual English,
French and Spanish equivalents is available from TPS.
