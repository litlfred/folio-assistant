Scientific terminology

Haemoglobins

The term “haemoglobin” is usually represented by the symbol Hb and
the various types of haemoglobin by capital letters or the names of the
laboratories, hospitals or places in which they were found (e.g. Hb F,
Hb S, Hb Hammersmith). The major and minor adult haemoglobins
are designated Hb A and Hb A, The four polypeptide chains of normal
human haemoglobin are designated by the Greek letters a, 8, y, and 8
and the paired chains in the form o,8,, 0,8, etc. For abnormal
haemoglobins, the locations of amino acid differences may be indi-
cated by designating the chain, the numbered position on it, the helix
position in parentheses and the amino acid substitution, for example:

087(F8)His > Tyr for Hb M Iwate.

Immunology

Immunoglobulins
Immunoglobulins should be represented by the symbol “Ig” and a
capital letter designating the specific class:

IgG IgA IgM IpD IgE.

Subclasses are indicated by a number attached to the class symbol, for
example:

IgG2 IgM3.
