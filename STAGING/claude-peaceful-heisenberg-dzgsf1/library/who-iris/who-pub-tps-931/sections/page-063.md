Restriction enzymes

The generally used nomenclature for restriction enzymes follows, more
ot less, the recommendations of Smith & Nathans (1973). The genus
and species name of the host organism is identified by the first letter of
the genus and the first two letters of the species to form a three-letter
abbreviation in italic (e.g. Escherichia coli becomes Eco). The strain or
type identification (or symbol for an extrachromosomal element)
follows in roman type (e.g. EcoP1, Hind). Roman numerals are used to
identify different restriction-modification systems; they are separated
from the enzyme designation by a space only if there is no strain
designated. For example:

BamHI EcoRI HindIII
Xma I Hae II.

Further reading

Smith HO, Nathans D. A suggested nomenclature for bacterial host modification
and restriction systems and their enzymes. Journal of molecular biology, 1973,
81:419-423.

Haematology

Blood groups

Blood groups (A, B, O, Le, Fy, Rh etc.) are set in roman, as are the
antigens after which the groups are named. The genes corresponding
to the antigens are usually denoted by italic letters (e.g. A).

Further reading
Issit PD, Crookston MC. Blood group terminology: current conventions. Trans-
fusion, 1984, 24:2-7.

Clfting factors

Clotting factors are usually designated by roman numerals (¢.g. factor
IX). An “a” added to the numeral indicates the activated form of the
factor (e.g. factor VIIa).
