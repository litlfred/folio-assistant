5. Footnotes

Footnotes are used to give explanatory or supplementary details when
insertion in the text itself is not advisable, because the insertion would
break the continuity of the text unduly, because of the length of the
passage, or because the details were added by someone other than the
author. Such details might include references, explanations of unfa-
miliar terms, last-minute additions or editor’s notes.

The number of footnotes should be kept to a minimum. As a general
tule, veryshort notes, suchas cross-references to other sections or pages
of the same article or work, should be given, in parentheses, in the text.

Complicated tabular material, lists and other entities that are not part
of the text should be put in an annex at the back of the book, not
included in footnotes. The footnote may read simply:

1 For a list of institutions involved see Annex 1.

For footnotes to tables see section 10.

Identification

Footnotes to the text should always be placed at the bottom of the
appropriate page. They are usually identified by superscript arabic
numerals. When this may cause confusion (e.g. in scientific text
containing mathematical exponents), lower-case letters should nor-
mally be used. Asterisks and other special typographical signs are used
only rarely (e.g. to indicate levels of statistical significance in tables
and, in membership lists for meetings of expert groups, to identify
people who were invited but unable to attend).

Footnotes to the text should be numbered consecutively, beginning
with “1”, throughout each page of a book or article of a journal. If two
(or more) passages appearing on the same page require the same
footnote, one footnote only should be given for both, the reference
mark being repeated in the text as appropriate.

If any punctuation immediately follows the word, part of a sentence,
or sentence to which a footnote refers, the reference mark should be
placed after the punctuation mark. The reference mark should be
placed after a bracket if it refers to the text in parentheses; otherwise
it should be placed immediately after the word or phrase to which it
refers.
