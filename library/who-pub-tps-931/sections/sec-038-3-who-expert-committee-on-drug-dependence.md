WHO Editorial Style Manval

List of participants

The list of participants should be headed by the name of the expert
group, irrespective of the form of the title of the report.

The names of members should be listed in alphabetical order, each
followed by the title of the post held, the name of the institute/
university department, and its geographical location. Titles, names of
institutes, etc. should normally be given in English, but if no exact
equivalent exists or in case of doubt, the name should be left in the
original language. The Chairman, Vice-Chairman and Rapporteur
should be identified.

The names of representatives of United Nations bodies and of nongov-
ernmental and intergovernmental organizations in official relations
with WHO should be listed under the heading “Representatives of
other organizations”.

Under the heading “Secretariat” only the secretary and any consult-
ants and temporary advisers who assisted in the preparation of the
meeting should normally be listed. Other names may be included only
under the special circumstances detailed in the WHO Manual, section
IX.1, Annex A, paragraph 160. Acknowledgements of any special
contributions made by people not included in the list of participants
may be included at the end of the report.

The secretary of the meeting is responsible for the correctness of the
details given in the list of participants. However, additional informa-
tion concerning members of expert advisory panels (i-e. all Expert
Committee members and many members of Study Groups and Scien-
tific Groups) can be obtained from the Office of Research Policy and
Strategy Coordination (RPS).

Changes in titles, addresses, country names and WHO unit/division
names that postdate the meeting should not be recorded.

Controver: sial statements

Every Technical Report carries a disclaimer, stating that the report
“contains the collective views of an international group of experts and
does not necessarily represent the decisions or the stated policy of the
World Health Organization”. Nevertheless, care should be taken to
rephrase statements that might prejudice the interests of the Organi-

Specifications

Specifications and similar requirements drafted by a meeting of experts
are not necessarily endorsed by WHO and should not be described as
WHO specifications unless they have been adopted by the World
Health Assembly or the Executive Board. They may, however, be
described as “specifications published by WHO”.

Definitions

Definitions recommended by an expert group should not conflict with
definitions previously published by WHO, unless the group considered
that previous definitions should be superseded, when this should be
explicitly stated. A phrase such as “the Expert Committee adopted the
following definitions for the purposes of its report” may be useful when
the status and scope of applicability of definitions are not clear.

Headings

Headings should be sufficiently numerous to separate clearly the
various topics discussed and to facilitate reading, but excessive use of
third-order and fourth-order headings should be avoided. A number-
ing system is normally used (1, 1.1, 1.2, 1.2.1, etc.) but should not be
extended beyond third-order headings.

Recommendations

The recommendations of an expert group are often split into those
directed to WHO and those directed to Member States. The former
frequently need to be reworded so as to advise the Director-General on
future programme activities without calling upon him to use WHO
staff, services or funds in a specified way. Recommendations of the
form “WHO should fund. ..” and “WHO should establish a programme
for...” are not acceptable. Direct recommendations to other United
Nations bodies and to nongovernmental organizations should be
avoided by appropriate rewording, for example “WHO should encour-
age/collaborate with/urge UNICEF...” rather than “UNICEF
should...”.

References

References should be numbered consecutively throughout the text
and should be listed together at the end of the report, before any
annexes (which may have their own reference lists). Only if a single
reference is cited in the report should it be given in a footnote. The
bibliographic style to be followed is given in section 9.

Normally references to WHO unpublished documents should not be
included; any relevant information they contain should be summa-
rized in the report. However, ifan unrestricted document, available on
request to readers, contains important supplementary information
that cannot be incorporated in the report, a reference to it may be
included in the reference list, together with an indication as to how the
document can be obtained.

Bibliographies

A bibliography is intended to provide the reader with a list of
publications that may be consulted as background reading or for
supplementary information. It should be reasonably representative of
the relevant literature that exists in the languages of publication of the
Technical Report Series (always English, French and Spanish, and
sometimes Arabic, Chinese or Russian). If the list provided does not
seem to fulfil these criteria, it may need to be modified, removed or
retitled.

Annexes

Expert groups often annex supplementary information, details of
technical procedures, and summary lists and tables to their reports;
such material should have been approved by the group at the time of
the meeting. All annexes should be referred to in the body of the
report, with an indication of the reasons for their inclusion. Working
papers and signed contributions may not be included as annexes.
