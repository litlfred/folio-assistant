9. Reference lists and bibliographies

A clear distinction should be made between lists of references and
bibliographies. A list of references contains only those works cited in
the text as sources of data or other information. It may include
unpublished documents, provided that these are available to inter-
ested readers. A bibliography is a list of published works relevant to
the subject matter of the publication and recommended for further
reading.

Authors are responsible for ensuring the accuracy of bibliographic
information. However, the editor should check any details that are
obviously wrong or that appear doubtful.

Bibliographies

Bibliographies in WHO publications should be reasonably representa-
tive of the world literature on the subject. It should be remembered
that most WHO books are translated into at least French and Spanish.
The names of authors should be arranged alphabetically, but a bibliog-
raphy may be subdivided into sections by subject. Bibliographies
should not contain references to unpublished works.

References

References may be cited in the text using either a numerical system or
the Harvard system. The numerical system is preferred, and is obliga-
tory for the Technical Report Series, the Bulletin of the World Health
Organization and World health forum. A reference to a finding reported
in an unpublished document should simply be followed, in parenthe-
ses, by the statement “unpublished data” or “unpublished observa-
tions” accompanied, if appropriate, by the name of the authority cited
and the date (P. Butler, unpublished data, 1991). Personal communi-
cations should be dealt with similarly. However, if an unpublished
document is known to be easily available, it may be included in the
reference list, together with an indication of how it may be obtained
(see example on page 29).
