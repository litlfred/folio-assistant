Italic type in printed publications is indicated in typescript by under-
lining. Italics are used in WHO publications in the following ways:

for the titles of books, journals and documents;

for foreign words and expressions not in common use (including
Latin species and genus names);

for variables in mathematical expressions;

for certain letters, prefixes and terms in scientific use;
(sparingly) for words requiring special emphasis.

5. Footnotes

Footnotes are used to give explanatory or supplementary details when
insertion in the text itself is not advisable, because the insertion would
break the continuity of the text unduly, because of the length of the
passage, or because the details were added by someone other than the
author. Such details might include references, explanations of unfa-
miliar terms, last-minute additions or editor’s notes.

The number of footnotes should be kept to a minimum. As a general
tule, veryshort notes, suchas cross-references to other sections or pages
of the same article or work, should be given, in parentheses, in the text.

Complicated tabular material, lists and other entities that are not part
of the text should be put in an annex at the back of the book, not
included in footnotes. The footnote may read simply:

1 For a list of institutions involved see Annex 1.

For footnotes to tables see section 10.

Identification

Footnotes to the text should always be placed at the bottom of the
appropriate page. They are usually identified by superscript arabic
numerals. When this may cause confusion (e.g. in scientific text
containing mathematical exponents), lower-case letters should nor-
mally be used. Asterisks and other special typographical signs are used
only rarely (e.g. to indicate levels of statistical significance in tables
and, in membership lists for meetings of expert groups, to identify
people who were invited but unable to attend).

Footnotes to the text should be numbered consecutively, beginning
with “1”, throughout each page of a book or article of a journal. If two
(or more) passages appearing on the same page require the same
footnote, one footnote only should be given for both, the reference
mark being repeated in the text as appropriate.

If any punctuation immediately follows the word, part of a sentence,
or sentence to which a footnote refers, the reference mark should be
placed after the punctuation mark. The reference mark should be
placed after a bracket if it refers to the text in parentheses; otherwise
it should be placed immediately after the word or phrase to which it
refers.

Ifa footnote refers to a quotation, the reference mark should be placed

Editor’s notes

Notes supplied by an editor, translator or compiler in a work including
original notes by the author must be differentiated from the original
notes. Such notes may be numbered in sequence with the author’s
notes.

Paragraphing and style

The paragraphing of footnotes should be in keeping with the overall
design of the book.. They may be set flush or in regular indented
paragraph style. Each footnote should end with a full stop.

6. Quotations

All quotations from printed material should correspond exactly to the
original in wording, spelling, punctuation, use of capital letters, etc.
When part of the original text is omitted from a quotation, the passage
in question should be replaced by three spaced dots. When three points
are used at the end of an incomplete sentence a fourth full point should
not be added. However, where the sentence is complete, the closing
point is set close up, followed by three points for omission. Omissions
should not change the meaning of the quotation.

Words inserted in or added to a quotation for clarity should be enclosed
in square brackets.

Short quotations should be enclosed in quotation marks and incorpo-
rated in the body of the text. Long quotations (more than three lines)
should usually be set off in some way (indented and/or smaller type).
In this case, quotation marks are not needed.

When a quotation occurs within a quotation, double quotation marks
should be used for the first quotation, single marks for the second one.

Allsigns of punctuation used with words in quotation marks should be
placed according to the sense. Thus, if an extract ends with a point or
exclamation or question mark, it should be included within the
quotation marks. In such a case, no final full stop is needed outside the
quotation marks, A number of useful examples are given in Hart’s rules
for compositors and readers at the University Press Oxford, 39th ed.
(Oxford, Oxford University Press, 1983:44-49).

Normally, quotations froma foreign language should be translated into
English, the words (translation from [the original language]) being
inserted at the end of the quotation. If a quotation contains only a few
foreign words, they may be left untranslated.
