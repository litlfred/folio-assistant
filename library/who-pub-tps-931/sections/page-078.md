17. Technical reports

Manuscripts submitted for publication in the Technical Report Series
should in general be edited in the usual way, in line with the guidelines
set out elsewhere in the WHO editorial sryle manual. However, it should
be borne in mind that most are the reports of WHO Expert Commit-
tees, Study Groups or Scientific Groups, whose members have in
theory approved the final text at the time of the meeting. Major
restructuring and substantial deletions or additions are therefore not
possible. The secretary of the meeting will often be able to identify
sections in the text or particular topics that provoked heated debate or
lengthy discussion during the meeting and that as such may require
cautious editing.

In general, expert groups review given fields of public health impor-
tance and make technical recommendations on subjects of interest to
WHO. Their findings and recommendations are intended to assist
national administrations and international health organizations in
formulating their health policies and in establishing standards. This
function, and any specific objectives identified in the introduction to
the group’s report, should be kept in mind during the editing of the text.

The following specific editorial points deserve particular attention;
useful additional information can be found in the WHO Manual,
section IX.1, Annex A.

Title of the report and expert group
The title of the report usually takes one of three forms:

1. EVALUATION OF CERTAIN FOOD ADDITIVES AND
CONTAMINANTS
Thirty-seventh report of the Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee
on Food Additives

2. ENVIRONMENTAL HEALTH IN URBAN DEVELOPMENT
Report of a WHO Expert Committee

3. WHO EXPERT COMMITTEE ON DRUG DEPENDENCE
Twenty-seventh report

The proposed descriptive title ofa report may be edited if necessary, but
the name of the expert group itself cannot be changed after the
meeting.
