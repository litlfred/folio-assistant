WHO Fiitorial Style Manual

List style

Whichever citation system has been used, the references should be
listed in order (numerical or alphabetical) at the end of the chapter or
full text, as appropriate, before any annexes.

If the Harvard system of citation has been used, the format shown in
the above examples should be modified slightly, the date being placed
in parentheses immediately after the authors’ names:

Davis A, Bailey DR (1969) Metrifonate in urinary schistosomiasis.
Bulletin of the World Health Organization, 41:209-224.

This system requires that each reference have a named author or be
listed under Anon. It is preferable to list, for example, WHO publica-
tions without named authors under WHO. In all cases, the citation in
the text should correspond exactly to the form used in the reference
list.

In the Harvard system, references should be listed in alphabetical
order, and the following rules should be observed:

¢ A single-author entry comes before a multiauthor entry begin-
ning with the same name.

¢ All works attributed to one person should be listed together in
chronological order, by year of publication.

¢ Two or more works by the same author and published in the same
year are distinguished by letters after the date:

Clark S (1990a) ...
Clark S (1990b) ...

Typographical style

In WHO publications, titles of books and journals are always given in
italics. Use of other fonts, e.g. bold for volume numbers or authors’
names, will depend on the book design.

10. Manuscript mark-up and layout

Manuscript mark-up

For most manuscripts destined for publication by WHO, the typesetter
will have a design specification prepared by the Graphics unit (GRA)
ora previous book asa model to work from, so that detailed typographi-
cal mark-up is rarely necessary. It is generally sufficient for editors to:

© code headings (A, B, C etc.);

© identify ambiguous letters, signs and symbols (Greek characters,
en tules etc.);

* ensure that requirements for bold, italic and small type and for
capital and lower-case letters are clear;

* indicate the required format for lists, quotations and any other
passages to be treated differently from the main text;

© note in the margin the approximate positions for insertion of
figures and tables;

® clearly separate from the main text (either physically or by means
of a coloured pen) figures, tables and their headings and foot-
notes;

© ensure that spacing requirements are unambiguous (insert a
vertical line to fill up a large area of unwanted space and mark
“run on” or “new page” wherever necessary).

In addition, all instructions for the printer and editorial comments
(e.g. “check in proof”) should be circled so that it will be clear that they
are not to be typeset.

For all publications, the preliminary pages prepared for the typesetter
should include the title page, copyright page (with space left for
Cataloguing in Publication data) and contents list. They should be
accompanied by the standard text for the inside front cover and, if
appropriate, a list of running heads. Information for the front-cover
design, text for the back cover and spine, and a list of related WHO
publications for the inside back cover should be prepared for GRA
(nonserial publications) or sent directly with the main manuscript to
the typesetter (series publications).

An English/French list of terms used for manuscript mark-up and proof
correction is given in Annex 4.

WHO Editorial Style Manval

Tables

Tables should be kept as simple as possible, with brief titles and column
headings. Restrict the use of rules, unless the table is particularly
complex. Vertical lines should generally be avoided, but straddle rules
over column headings are useful for clarifying hierarchical arrange-
ments. When units of measurement or multiplication factors appear in
column headings, ensure that there is no possibility of misinterpreta-
tion; “Concentration (mg/litre)” is clear, but “No. of cases detected
(x 10°)” is ambiguous. A column should not contain any blanks; use
dashes or abbreviations where data are missing (for example NA, ND,
for “not applicable”, “not determined”).

Additional information should be given in the form of footnotes,
generally identified with superscript letters placed in the appropriate
order in the table from left to right and top to bottom. The same mark
may be used on two or more elements if the corresponding note applies
to them, for example:

Data based on a teratogenicity study in which ochratoxin A was
administered on day 9 of gestation.

Fora table consisting only of words or letters, superscript numbers may
be used as reference marks to identify footnotes, and for a table that
includes mathematical or chemical equations a series of symbols may
be used, because of the risk of mistaking letters or numbers for
exponents:

* (asterisk), | (dagger), $ (double dagger), § (section mark).

When more symbols are needed, these may be doubled and tripled in
the same sequence. Asterisks are conventionally also used to indicate
levels of statistical significance.
