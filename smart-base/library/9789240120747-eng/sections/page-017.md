Introduction
This handbook also aligns with WHO’s SMART 
Guidelines Initiative. SMART Guidelines are 
Standards-based, Machine-readable, Adaptive, 
Requirements-based and Testable recommended 
digital capabilities, health and data content 
aligned with normative recommendations 
(17). SMART Guidelines are WHO’s approach 
to systematize and accelerate the consistent 
application of recommended, life-saving 
interventions in the digital age. 
SMART Guidelines are a comprehensive set of 
reusable digital health components which provide 
a five-step pathway, with each layer increasing 
in complexity, to advance the adoption of best 
clinical and data practices. The five knowledge 
layers comprise: 
•	
L1 Narrative: enhanced clinical, public health 
guidelines and data recommendations 
•	
L2 Operational: digital adaptation kits (DAKs)
•	
L3 Machine readable: machine-readable 
recommendations
Source: WHO SMART guidelines: optimising country-level use of guideline recommendations in the digital age (Mehl et al., 2021) (18).
•	
L4 Executable: reference applications and 
services
•	
L5 Dynamic: precision health model. 
These layers inform developers how to translate 
recommendations into specifications and 
standards; technologists how to integrate 
recommendations into updatable digital systems; 
and countries how to localize, make interoperable, 
institutionalize and update digital systems 
consistent with evidence-based recommendations 
(17). Each layer can be adapted and localized 
within the country context (Fig. 2). 
This handbook, following SMART Guidelines, 
offers guidance on L1 and L2 for adapting 
guidelines to implement a digital solution for 
reporting substandard and falsified medical 
products.
