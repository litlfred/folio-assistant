4. Determine the requirements
4.3 Develop business processes and 
workflows
A business process is a set of related activities 
or tasks performed together to achieve specific 
objectives, such as reporting substandard and 
falsified medical products in this case (35). 
Workflows are a visual representation of the 
progression of activities (tasks, interactions, 
decision points) that are performed within the 
business process (35). The activities in a workflow 
include those that are manual and machine 
automated.
A business process matrix (see Table 10 for 
example) is useful for:
•	
taking stock of the business processes that 
need to be mapped;
•	
gaining clarity about who the key 
stakeholders are and how they will interact 
with the substandard and falsified reporting 
tool;
•	
gaining clarity on the linkages across different 
levels of the health system that enable 
integrated service delivery of care pathways; 
and
•	
determining a clear start and endpoint for 
each business process based on previous 
desk reviews, interviews and service-delivery 
observations (4).
Develop workflows to illustrate the various 
activities required to achieve each business 
process. Create as-is workflows to identify 
gaps and develop to-be workflows to 
address these deficiencies. Conventional 
diagramming techniques should be employed 
to ensure that the workflow is universally 
comprehensible. It may be necessary to 
generate multiple maps, with connectors 
indicating their linking points. Business 
Process Model and Notation (BPMN) 
version 2.0 (36) is a widely adopted set of 
diagramming conventions. Adhering to this 
standard documentation convention is crucial 
to facilitate collaboration and comparisons 
across systems. See Annex 3 for BPMN 
symbols.
