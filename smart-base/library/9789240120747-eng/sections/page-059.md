A smartphone app was developed and piloted in Indonesia and the United Republic of Tanzania to help 
health care professionals report a suspected substandard and falsified medical product to the NMRA. The 
pilot study focused on providing a reporting app that was easy, simple to use, reliable and fast.
•
App users had access to an outbox to store a 
report offline. Once network coverage was 
obtained, the alert automatically sent to the 
server.
•
Users could save draft reports to complete at 
another time.
•
Alerts provide a direct link to the WHO Medical 
Product Alert webpage.
•
Users were able to translate the app into local 
languages.

In both Indonesia and the United Republic of 
Tanzania, the pilot study yielded a total of 36 
reports of 27 medical products submitted using 
the smartphone app. In addition, the pilot study 
increased awareness and education 
surrounding substandard and falsified medical 
products, with health care professionals learning 
techniques on how to identify substandard and 
falsified medical products.
•
Health care professionals liked the 
simplicity, user-. friendliness and 
convenience of the smartphone app 
compared with paper-based 
reporting.
•
Health care professionals 
appreciated how the app created a 
feedback loop with NMRAs.
•
Health care professionals preferred 
taking and uploading photographs 
of the suspect product versus 
manually filling in information.
•
Health care professionals 
appreciated the limited manual data 
entry.
•
NMRA staff praised the flexibility of 
not having to be office-bound to 
receive reports.
•
The workload associated with the 
pilot app was deemed manageable.
•
Strong support from management/
supervisors was pivotal to increase 
trust and ensure the success of the 
app rollout.
•
Older models of various 
smartphones experienced issues of 
incompatibility with the app.
•
In the United Republic of Tanzania, 
weak/unreliable internet connectivity 
led to some installation and login 
issues.
•
NMRAs felt the back end was not fit 
for purpose, leading to app and 
website delays.
•
Health care professionals suggested 
adding features such as displaying a 
history of all submitted reports and 
NMRA and WHO alerts on confirmed 
substandard and falsified medical 
products.
•
NMRAs expressed concern about the 
impact of a broader rollout in light of 
competing priorities.
•
The app included a feature to 
schedule and assign follow-up to an 
NMRA staff member. This had to be 
removed mid-pilot due to useability 
issues. The NMRA resorted back to 
maintaining two systems – the pilot 
website and Excel.
