Currently, many countries rely on paper-based 
reporting systems for identifying substandard 
and falsified medical products. While these 
systems can be effective, the manual nature of 
reporting often discourages busy health care 
professionals from participating. Additionally, the 
inconsistencies between data provided by various 
paper reporting procedures can lead to significant 
issues at the regulatory level, as the submitted 
data are often not standardized. In some 
countries, there are no formal reporting systems 
in place, leading to reliance on verbal recounts of 
suspect products (27).
This underscores the critical need for adopting 
standards-based digital solutions to support 
and improve the reporting of substandard and 
falsified medical products. Digital reporting 
systems, such as smartphone applications, 
streamline the process, making it more accessible 
and efficient for health care professionals. 
For instance, in a pilot project coordinated by 
WHO, smartphone applications for reporting 
substandard and falsified medical products were 
developed and successfully implemented in 
Indonesia and the United Republic of Tanzania 
(27, 28). These digital tools not only facilitate more 
consistent and standardized data collection but 
also enhance the overall capacity of regulatory 
authorities to monitor and address the prevalence 
of substandard and falsified medical products 
effectively.
Who this handbook is for 
This handbook has been developed for countries, 
organizations and teams seeking to plan and 
implement digital solutions for reporting 
substandard and falsified medical products by 
health care professionals and aims to provide 
guidance to the various target audiences across 
the different implementation phases and areas, as 
outlined in Table 1.
