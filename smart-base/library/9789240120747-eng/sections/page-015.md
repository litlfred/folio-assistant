How to use this handbook 
This handbook provides guidance to countries 
on how to adopt digital solutions for reporting 
of substandard and falsified medical products. It 
outlines various steps to ensure a comprehensive 
approach that aligns with existing digital health 
guidance and other intersecting strategies and 
architectures.
This handbook can be a useful resource in the 
following scenarios: 
•	
When moving from a paper-only based 
reporting system to a digital reporting 
system.
•	
When moving to a unified digital solution and 
approach, e.g. when there are some groups of 
health care professionals using paper-based 
systems while others using digital systems.
•	
When combining isolated digital reporting 
systems to create a complete, connected and 
interoperable digital reporting system.
•	
When evaluating existing digital solutions 
for enhancement to support reporting of 
substandard and falsified medical products.
Before using this resource
The key assumption for users of this resource 
is that a comprehensive digital health strategy 
exists. In addition, it would be beneficial to 
have a comprehensive digital health supply 
chain strategy that aligns with the digital health 
strategy.
If there are no national digital health and supply 
chain strategies, refer to the National eHealth 
strategy toolkit produced jointly by WHO and the 
International Telecommunications Union (ITU) 
(29), the Global strategy on digital health 2020–2025 
(30) (which has been extended to 2027 during the 
Seventy-eighth World Health Assembly) and the 
Digital transformation handbook for health supply 
chain architecture (31). Additionally, the SDG digital 
investment framework developed by ITU and the 
Digital Impact Alliance provides more guidance 
on developing a digital health architecture that 
leverages a whole-of-government approach and 
digital public goods (32). 
These are many other resources for implementing 
digital health interventions and systems available 
from WHO, other United Nations agencies and 
numerous nongovernmental organizations (see 
Fig. 1). 
This handbook uses the framework and 
approaches set forth in the Digital implementation 
investment guide (DIIG), which helps governments 
and technical partners plan a digital health 
intervention (3).
