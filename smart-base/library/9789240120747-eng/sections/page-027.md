Implementing digital reporting systems requires more than just technology. It calls for a clear, strategic 
and participatory approach that aligns regulatory policy, governance and leadership. This section 
explores how countries can establish a shared understanding of the challenges and opportunities 
surrounding substandard and falsified medical products and create a strategic plan to ensure 
sustainable, effective adoption of digital health tools.
2.1 Review regulatory policies for 
substandard and falsified medical 
products reporting
The regulatory framework is the backbone for 
any substandard and falsified medical products 
reporting system, shaping data flow, privacy, 
liability and enforcement mechanisms. Before 
implementing a digital reporting system, 
countries should evaluate existing regulatory 
policies, identify gaps and determine whether 
new regulations or legislative amendments 
are necessary to support the implementation, 
operationalization and adoption.
•	
Pharmacovigilance and quality surveillance 
integration. Substandard and falsified 
medicines reporting often intersects with 
pharmacovigilance systems. Harmonize 
regulations and encourage cross-functional 
use of data to foster a more holistic approach 
to medicine safety and quality.
•	
Reporting obligations. Clarify which 
entities – retailers, health care workers, 
customs, law enforcement, manufacturers, 
distributors, health facilities – are required to 
report. Establish timelines and processes for 
mandatory or voluntary submissions.
•	
Enforcement mechanisms. Ensure that 
regulatory authorities have the legal mandate 
and capacity to act on digital reports by 
conducting timely investigations, imposing 
sanctions or initiating withdrawals or recalls 
where necessary.
