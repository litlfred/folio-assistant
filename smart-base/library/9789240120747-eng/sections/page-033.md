3. Define the future state
3.2 Link to the enterprise 
architecture
The DIIG provides an enterprise architecture 
framework for digital health interventions (Fig. 3). 
“A digital health enterprise architecture, if 
available in the country, outlines current 
and planned business processes, data, 
systems and technologies and provides 
an overview of the standards, information 
exchange and interoperability profiles 
that can be optimized across the 
enterprise. A clearly established 
enterprise architecture describing how 
different processes, data, systems 
and technologies function together 
is crucial for guiding interoperability 
to support data exchange between 
digital health applications, as well as 
collective functioning goals. An enterprise 
architecture blueprint lays the foundation 
for scaling up and sustaining digital 
health applications that are standardized 
and interoperable and that will facilitate 
access to higher quality, more complete 
information, in turn resulting in better 
decisions and more improved health 
outcomes across multiple areas.” (3)
A digital health enterprise architecture should be 
developed in alignment with the DIIG framework 
if one has not already been developed. If a digital 
health enterprise architecture is already in place, 
it should be aligned with the DIIG framework. 
As shown in Fig. 3, the substandard and falsified 
reporting application will fall under the point of 
service applications category. 
Linking to the digital health enterprise 
architecture will guide how other digital health 
applications interoperate with the substandard 
and falsified reporting application. The 
architecture will demonstrate how the 
substandard and falsified reporting application 
interacts with other components, such as registry 
services, to uniformly identify facilities, clients 
and products during reporting. This architecture 
will provide critical insights for the following key 
technology considerations.
Open standards and interoperability. Identify 
and implement validated open standards 
to ensure effective data exchange and 
interoperability of systems. Open standards will 
future proof the digital health implementations 
and promote interoperability. The enterprise 
architecture will help establish a standardized 
data exchange framework that allows for the 
seamless flow of information between the 
reporting application and the different systems 
and stakeholders. This will facilitate more efficient 
reporting and monitoring of substandard and 
falsified medical products.
