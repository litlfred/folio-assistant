Instituting data management and governance policies 
17
*Master data management procedures should include 
the following key elements to continuously and securely 
maintain master data:
•	
Data governance framework: Establish clear policies, 
roles, responsibilities, and processes to ensure data 
quality and consistency for use across the health 
supply chain.
	
Illustrative data management roles:
-Data owner: responsible for the overall strategy, 
governance, and quality of the product master 
data. The data owner:
-	 Defines the rules, policies, and standards for 
data quality, structure, and governance.
-	 Makes decisions on how the data should be 
managed and ensures alignment with 
business objectives.
-	 Approves changes to the product master data 
and ensures compliance with regulatory and 
business requirements.
-	 Is accountable for the accuracy, completeness, 
and relevance of the product data in the 
organization.
-	
Data custodian: responsible for the technical 
management and security of product master data. 
The data custodian:
-Ensures that the product master data is 
properly stored, protected, and backed up.
-Manages access controls, data migration, and 
archiving processes.
-	 Implements the rules and standards defined by 
the data owner, ensuring that the data remains 
secure and operationally available.
-	 Coordinates with IT and other technical teams 
to ensure the data infrastructure supports 
business needs.
-	
Data steward: responsible for the day-to-day 
management, maintenance, and quality control of 
the product master data. The data steward:
-	 Ensures that data is accurate, complete, and 
up to date by monitoring and resolving data 
quality issues.
-Oversees data entry, validation, and 
correction processes.
-	 Acts as the liaison between business users and 
technology, facilitating communication about 
data needs and issues.
-	 Ensures adherence to data governance policies 
set by the data owner.
•	
Data quality management: implement regular data 
quality assessments, validation rules, and cleansing 
routines to maintain accurate and reliable product 
master data.
•	
Data security and privacy: define and enforce security 
protocols and access controls to protect sensitive data 
from unauthorized access and breaches.
•	
Data integration and interoperability: establish 
protocols for harmonizing disparate product master 
data sets to ensure seamless exchange of transactional 
data that references master data.
•	
Data lifecycle management: create a comprehensive 
strategy for managing data from creation to disposal, 
ensuring that data remains relevant and compliant 
with regulatory requirements.
•	
Change management: establish processes for 
managing changes to data definitions, structures, and 
coding schemes to adapt to evolving business needs.
•	
Monitoring and reporting: implement continuous 
monitoring and reporting mechanisms to track data 
management activities and performance metrics.
