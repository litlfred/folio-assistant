Sharing product master data as a global good 
Sharing health product master data with a global 
repository—such as the WHO Global Product Catalogue—
holds significant value and importance as a global 
good for several reasons:
•	
Access to accurate information: A centralized 
repository enables health professionals and 
organizations worldwide to access accurate and 
up-to-date information on health products.
•	
Standardization: A global repository aggregates 
master data from multiple sources for the same or 
similar products and facilitates the harmonization of 
data differences to support standardization.
•	
Reduced redundancy: Sharing data at a global level 
reduces duplication of effort in the creation and 
maintenance of health product master data. 
Countries can benefit from each other’s efforts, 
enhancing overall efficiency.
•	
Crisis management: During public health 
emergencies—such as pandemics—access to globally 
standardized data enables coordinated responses and 
the efficient distribution of essential health products.
•	
Equal access: Sharing data ensures that all countries, 
regardless of their economic status, have access to 
critical health product information.
•	
Capacity building: Low- and middle-income countries 
can strengthen their capacity by leveraging global data 
and best practices shared through the repository.
Countries may choose to share their product master data 
with the WHO Global Catalogue through the PCMT. This 
mechanism can support global harmonization efforts and 
contribute to improved data quality and interoperability.
