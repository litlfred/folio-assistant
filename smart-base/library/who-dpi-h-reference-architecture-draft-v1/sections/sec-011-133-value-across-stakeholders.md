The value of DPI-H is also distributed across a broad range of stakeholders, each of whom 
330 
benefits differently from the same shared infrastructure. For example: 
331 
● Patients benefit from continuity of care with their clinical history, identity, and 
332 
entitlements following them across facilities and programmes rather than being 
333 
reconstructed at each encounter.  
334 
● Health workers benefit from reduced administrative burden, fewer duplicate data entry 
335 
tasks, and access to more complete and reliable information at the point of care.  
336 
● Health programme managers gain a consistent, cross-cutting data foundation that 
337 
supports monitoring, evaluation, and resource planning across programmes rather than 
338 
within silos — and, as data is aggregated across programmes and sectors, a basis for 
339 
the intelligence that informs sector objectives, financing decisions, and operational 
340 
guidelines.  
341 
● Ministries of Health gain visibility and governance over a coherent national digital 
342 
health ecosystem rather than a patchwork of disconnected systems.  
343 
● Ministries of Finance and cross-sector counterparts benefit from the transparency 
344 
and accountability that shared DPI-H makes possible; with visibility into health 
345 
expenditure, fraud detection, supply chain efficiency, and whether health financing 
346 
schemes are paying providers correctly and on time and reaching intended beneficiaries, 
347 
providing the evidence base for informed resource allocation decisions. 
348 
● Development partners and donors benefit from infrastructure that outlasts individual 
349 
funding cycles; with their investments building upon a shared foundation, rather than 
350 
duplicating it.  
351 
● Researchers benefit from access to more complete and consistent health data — under 
352 
appropriate consent and governance — supporting studies, evaluation, and evidence 
353 
generation, which is more difficult to support when data is fragmented.  
354 
● The commercial sector can innovate on top of the shared infrastructure, building new 
355 
value-added services and applications rather than recreating core components.  
356 
● DPI Authorities can leverage DPI-H to extend the utility of foundational DPI into 
357 
contexts it was not designed to handle independently. 
358 
● The health system as a whole benefits from the compounding returns of 
359 
interoperability and shared DPI, rather than degrading under the weight of 
360 
fragmentation. 
361 
 
362 
DPI-H does not eliminate the need for programme-specific and point-of-service applications. It 
363 
provides the foundation upon which they can be built more efficiently, connected more reliably, 
364 
and sustained more affordably — making every subsequent investment in the health system 
365 
more productive than it would otherwise be.
