DPI-H addresses fragmentation directly. By investing in shared core capabilities, countries 
316 
create infrastructure that every programme can draw upon without rebuilding. The savings in 
317 
development cost, maintenance burden, and operational complexity are substantial, and they 
318 
grow more valuable as the health system expands.

320 
This shift from product procurement to capability investment also changes the nature of what 
321 
countries own. Vertical technology procurement tends to lock health systems into vendor-
322 
specific solutions, with significant switching costs if a product is discontinued, becomes 
323 
unaffordable, or fails to evolve with changing requirements. DPI-H, built on open standards and 
324 
modular architecture, creates infrastructure that countries can govern, extend, and adapt — and 
325 
upon which local innovators, both public and private, can develop new services without 
326 
recreating the foundational layer.
