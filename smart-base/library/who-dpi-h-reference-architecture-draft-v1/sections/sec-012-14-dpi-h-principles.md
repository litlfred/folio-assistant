The design and implementation of Digital Public Infrastructure for Health is guided by a set of 
369 
principles that reflect the technical requirements of shared digital infrastructure and the 
370 
responsibilities inherent in managing health data at population scale. At its foundation, DPI-H 
371 
promotes the use of open standards and interoperable design, enabling systems to exchange 
372 
data reliably and evolve without vendor lock-in. The principles below should inform every aspect 
373 
of how DPI-H is conceived, built, governed, and sustained. 
374 
 
375 
Interoperability: DPI-H should support both data and system interoperability, enabling the 
376 
exchange and use of information across platforms, providers, and potentially, sectors. It is 
377 
designed to accommodate domain-specific extensions without disrupting other users or 
378 
systems, ensuring that as the health digital ecosystem grows, continuity and scalability are 
379 
preserved rather than compromised. 
380 
 
381 
Reusability: DPI-H components should be designed for reuse across programmes and use 
382 
cases, allowing shared functionality and data to serve multiple purposes without duplication. 
383 
This reduces cost, lowers implementation burden, and provides a stable foundation upon which 
384 
additional solutions — public and private — can be built and iterated. 
385 
 
386 
Open Standards and Specifications: DPI-H should be built on open standards, specifications, 
387 
and APIs developed through recognised Standards Development Organisations (SDOs) and 
388 
open governance processes that are inclusive in participation and accessible in their outputs. 
389 
Open standards promote interoperability, portability, and transparency, enable consistent data 
390 
structures, and support the long-term sustainability of national digital health investments by 
391 
removing dependency on proprietary technology. 
392 
 
393 
Public Governance: DPI-H should be governed in the public interest under clear legal and 
394 
regulatory frameworks that protect privacy, security, and individual rights. Public governance 
395 
refers to accountable stewardship under public mandate — ensuring the infrastructure fulfils the 
396 
functions the health sector needs to meet health and health-related needs, and remains subject 
397 
to institutional oversight — rather than requiring that every component be publicly owned or 
398 
operated. Components may be built or operated by public, private, or non-governmental actors, 
399 
provided they remain accountable to public oversight. Such governance structures provide the 
400 
accountability and trust that are prerequisite to the widespread adoption of shared health 
401 
infrastructure. 
402 
 
403 
Security and Privacy: DPI-H embeds security and privacy protections by design, treating them 
404 
not as compliance requirements but as foundational architectural properties. Protection 
405 
encompasses both dimensions — security against unauthorised access, and privacy as a right 
406 
— ensuring that access to personal health data is controlled, lawful, and appropriate. DPI-H 
407 
applies zero-trust principles and rights-enhancing measures by default, while also ensuring 
408 
resilience and continuity of essential health services against evolving cyber and operational 
409 
risks. 
410

411 
Autonomy and Sovereignty: DPI-H supports national ownership and control of digital health 
412 
systems, enabling countries to implement solutions aligned with their own priorities, policies, 
413 
and governance structures. Countries should not be dependent on actors outside their 
414 
jurisdiction for the continued operation or evolution of their core digital health infrastructure, and 
415 
the capabilities, standards, and governance of DPI-H should remain under national control. 
416 
 
417 
These principles establish the foundational character of DPI-H as shared digital infrastructure. 
418 
The reference architecture that gives effect to these principles is further shaped by a set of 
419 
architectural principles that govern how DPI-H is designed, structured, and applied in practice — 
420 
addressed in the Architecture Principles sub-chapter of the Reference Architecture Framework.
