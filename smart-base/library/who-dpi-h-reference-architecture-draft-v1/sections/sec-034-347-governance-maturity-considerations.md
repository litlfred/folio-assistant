517 
Governance cannot be designed as though all implementing countries begin from the same point. 
518 
Countries vary significantly in institutional capacity, legal frameworks for digital health, existing 
519 
governance structures and financial resources, and the guidance needs to be applicable across 
520 
that range. 
521 
As an illustration, a country in an early stage of DPI-H development may lack the institutional 
522 
infrastructure to stand up fully separated strategic and operational governance bodies at once. In 
523 
such a setting it may be appropriate for a single ministerial steering committee to hold both 
524 
strategic and senior operational authority in the near term, with a defined roadmap to separate 
525 
these functions as capacity develops. The principles in this section — particularly the separation 
526 
of mandate from execution, and the equity-representation and financial-sustainability mandates 
527 
— remain applicable regardless of institutional maturity; what varies is the structural form through 
528 
which they are realised. 
529 
Countries and regional bodies should use governance maturity assessment as part of DPI-H 
530 
readiness evaluation, identifying which governance capacities already exist, which need to be 
531 
built, and in what sequence governance functions should be formalised as the infrastructure 
532 
develops.
