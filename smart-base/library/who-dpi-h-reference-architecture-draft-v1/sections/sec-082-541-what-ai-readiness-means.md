AI readiness describes how well placed a country is to adopt AI safely, effectively, and 
142 
sustainably; it is less a property of any particular model than of the environment into which a 
143 
model would be introduced. It can be understood across three dimensions:  
144 
• 
the quality and governance of the data a model would use;  
145 
• 
the legal and policy framework governing its use; and  
146 
• 
the institutional capacity to deploy, evaluate, monitor, and maintain it.  
147 
Country-level legislation and organisational policies governing AI — covering data protection, the 
148 
configuration of input and output safeguards, the quality and representativeness of training data, 
149 
and the monitoring of model drift over time — are practical prerequisites for moving from pilots to 
150 
routine use. WHO's guidance on the ethics and governance of AI for health44, and its guidance 
151 
on large multi-modal models45, set out principles and recommendations that countries can draw 
152 
on, and several jurisdictions are establishing legal frameworks of their own.43,45,46,47
