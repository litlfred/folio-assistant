The most consequential investment a country can make before implementation begins is an 
29 
honest assessment of its current digital health landscape. Countries that skip this step tend to 
30 
build on unstable foundations — replicating fragmentation in new infrastructure, integrating with 
31 
systems already scheduled for replacement, or investing in capabilities they already hold in 
32 
another form. 
33 
A national digital health architecture assessment should answer four questions. 
34 
 
 
89 
What do we already have? Inventory all existing health information systems, registries, and 
35 
digital tools, including those funded by development partners and individual programmes. For 
36 
each, assess whether it performs a function that aligns with a REDDHI component, whether it is 
37 
based on open standards, what population or geography it covers, and who owns and operates 
38 
it. 
39 
What is the state of our foundational DPI? Assess the reach and accessibility of national 
40 
identity, payments, and data-exchange infrastructure. Understand which populations are 
41 
covered, whether health use cases are legally and technically supported, and what integration 
42 
mechanisms exist or need to be established. 
43 
What are our governance and institutional conditions? Assess whether a legal and 
44 
regulatory framework exists for health data, whether there is a designated institutional home for 
45 
digital health architecture decisions, whether procurement mechanisms are aligned with open-
46 
standards requirements, and whether the capacity to design, implement, and sustain shared 
47 
infrastructure exists in government. 
48 
What are our priority health goals? Identify two to three high-value health goals — drawn 
49 
from the seven priority goals that underpin this architecture — that have political support, a 
50 
willing implementation community, and the potential to demonstrate tangible value quickly. 
51 
Within each, identify the specific high-value use cases that will drive the first implementations. 
52 
These goals, and the use cases within them, can anchor the initial implementation and provide 
53 
the evidence base for broader investment. 
54 
The outputs of this assessment form the foundation of a national digital health architecture 
55 
roadmap: a living document that maps the current state, the target direction, the gap between 
56 
them, and the sequenced investments required to close it. The roadmap is strengthened where 
57 
it also sets out a benefits case and a simple logic model — connecting each investment to the 
58 
capabilities it enables and the health outcomes it is expected to support — and where it draws 
59 
on recognised digital-readiness and capability assessment frameworks, to establish a baseline 
60 
against which progress can later be measured.
