Across the priority health goals, the most credible near-term applications tend to be assistive 
109 
rather than autonomous, and they fall broadly into two kinds: operational uses embedded in 
110 
routine workflows, and analytical uses that support planning, monitoring, and decision-making. 
111 
Illustrative examples, drawn from the domains examined in developing this architecture, include: 
112 
• 
in health financing, support for claims adjudication and for the detection of fraud, waste, 
113 
and abuse. It is useful to distinguish the two: detecting fraud relies on pattern analysis 
114 
across large volumes of claims, whereas adjudication support concerns the evaluation of 
115 
individual claims. Different techniques apply, from rule-based systems to machine 
116 
learning, and current use of large language models in this area remains limited; 
117 
• 
in the supply chain, demand forecasting, product classification and mapping to reference 
118 
catalogues, quality control, and the detection of diversion or other anomalies; 
119 
• 
in clinical services, diagnostic and decision support, image interpretation, risk prediction, 
120 
and the detection and forecasting of outbreaks. These are functions of point-of-care 
121 
clinical applications and analytical capabilities, supported by the shared foundation, rather 
122 
than functions of any single shared component; 
123 
• 
in planning and reach, estimating settlement patterns and population distribution from 
124 
imagery to support microplanning and last-mile delivery. The limits here are instructive: a 
125 
model may identify that a structure exists without determining its function, and a residential 
126 
dwelling, a school, and an unoccupied structure carry very different implications for 
127 
population estimates. Associated, governed reference data is what allows such a 
128 
distinction to be drawn; and 
129 
• 
across domains, extracting structured data from unstructured or scanned documents — 
130 
for example, reading a scanned invoice, form, or report and converting it into coded, 
131 
structured records that can be stored and reused — together with assistance in coding 
132 
and in navigating complex guidance for different kinds of user. 
133 
In each case, current good practice favours starting with assistive tools under human oversight 
134 
rather than full automation, and combining automated analysis with human review at the points 
135 
where consequences are significant. Framing adoption this way also helps decision-makers 
136 
assess the broad benefit claims that frequently accompany AI offerings, by anchoring the question 
137 
in a specific use case, the data it depends on, and the oversight it requires.
