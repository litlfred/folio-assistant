A reference architecture is a technology-neutral blueprint that captures the common building 
14 
blocks, relationships, and design principles of a technical environment, so that organisations do 
15 
not have to start from a blank page. Drawing on established enterprise architecture practice, it 
16 
serves as an authoritative reference point that guides the development of more specific 
17 
architectures and solutions, while offering a structured framework of reusable building blocks.3,4 
18 
Its value lies in giving diverse stakeholders a shared frame of reference — a common 
19 
vocabulary and structure that aligns decisions, reduces duplication, and makes application 
20 
components easier to connect and sustain over time. In health, where services are delivered 
21 
across many programmes, institutions, and levels of care, this is especially important. A 
22 
reference architecture provides countries with a coherent way to translate high-level health 
23 
policy goals into digital infrastructure that is interoperable, governable, and adaptable to their 
24 
own context.
