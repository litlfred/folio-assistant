A recurring theme in digital public infrastructure discourse is that sectors should leverage 
174 
foundational DPIs — identity, payments, and data exchange — rather than build parallel, 
175 
sector-specific equivalents that reinforce fragmentation of a country’s digital architecture. This is 
176 
sound guidance where foundational DPIs exist, are trustworthy, and are accessible, and the 
177 
health sector should build on them wherever it can. Foundational DPIs are, by design, general-
178 
purpose: they provide broadly reusable capabilities and deliberately leave sector-specific logic 
179 
to the sectors that use them. Health, however, presents conditions that require more than 
180 
foundational DPI can currently provide — in some cases because foundational DPI is genuinely 
181 
absent or incomplete, and in others because the capability belongs with health-specific 
182 
components by design. The aim is not to create permanent parallel infrastructure, but to 
183 
leverage foundational DPI where it serves, add health-specific components where it does not, 
184 
and ensure that gaps in foundational DPI do not become a barrier to delivering care. 
185 
 
186 
For a significant number of low- and middle-income countries, foundational DPIs are either 
187 
nascent or absent. This means that in some cases, even where a country has foundational 
188 
DPIs, health may require specific capabilities, exchange standards, and governance 
189 
arrangements that go beyond what a general-purpose DPI can provide. Where foundational 
190 
DPIs do not yet exist — as is the case in many low- and middle-income countries — health 
191 
systems cannot wait. Care delivery, disease surveillance, health financing and supply chain 
192 
management need to function in the present.
