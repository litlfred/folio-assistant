Governance is the set of arrangements through which decision authority is distributed, 
223 
exercised and accounted for across all those who depend upon, contribute to, or are served by 
224 
the shared infrastructure. It is not principally an administrative function but rather the means by 
225 
which shared infrastructure remains trustworthy, financially sustainable and equitable over time, 
226 
and through which the architecture continues to deliver the semantic consistency and reliability 
227 
the rest of this guidance depends upon. 
228 
 
229 
Because DPI-H is shared infrastructure — relied upon and contributed to by many actors, and 
230 
serving the health system as a whole rather than any single programme or institution — 
231 
governance attends both to the strategic direction of the shared infrastructure and to its day-to-
232 
day operational integrity. These are distinct responsibilities calling for distinct structures, though 
233 
they operate in close coordination. A consistent theme across the development of this architecture 
234 
is that this coordination, and the institutional arrangements behind it, come before technology 
235 
rather than after it: countries that establish authoritative registries, harmonise their core data, and 
236 
agree who decides what tend to progress, while those that begin from application components 
237 
without these arrangements tend to fragment.
