Where DPI-H supports multi-country or regional health coordination, governance design requires 
496 
considerations beyond the national model. 
497 
Jurisdictional complexity. Regional deployments span multiple national legal and regulatory 
498 
frameworks. Governance structures should define which body holds decision authority for shared 
499 
as opposed to country-specific components; how national sovereignty over health data and digital 
500 
systems is preserved within a regionally shared architecture; what dispute-resolution mechanisms 
501 
apply when national interests conflict; and how differing regulatory obligations are reconciled in 
502 
the design and operation of the infrastructure. 
503 
Regional strategic governance. Regional deployments should establish a regional strategic 
504 
governance body with explicit authority over shared components. Such a body would include 
505 
representation from each participating country at the appropriate ministerial or institutional level; 
506 
hold defined authority to make binding decisions on shared infrastructure within the scope of 
507 
agreed regional commitments; operate with its own financial sustainability mandate for the shared 
508 
regional layer, distinct from national obligations; and maintain explicit coordination with national 
509 
strategic governance bodies.

Layered governance. Regional DPI-H governance is best understood as a layered structure: 
511 
regional governance addresses what is shared across borders, national governance addresses 
512 
what is country-specific, and defined handoff points determine which body has authority over 
513 
which decision. This structure should be documented explicitly, including a catalogue of shared 
514 
versus country-specific components, the governance body with authority over each, and protocols 
515 
for decisions that affect components at more than one layer.
